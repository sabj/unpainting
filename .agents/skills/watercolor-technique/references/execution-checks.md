# Execution checks

The plan names the intended technique. The sheet and brush records show what happened. Check both before changing the next layer.

## Measure each thing separately

- Geometry: planned paths, alignment, executed strokes and paint laid down should agree on the same landmarks.
- Paper: surface water, absorbed water and how much pigment has set into the paper across the area covered by the stroke and its neighbors.
- Brush: water and pigment held in the brush, tip water, pigment concentration and delivered paint are different quantities.
- Coverage: total pigment gained measures deposited mass. It does not show a continuous area of color or its visible value.
- Appearance: compare wet and dried states for edge spread, spots where bare paper shows through, granulation (mottled pigment texture) and transparency.

Review the picture as well as the material measurements. Check its intermediate stages even when the finish looks good.

## Find the earliest stage at fault

Keep useful drawing geometry while you test a technique change. Check the ink drawing, broad wash, dried layer and finish against their separate jobs. If a gap already exists when paint is laid down, inspect whether the brush touched the paper and released paint first; changing a later drying step will not fix it.

Save checkpoints at the simulation resolution. Record sheet size, paper settings, palette, brush load, operation order and source version. A reduced whole-picture view serves composition. Fine ink and texture need close-up views at the simulation resolution.

## Keep comparisons fair

Change one decision at a time. Keep the same target, sheet and simulation resolution. A single-mark calibration, a study of one passage and a whole-picture comparison answer different questions. A close-up with a recomputed first wash or a different starting position in the paper texture is a separate experiment.
