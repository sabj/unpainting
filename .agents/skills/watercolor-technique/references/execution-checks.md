# Execution checks

Check the sheet and brush records against the plan before changing the next layer.

## Measure each thing separately

- Geometry: planned paths, alignment, executed strokes and paint laid down agree on the same landmarks.
- Paper: surface water, absorbed water and how much pigment has set into the paper across the stroke's footprint and neighbors.
- Brush: water and pigment held, tip water, pigment concentration and delivered paint are different quantities.
- Coverage: total pigment gained measures deposited mass, not a continuous area of color or its visible value.
- Appearance: compare wet and dried states for edge spread, spots where bare paper shows, granulation (mottled pigment texture) and transparency.

## Find the earliest stage at fault

Keep useful drawing geometry while you test a technique change. Check the ink drawing, broad wash, dried layer and finish against their separate jobs. If a gap exists when paint is laid down, check first that the brush touched the paper and released paint; a later drying step will not fix it.

Save checkpoints at simulation resolution with sheet size, paper settings, palette, brush load, operation order and source version. Fine ink and texture need close-ups; a reduced view serves composition.

## Keep comparisons fair

Change one decision at a time, with the same target, sheet and resolution. A close-up with a recomputed first wash or a different starting position in the paper texture is a separate experiment.
