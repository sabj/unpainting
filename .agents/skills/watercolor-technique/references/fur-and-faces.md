# Fur and faces

Fur texture should explain a recognizable form. Settle the silhouette, facial landmarks, connected value groups and light hierarchy before small marks.

## Order

1. Ink the silhouette, overlaps, eyes, nose and mouth. Let it dry and set.
2. Paint the connected light and shadow masses. The subject should be recognizable in a low-detail study.
3. Assign each light: bare paper, pale local color, or a masked strand or area where paint can be removed. A pale muzzle is not automatically paper white.
4. Record growth direction, strand curvature and group size per region; pressure and stroke length follow them.
5. Add directional fur marks and selected darks. Keep some underpainting visible.

## Selective lights

[Anna Mason's spaniel demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ql_dmqDH9k8) uses a preparatory drawing and fine masking for selected whiskers. Pale directional color precedes stronger fur marks; after the mask comes off, pale paint sets the whiskers' contrast.

Reserve only the lights that need it. Apply masking fluid to dry paint, remove it only when the surrounding paint is dry, then check the revealed width and edge.

## Different fur, different marks

[Rebecca Rhodes's curly black fur](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_hM0z6PO1XQ) builds interlocking light sides and darker curved valleys. [Emily Olson's fur studies](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ryzkAsjIi9E) separate soft wet masses, short broken marks and longer curved locks. Use short muzzle strokes, longer ear locks and curved groups where the target shows them.

## Facial values

[Rhodes's nose study](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V-QyeyoWRjw) starts with a pale value map, develops dark nostrils and midtones, then adjusts small lights and selected edges against the fur. Locate nostrils, eye boundaries, muzzle planes and reflected lights explicitly; check proportions and contrast before texture. Soften chosen transitions only: broad softening removes the contrast that makes features recognizable.

## Studies

Isolate an uncertain shape, edge or material in a small study at the real passage's sheet size and resolution. Calibrate moisture and timing in the simulator, not from demonstrations.
