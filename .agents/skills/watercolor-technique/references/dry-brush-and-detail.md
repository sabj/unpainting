# Dry brush and detail

Choose the edge the stroke needs to make. A crisp architectural boundary, a broken fur mark and a soft cloud transition each need different paper moisture, brush pressure and paint load. Dry paper in one region can sit beside wet paint, so check the area covered by the stroke and nearby wet areas.

## Dry brush still carries paint

[Winsor & Newton's dry-brush demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=72sGLG0fQPc) shows a range from solid bands of paint to light broken sweeps:

- [0:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=72sGLG0fQPc&t=18s): low side contact and horizontal passes give a connected yellow band, then a reload.
- [0:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=72sGLG0fQPc&t=28s): sweeps get lighter and more broken but keep large painted sections.
- [1:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=72sGLG0fQPc&t=61s): blue crosses the dried yellow, leaving blue, yellow, green overlaps and spots of bare paper.

Dry brush means a limited brush water load and broken contact with the paper tooth, the surface texture. It does not mean an empty brush. Keep enough paint for the mark's solid areas and get the breaks from reduced contact or load.

[Moor to Sea Arts' brush-mark sheet](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/541c2f79e4b02b592c399f7f/1e44ac9a-94e0-468a-b6be-4aa3daeff4a2/Dictionary_of_Marks_Brushes.jpg), from its [tutorial page](https://www.moortoseaarts.co.uk/tutorial-videos), compares connected and broken bands, crisp and soft lines, and side-to-point contact.

## Separate layers deliberately

[Andy Griffiths's wet/dry examples](https://www.solvingwatercolour.com/24-essential-watercolor-painting-techniques/) separate wet-in-wet, wet-on-dry and dry brush. Use wet-on-dry for distinct planes and dry brush for texture. Check surface dryness and how much pigment has set into the paper before a glaze that must keep the layer below.

[Chris Jones's tree sequence](https://chrisjonespaints.co.uk/tutorial-how-to-paint-a-watercolour-tree/) builds connected color masses, keeps gaps and limits late detail. A stroke on fresh paper can run into the wet edge of the one before. Do not dry between fragments of one passage.

## Test a representative mark

1. Reduce the uncertain technique to a few gestures at the real sheet size and simulation resolution.
2. Save the wet and dried states.
3. Compare shape, coverage, taper and edges with the source.
4. Log required and measured paper moisture, prepared brush load and actual brush contact.
5. Try the same decision on a simple, recognizable subject.

If the mark differs from the plan, check delivered paint and geometry before changing later layers. Brush load alone does not show whether the mark has the intended shape and coverage.
