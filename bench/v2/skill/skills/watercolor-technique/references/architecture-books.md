# Architecture and connected washes

A passage is a wash or group of marks with one job.

Establish geometry and light relationships before surface texture. A pale wall, lit jamb and dark opening should already explain the building at small scale.

## Supporting sources

- David Cox, [*Treatise on Landscape Painting and Effect in Water Colours*](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/65962/pg65962-images.html): [outline](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/65962/pg65962-images.html#ON_OUTLINE), [light and shade](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/65962/pg65962-images.html#ON_LIGHT_AND_SHADE_AND_EFFECT), and [laying on tints](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/65962/pg65962-images.html#METHOD_OF_LAYING_ON_THE_TINTS). The early window, door and gable plates isolate structural relationships. They are illustrations with no recorded watercolor sequence.
- [Paul Clark's Italian village](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MVyxMQi7Z7U&t=174s): broad angular placements, retained pale wall planes, color added to wet washes, drying and selective detail.
- [Toby Haseler's line-and-wash sketch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=71lPMn5MwvU&t=248s): an opening pen drawing, loose washes, connected sidewall and roof shadow, then selected finishing accents.
- Harry Willson, [*The Use of a Box of Colours*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/32681): prepared tints and selected structural marks. Erasing by abrasion needs separate simulation code. Gentle lifting does not replace them.

## Planning decisions

1. Locate wall, roof, eave, opening and overlap landmarks with shared endpoints. Windows inherit the orientation and scale of the plane containing them.
2. Reserve the broad lit plane and any important narrow border. Choose paper, pale paint, negative painting (painting around a light shape) or supported masking explicitly.
3. Prepare mixtures for the dominant value groups. Use brush contact covering the whole surface and deliberate direction before brick, tile or foliage marks.
4. Connect washes where the reference shows one shadow or wet passage crossing regions. Keep separately lit faces distinct. Separately labeled areas can share a continuous wash.
5. Assign hard, soft, broken and lost edges individually. A crisp eave can coexist with softly varied paint inside its shadow; soften only the intended boundary.
6. Inspect drawing and first washes before detail. If geometry or value grouping is uncertain, test one opening or gable with a few tones. Later texture should explain a recognizable form.

For ink-and-wash, draw the defining structure before the washes. The Haseler demonstration's later pen work supports optional selected accents. A second drawing pass is not required. Verify the paper state needed by the actual medium.

Artist demonstrations do not supply calibrated brush pressure, water ratios or drying thresholds. Measure those in the simulator and inspect the resulting marks.
