# Fur, facial values and selective masking

A passage is a wash or group of marks with one job.

Fur texture should explain a recognizable form. Compare the silhouette, facial landmarks, connected value groups and light hierarchy before adding small marks. Distinguish bare paper, pale paint, masked strands and areas where paint has been removed.

## Selective lights

[Anna Mason's spaniel demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ql_dmqDH9k8) uses preparatory drawing and fine masking for selected whiskers. Pale directional color precedes stronger fur marks; broader glazes adjust the relationship between light and dark areas. After masking is removed, pale paint controls the whiskers' contrast.

Reserve only the lights that need it. A pale muzzle plane is not automatically paper white. Use supported masking application and removal with the required paper state; inspect the revealed width and edge before accepting it.

## Different fur calls for different marks

[Rebecca Rhodes's curly black fur](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_hM0z6PO1XQ) develops interlocking light sides and darker curved valleys. [Emily Olson's fur studies](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ryzkAsjIi9E) distinguish soft wet masses, short broken marks and longer curved locks. These examples support choosing direction, group size and edge type before selecting a brush technique.

Organize marks by growth direction and form. Use short muzzle strokes, longer ear locks and curved groups where the reference supports them. Keep selected underpainting visible. Repeated circles, equal dabs or uniform noise do not describe every coat.

## Facial value hierarchy

[Rhodes's nose study](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V-QyeyoWRjw) begins with a pale value map, develops dark nostrils and midtones, then adjusts smaller lights and selected edges relative to the surrounding fur.

Locate nostrils, eye boundaries, muzzle planes and reflected lights explicitly. Check their proportions and contrast before texture. Soften chosen transitions while retaining important landmarks; a general softening pass can remove the distinctions that make the face readable.

## Transfer to the simulator

For each passage, record its reference cue, value shape, light category, edge types, gesture direction, brush width, water and pigment load, required paper state, and checkpoint. Inspect actual drawing, paint laid down and drying separately.

Edited demonstrations show useful sequences and appearances. They give no measured moisture thresholds or waiting times, so calibrate those in the current simulator. [Value-passage checks](fur-and-value-passages.md) provide a review checklist.
