# Dry brush carries a flowing paint load

[Winsor & Newton's dry-brush demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=72sGLG0fQPc) shows a continuum from solid bands of paint to lighter, broken sweeps. Its explanation relates loading, pressure and speed without prescribing a numerical water ratio.

- [0:18–0:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=72sGLG0fQPc&t=18s): low side contact and horizontal strokes produce a connected yellow band; the brush is reloaded.
- [0:28–0:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=72sGLG0fQPc&t=28s): sweeps become lighter and more broken while retaining substantial painted sections.
- [1:01–1:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=72sGLG0fQPc&t=61s): a blue layer crosses the earlier yellow, preserving blue, yellow, green overlaps and spots of bare paper. Measure absorbed water separately from surface appearance.

[Moor to Sea Arts' brush-mark sheet](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/541c2f79e4b02b592c399f7f/1e44ac9a-94e0-468a-b6be-4aa3daeff4a2/Dictionary_of_Marks_Brushes.jpg), linked from the [artists' tutorial page](https://www.moortoseaarts.co.uk/tutorial-videos), compares connected and broken bands, crisp and softer lines, and side-to-point contact. It shows types of brush marks, but gives no numerical simulator settings.

Choose paper moisture, the amount of liquid in the brush, pigment concentration, contact and speed separately. Wet paint on dry paper can make a connected crisp mark. For dry-brush texture, retain enough paint for the intended solid areas of the mark and use reduced contact or load for selected breaks.

Check paint released by the brush and deposited water alongside the initial paper moisture. The amount held in the brush or the fraction filled by a dip alone does not establish a useful mark. Compare the solid areas, gaps and narrowing ends of a mark with a reference brush mark.
