# Trees and skies

A passage is a wash or group of marks with one job.

Plan connected passages and edge types before individual texture marks. Distance, light and shape determine whether a passage should merge softly, preserve crisp gaps or break across the paper tooth (surface texture).

## Foliage and branches

[Paul Clark's tree demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ygV5PuvzjE4&t=217s) shows side contact and broken foliage marks; its [oak study](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ygV5PuvzjE4&t=458s) develops an irregular light canopy, shadow paint added to wet washes, trunk and ground. [Oliver Pyle's tree demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wU6jjU-CwYs&t=565s) distinguishes softer distant foliage from nearer openings and structure.

Choose the canopy's main value shape and reserve visible gaps showing sky through foliage as it grows. Charge coherent shadows while the wash remains wet, or use distinct dry-paper groups when the reference calls for crisp shadows. A ragged edge can surround a connected wet interior.

Use the brush's side or belly for a broad mass and its point for selected structure. [Pyle's branch sequence](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wU6jjU-CwYs&t=2538s) narrows the gesture through changing contact. Plan how to narrow the stroke and lift the brush; allow selected endings to disappear into foliage. Join trunk bases to the ground while their intended contact remains wet.

## Sky and clouds

[Pyle's sky demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn5C1SSxucs&t=290s) joins prepared warm horizon and cooler upper mixtures in a broad passage. Establish the large gradient and light direction before cloud detail. Keep a workable wet edge across intended joins.

Choose how each important cloud light survives: reserve paper, paint around the light shape or lift at an appropriate state. [Clark's sky demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZDnlFOgd6Fk&t=88s) uses large lifted upper clouds and finer lower streaks. A later dry–rewet–shadow sequence develops soft undersides while preserving brighter tops.

[Pyle's cloud work](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn5C1SSxucs&t=431s) varies brush scale and waits for a workable moisture window. Measure local paper state in the simulator; video elapsed time is not a drying instruction. A cloud can have a crisp reserved top and a softened shadow side.

## Softening and lifting

[Pyle's corrections](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn5C1SSxucs&t=1005s) distinguish blending a selected edge with a damp brush from removing pigment. Clean picked-up pigment from the brush between passes. Lifting need not recover paper white, so reserve essential lights early.

## Review the passage

Inspect the first wash for connectedness, dry value, light hierarchy and boundary roles before adding branches or cloud texture. Record actual paper state, brush load, contact and paint laid down. Paper grain does not justify uniform texture across a smooth atmospheric mass.

If broad strokes leave unintended gaps, inspect both delivered paint and physical contact with the declared paper. Paint held in the brush, brush-tip moisture and surface coverage are separate measurements. Compare paper stocks through representative marks while preserving the required physics; do not infer one brush load or paper-texture setting from a single run.
