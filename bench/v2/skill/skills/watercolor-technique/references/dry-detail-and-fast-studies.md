# Dry detail and brush contact

A passage is a wash or group of marks with one job.

Choose the edge the stroke needs to make. A crisp architectural boundary, a broken fur mark and a soft cloud transition need different paper moisture, brush pressure and paint loads. Dry paper in one region can coexist with wet paint nearby; inspect the area covered by the stroke and nearby wet areas.

## Dry brush carries paint

[Winsor & Newton's demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=72sGLG0fQPc&t=17s) moves from a solid band of paint to lighter, broken sweeps. Loading, pressure and speed work together. Dry brush means limited liquid and broken contact with paper tooth (surface texture). [The brush-water guide](brush-water-continuum.md) develops this distinction.

For intentional texture, choose direction, brush contact area and pigment concentration, then retain enough fluid paint for the intended solid areas of the mark. Check paint released by the brush before attributing gaps to the paper.

## Separate layers deliberately

[Andy Griffiths's wet/dry examples](https://www.solvingwatercolour.com/24-essential-watercolor-painting-techniques/) distinguish wet paint on wet paper, wet paint on dry paper and drier brush contact. Use wet-on-dry for distinct planes and appropriate dry contact for texture. Verify both surface dryness and how much pigment has set into the paper before a glaze that must preserve the base.

[Chris Jones's tree sequence](https://chrisjonespaints.co.uk/tutorial-how-to-paint-a-watercolour-tree/) supports building connected color masses, preserving gaps and limiting late detail. A fresh-paper stroke can continue into the wet edge of the preceding stroke. Do not dry automatically between fragments that belong to one passage.

## Verify a representative mark

Reduce an uncertain technique to a few gestures at the intended physical size and simulation cell density. Save immediate and dried states. Review shape, coverage, taper and edges against the source, then test the same decision on a simple recognizable subject.

Log the required and measured paper state, prepared brush load and actual brush contact. Check both resemblance to the source and whether the intended paint behavior occurred. If a mark differs from the plan, inspect geometry and paint laid down before changing later layers.
