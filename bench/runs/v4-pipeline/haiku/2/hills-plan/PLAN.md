# Painting Plan: Hills, loose ink and wash, V4-2

## Subject and intent
A rolling landscape with multiple green hills, forested areas, and winding paths. The painting celebrates the structure of the terrain through value planes and directional ink marks rather than photographic detail. Light-to-dark layering drives the composition.

## Value progression and composition
- **Lights**: Pale sky and distant trees, cream-colored paths on green hills, lit slopes on upper hills
- **Midtones**: Green hill masses, mid-forest shadows, gray-green distant slopes
- **Darks**: Deep forest shadows, foreground tree masses, valley creases and path edges
- **Focal point**: The central green hill with its winding path structure

## Regions and techniques

### 1. Sky and upper background (0-20% height)
**hills.sky region**
- **Technique**: Wash question – pale atmospheric graded wash
- **Move**: Wet on dry, pale green-gray tint
- **Paper state**: Damp with sheen, allowing soft merging into forest
- **Load**: Watery, pale concentration
- **Edge**: Soft transition into forest below
- **Value**: Lightest value, establishing atmospheric depth
- **Protection**: None; this is first wash

### 2. Upper forest and mid-tone slopes (15-40% height)
**hills.forest region**
- **Technique**: Ink and wash – forest structure on a value wash
- **Move**: Wet on dry for base wash; dry for directional marks
- **Paper state**: Dry by time of detail marks
- **Load**: Medium concentration for base; controlled load for tree marks
- **Edge roles**: Soft edges from sky; forest shapes define terrain
- **Directional strokes**: Upward flicks and curves following tree forms
- **Ink pattern**: Scattered gestural marks; density follows shadow
- **Values**: Mid-greens to mid-grays, transitional values
- **Details**: Tree silhouettes, slope contours, very loose

### 3. Central green hills with paths (35-75% height)
**hills.main region (central)**
- **Technique**: Wash with shadow glazes – hill planes unified by value
- **Move**: Wet on dry for base washes; dry overlay for shadow glazes
- **Paper state**: Dry at glaze application
- **Load**: Medium for base; controlled medium for glazes
- **Reserves**: Central path kept as paper or very pale tint
- **Edge roles**: Hard edge on path uphill side; soft on hill turning away
- **Path structure**: Traced ink lines define route; washes do not fill pathways
- **Shadow glazes**: One continuous glaze crossing the region where terrain darkens
- **Values**: Mid greens lit, darker greens in valley creases
- **Progression**: Base pale green → mid shadow glaze → dark accents

### 4. Foreground and lower slopes (65-100% height)
**hills.foreground region**
- **Technique**: Dark mass with gestural ink strokes
- **Move**: Wet on dry for dark base; dry brush or pen for texture
- **Paper state**: Dry, controlled contact for marks
- **Load**: Thick pigment for mass; stiff load for strokes
- **Edge**: Broken foreground edge, hard shadow lines on terrain
- **Gestural marks**: Downward flicks and scribble following ground plane
- **Values**: Dark forest greens and browns, darkest values
- **Character**: Texture and value, not individual forms

## Protected lights and reserves
- **Central path system**: Reserved as near-white paper or minimal pale wash, defining the focal area
- **Upper left path edge**: Reserved narrow band, establishing secondary route
- **Hill highlights**: Pale green-white on upper slopes, toned slightly in shadow areas only
- **Sky and distance**: Kept pale throughout; no dark reserves needed here

## Drawing strategy
- **Opening drawing**: Traced paths from the target (opening-lines.json), placed first. Pen lines define major path routes and valley creases. Sparse, gestural, not traced contours.
- **Closing drawing**: Same path structure applied last as ink refinement on dry paint, selective toning only at focal points (central path intersection, valley shadows). Not a fill; only accent lines where form is ambiguous.
- **Ink pigment**: Black sepia for neutral, warm shadows; mixed with greens for earth tones
- **Pressure and width**: Variable; lighter on lit slopes, heavier in shadow valleys

## Moisture and load decisions
- **Early washes**: Watery, transparent, big brush – paper shows through
- **Mid values**: Medium water, medium saturation – edges sharpen
- **Late shadows**: Dry brush load, stiff contact – accents and texture
- **Transition between regions**: Check paper state across boundaries; never force wet into dry or vice versa

## Light-to-dark breakdown by pass (approximate)
1. **Pass 1**: Sky pale wash
2. **Passes 2-4**: Upper forest light base and shadow glaze structure
3. **Passes 5-12**: Green hill mid-tone washes, one per slope plane
4. **Passes 13-18**: Valley shadow glazes crossing regions
5. **Passes 19-25**: Foreground dark masses and accent strokes
6. **Pass 26+**: Closing ink on paths and focal accents

## What success looks like at each stage
- **After opening drawing**: Path lines sit confident and spare, no tremor or over-work
- **After first wash (sky)**: Pale atmospheric dome, soft edge into forest
- **After mid washes (hills)**: Green planes with soft edges define terrain; paths remain clear
- **After shadow glazes**: Form emerges; valleys and folds read through value contrast alone
- **After closing ink**: Path structure crystallizes; focal hill becomes clear; rest stays loose

## What would show failure
- **Blur at path edges**: Overly wet base wash or too many soft edges; fix with controlled dry overlay
- **Patchwork sky**: Separately dried islands of tone rather than one graded wash
- **Muddy greens**: Overworked mid-layers or wrong pigment mix; use cleaner mixes and fewer passes
- **Heavy foreground ink**: Marks filled the dark mass instead of sitting on a value wash; ink should be sparse and gestural
- **Lost path structure**: If closing ink is so light that paths disappear, re-ink with slightly higher pressure
- **Uniform texture**: Marks the same weight and angle everywhere; vary density and direction by region
