# Painting Plan: South France Urban Sketch, V4 Try 2

## Value Thumbnail

Three values:
- **Light**: pale blue sky, bright foreground field patches, white building walls
- **Mid-tone**: green foliage, golden fields, building roof and shadows
- **Dark**: tree trunks, building window recesses, dense cypress masses, terrain shadows

Focal point: the stone building and cypress tree composition on the right side, emphasized through pen structure and warm value contrast against green.

## Reference Cues: Painter's Own Segmentation

The plan uses empty families `[]` to let the painter apply its own value-based color segmentation to the landscape. The painter is given only the overall urban-sketch style for the whole image, trusting its segmentation algorithm to find:

**Sky region**: Pale blue distant sky transitioning to warmer near-horizon, soft and atmospheric. Subtle gradation without strong cloud forms.

**Foliage and trees region**: Green pastures and cypress/tree masses forming rolling terrain. Trees have soft silhouettes against lighter sky. Vertical cypress forms distinct from softer canopy shapes. Shadow structure from sun angle (upper right).

**Building region**: Stone structure with clear architectural form. Tan/warm walls in sunlight, darker roof edges, window recesses very dark. Sharp edges where building meets sky and adjacent terrain.

**Field region**: Golden-warm foreground field with dappled light and shadow. Catches strongest light, warmest local color at ground level.

## Resolution and Technique

The painter applies light-to-dark layering across the whole scene with pen work (urban-sketch style) integrated throughout:

**Wash technique across landscape**: All areas use transparent watercolor wash for value separation. Sky as soft graded passage, fields and foliage as connected mid-tone and dark washes. Dry on dry approach for crisp edges between different value regions.

**Pen work (closing ink)**: Traced from the target image. Building corners, roof lines, window mullions, door openings define architecture. Cypress trunks as vertical centerlines. Field boundaries where pen lines provide structure. All ink work is selective and at focal point (building and adjacent trees), not as fill or scribble.

## Paper State, Load and Moves

**Sky pass**: Glossy wet paper, pale blue mixed from cerulean and raw sienna. Broad horizontal strokes, big mop brush. Water-rich pigment, let it spread. Damp throughout the passage. Lift cloud lights with a thirsty brush while still open.

**Mid-field and trees (first wash)**: Damp (not glossy) paper after sky dries. Green mixed from cerulean, ultramarine, hansa yellow, raw sienna. Medium to thick load, connected passages by value family, not individual marks. Dry brush contact on dry neighbor areas.

**Building first wash**: Dry paper, warm ochre-sienna mix. Medium pressure, controlled loading. Building walls painted as distinct planes; one hard edge where it meets dark trees, soft where adjacent field bleeds.

**Foreground field**: Damp paper, gold ochre and hansa yellow mixed warm. Generous water, gestural horizontal strokes following slope. Paper showing between strokes.

**Shadow glazes** (after all first washes dry): Dry paper, thin transparent glazes of ultramarine and sepia. Shadow glaze across tree mass shadow side (covering trees and adjacent field). One building shadow glaze across wall and ground. No multiple passes in one area—one glaze per shadow.

**Foreground texture** (dry final pass): Dry brush, pale ochre and hansa yellow thin mix. Horizontal flicks following grass slope, at focal foreground only. Breaks contact on paper tooth.

**Closing ink**: Dry paper, sepia ink. Architecture: building corners, roof edge line, window mullions, door opening. Trees: cypress trunk centerlines, some branch suggestions. No scribble fill; pen lines only where structure is needed.

## Protected Lights

1. Sky light in upper middle (0.3-0.6 horizontal, 0.08-0.18 vertical): pale reserved for sky luminosity
2. Bright building wall (0.7-0.95 horizontal, 0.45-0.65 vertical): reserved white or pale wash only
3. Foreground bright field patches (0.15-0.35 horizontal, 0.75-0.85 vertical): reserved for sun-caught grass

These are protected from dark washes and shadow glazes by paper reserve or planned pale local wash only.

## Edge Roles

**Hard edges (architectural and cypress structure)**:
- Building corners where stone meets sky
- Building-field boundary on left and bottom
- Cypress trunk centerlines
- Window mullions and door edges
- Roof line sharp against sky

**Soft edges (landscape)**:
- Sky-field horizon: soft gradient inside the atmospheric transition
- Tree silhouettes against lighter sky: one side soft where they blend, one side maintains structure
- Field patches: soft internal transitions, hard boundaries only at reserves
- Building transition to adjacent green: one side sharp (building), one side soft (foliage spill)

**Lost edges (minimal)**:
- Some grass marks dissolve into foreground field
- Internal tree shapes where shadow meets sunlit canopy

## Passage and Layer Order

1. **Opening drawing**: Pen lines traced from target. Building structure, cypress trunks, major field boundaries, focal point architecture lines. Sparse, confident gestures only.

2. **Pass 1: Sky wash** (wet in wet on glossy paper)
   - One continuous graded passage, pale blue, upper 25%
   - Dryer toward top, wetter toward horizon
   - Lift cloud lights while open
   - Dry completely before next pass

3. **Pass 2: Mid-field and tree first wash** (wet on dry, damp paper)
   - Green passages for foliage masses and mid-field
   - Connected within value family (one green-field area painted together)
   - Each region gets one loaded pass, paper shows through
   - Building walls painted pale ochre in same phase, separated by dry contact
   - Dry before proceeding

4. **Pass 3: Foreground field warm wash** (wet on dry, damp paper)
   - Golden ochre foreground, big horizontal strokes
   - Generous water, one passage from side to side
   - Lighter where sun hits, warmer throughout
   - Avoid painting over building reserve
   - Dry completely

5. **Passes 4-5: Shadow glazes** (wet on dry, dry paper)
   - Tree shadow glaze across tree mass and adjacent field shadows
   - Building shadow glaze across wall shadow side and ground shadow at building base
   - Thin transparent glazes, one stroke per glaze
   - Glazes cross region boundaries as needed for unified shadow
   - Dry between glazes

6. **Pass 6: Building mid-value detail** (wet on dry, dry paper)
   - Roof mid-tone where it catches warm light
   - Building shadow plane on left side of walls
   - One hard edge, one soft edge per plane
   - Medium pressure, controlled load

7. **Pass 7: Foreground grass texture** (dry brush, dry paper)
   - Pale ochre dry-brush flicks in foreground focal area only
   - Horizontal direction following slope
   - Thin load, paper breaking contact
   - Gestural, not stippled

8. **Pass 8: Closing ink** (dry paper, final)
   - Architecture: building corners, roof line, window mullions, door opening
   - Trees: cypress centerline, branch accents
   - Selective placement at focal points and structure only
   - No fill, no scribble

## What Would Show Failure

1. **Blurry sky or foliage**: indicates over-wet or over-merged. Sky should read as graded light area, trees as distinct mass shapes. Fix: check paper state before each pass, avoid over-tilting the sheet.

2. **Flat building**: loss of light planes and shadow planes. Building should read as three-dimensional with wall planes distinct from roof and shadow. Fix: ensure first wash is pale enough, shadow glaze is thin and selective, hard edges at corners are sharp.

3. **Muddy greens or browns**: over-mixing or greens loaded too heavy in first pass. Colors should read as transparent glazes, not opaque fills. Fix: use thinner, more watery mixes, fewer pigments per mix (2-3 max).

4. **Pen lines not visible or too heavy**: ink over blurry paint is a known failure. If pen lines are lost, the paint beneath was too soft/wet or ink too heavy. Fix: paint dry, light selection ink, restore edges in paint before adding ink.

5. **Foreground texture as stipple or uniform noise**: gestural marks should follow slope with varied density. Fix: dry brush only at focal point, vary pressure and direction, leave gaps and paper showing.

6. **Loss of building structure due to tree shadows running through it uncontrolled**: building must stay distinct even where shadowed. Fix: building reserve protects walls; shadow glaze stops at building boundary or is applied with careful edge control.

7. **No value separation between sky and distant field**: horizon should be distinct. Fix: ensure sky dries with enough value drop, field gets its own pass afterward.

## Changes from Base V096

**Token change**: lay3 (3 mid-layers) instead of lay4 (4 mid-layers)
- **Why**: Urban sketch style calls for lighter, less-heavily-built approach. Fewer layer passes allow the pen work and paper to show more clearly, maintaining sketch aesthetic. 3 layers still provides adequate value range for landscape depth.

**Config simplification**: Empty families `[]` and no reserves
- **Why**: Rectangular region boundaries created unsupported axis-aligned seams that the painter refused. Simplified to let the painter apply its own value-based segmentation, which is more robust for landscape with soft transitions. Urban-sketch style applied globally rather than per-region.

**Extra-args.json**: Warm pigment set specified
- Ochre, raw sienna, hansa yellow, ultramarine, cerulean, sepia
- **Why**: South France warm Mediterranean light requires warm earth tones (ochre, sienna, hansa yellow) paired with cool shadows (ultramarine, sepia). This palette supports the urban sketch aesthetic with clear local color.

## Stage Intents

**After opening drawing**: Building structure visible, cypress trunks established, major field boundaries suggested. Sparse, confident lines only.

**After sky wash**: Pale blue atmospheric upper third, soft gradation to warmer horizon. No visible brush strokes, smooth blend within the passage. Paper texture showing subtly.

**After mid-field and building washes**: Field areas show value separation; trees read as distinct mid-tone green masses; building walls glow with pale warm color distinct from green neighbors. No detail yet, just value planes and local color.

**After foreground wash**: Strong golden warm value in bottom third contrasts with mid-tone greens above. Field reads as lit plane catching main light source. Paper still visible in lighter strokes.

**After shadow glazes**: Tree shadows create form and direction; building shadow defines wall planes and roof angle. Shadows are transparent, not opaque fills. Value goes dark to dark areas only, not over the lights.

**After building detail and texture passes**: Roof line reads as warm mid-tone distinct from pale walls and dark shadows. Foreground grass has directional texture. Detail is concentrated at focal point (building and adjacent cypress).

**After closing ink**: Architecture comes alive: building corners sharp, window mullions clear, roof edge defined. Cypress trunks establish vertical structure. Pen work is selective and confident, not labored. Drawing enhances form already established by paint.

## Smallest Feature in Cells

- Building window mullions: ~8 cells wide at 1216 px output (ink-and-wash, pen-drawn)
- Cypress trunk centerline: ~4-6 cells wide (ink line)
- Foreground grass marks: ~10-15 cells wide (dry brush, gestural)
- Tree branch accents: ~8-10 cells wide (pen lines)

All detail at focal point (building and nearby cypress). Distant landscape stays as value washes without individual marks.

## Conclusion

This plan treats South France as an urban-architectural subject first, landscape second. The pen work defines structure (building and trees); light transparent washes build atmosphere and color. Sky and fields recede through value and soft edges. The stone building and cypress stand forward through sharp structure, warm color, and concentrated detail. The approach honors both the watercolor medium (light-to-dark layering, transparent glazes) and the urban-sketch style (visible pen work, sketch-like spontaneity, less-finished effect than tight realism).
