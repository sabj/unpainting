# Trumbull V4 Try 2: Precise Architectural Watercolor

## 1. Value Structure and Intent

**Value thumbnail:** Five values from light to dark
- **Lights**: Pale sky, golden lit stone, paper whites in cloud highlights and window glass
- **Light-mid**: Pale sky grade, lit facade planes
- **Mid**: Side wall planes, roof shadows, shadowed windows
- **Dark-mid**: Deep window recesses, cast shadows on facade
- **Darks**: Street level foreground with figures and signs, deepest shadows

**Focal point:** The main facade with its arched windows and distinctive golden stone material

**Progression intent:** Light-to-dark layering throughout. Large light washes establish form, then mid-values add dimension, shadow glazes unify across boundaries, closing ink restores structure at focal points only.

---

## 2. Lights Reserved (before any wash)

1. **Brightest cloud highlights** (0.4–0.55, 0.02–0.08): Bare paper, deepened with pale glaze only
2. **Lit stone face glints** (0.25–0.75, 0.25–0.55): Scattered paper whites showing texture and shine
3. **Window glass reflections** (0.3–0.7, 0.3–0.6): Preserved for luminosity in recessed apertures

---

## 3. Per-Region Technique and Painting Order

### Pass 1: Light Washes (wet on dry for most, wet in wet for sky)

**Region 1: Sky (trumbull.sky, 0–1, 0–0.2)**
- **Reference cue:** Pale blue-grey sky, graded darker toward horizon, soft cloud edges
- **Resolution:** Wash question—the sky's value plane carries form
- **Paper state:** Glossy wet (one continuous passage)
- **Pigment load:** Watery, pale
- **Moves:** Wet in wet. Charge pale blue at top, grey-blue mid-gradient. Lift cloud highlights while wet with thirsty brush on one edge; leave the other soft.
- **Edge roles:** Soft everywhere; horizon edge blends into facade
- **Issue to avoid:** Separately tiled cloud patches (failure subject-d). Work as one continuous graded passage.

**Region 2: Facade main (trumbull.facade, 0.1–0.9, 0.15–0.85)**
- **Reference cue:** Large golden stone planes, warm pale tone, arched openings
- **Resolution:** Wash question—the lit plane's edge and value give form
- **Paper state:** Damp (shine just off), flowing
- **Pigment load:** Medium (3-4 mm bristle depth)
- **Moves:** Wet on dry. One connected passage across the whole lit wall, warm ochre and raw sienna mix, pale and transparent. No fragmentation; the facade is one lit mass (avoid failure Y1: pale islands).
- **Edge roles:** Hard edge between lit facade and darker side walls; soft edge into foliage on right
- **Feature check:** Windows are dark shapes within the wash, not individually outlined yet

**Region 3: Side wall left (0–0.15, 0.15–0.85)**
- **Reference cue:** Left plane, mid-warm, distinct turn from main facade
- **Resolution:** Wash question
- **Paper state:** Damp
- **Pigment load:** Medium
- **Moves:** Wet on dry, same pass as facade if possible or immediately after
- **Edge roles:** Hard edge where it meets facade; soft edge into sky

**Region 4: Side wall right (0.85–1, 0.15–0.85) with foliage**
- **Reference cue:** Right plane, mid-warm below, green foliage above
- **Resolution:** Wash question for wall; foliage on soft atmospheric-wet style
- **Paper state:** Damp
- **Pigment load:** Medium below, slightly wetter for foliage
- **Moves:** Wet on dry
- **Edge roles:** Hard edge where it meets main facade; foliage edge soft and diffuse

**Region 5: Roof (0.1–0.9, 0.08–0.18)**
- **Reference cue:** Peaked roof forms, dark grey-blue, mid-value
- **Resolution:** Wash question—value and edge shape the roof planes
- **Paper state:** Damp
- **Pigment load:** Medium
- **Moves:** Wet on dry, applied as the sky dries (just damp, not glossy)
- **Edge roles:** Hard edges where roof planes change angle; soft edge where roof meets sky

**Region 6: Foreground street (0–1, 0.8–1)**
- **Reference cue:** Street level with figures, signs, darkest values
- **Resolution:** Wash question—mid-dark value for street, with figures as shapes within
- **Paper state:** Damp to dry (lighter pressure, smaller brush)
- **Pigment load:** Medium, stronger
- **Moves:** Wet on dry
- **Edge roles:** Hard ground line; figures as silhouettes
- **Note:** Painted last in this pass after facade dries

### Pass 2: Mid Values on Dry Paper

**Roof planes** (if not in first pass): Darker grey-blue on dry paper, shaping the peaked forms

**Side wall shadows**: Where side walls turn from light, paint a mid-tone glaze edge, hard on the crease side, soft on the lit side

**Window openings**: Dark mid-value shapes establishing the arches and mullion shadows (not the deep recesses yet—those are darks)

**Facade shading**: Subtle mid-tone wash where the stone turns (upper half darker than lower, following the reference)

### Pass 3: Shadow Glazes (dry paper, crossing boundaries)

**Main shadow glaze**: One connected glaze across the facade from upper left to lower right, crossing all regions and window areas. This unifies the light and shadows as one shape, following the architectural cast shadows and the side planes turning into shade.

**Window recesses**: Now paint the deep shadows of the window depths as the darkest passage, still on dry paper but with concentrated dark pigment

### Pass 4: Closing Ink (selective, on dry paint)

**Opening ink** (already drawn): Sparse pen lines for roof ridge, arches, mullions in windows, key structural corners at focal points

**Closing ink**: Reestablish mullions and arches where they explain form and carry structure. Focus on the central arched windows where they are sharpest. No scribble; clean lines only.

---

## 4. Moisture and Load Strategy

- **Pale washes**: Watery loads, big brush (20–25 mm), flowing strokes
- **Mid values**: Medium load (3–4 mm pigment depth), controlled edges, medium round or flat
- **Shadows and darks**: Thick, concentrated pigment on a damp brush, smaller brush (8–12 mm)
- **Ink lines**: Fine nib (0.3–0.5 mm), consistent pressure, dry paper

---

## 5. Glaze List and Cross-Boundaries

1. **Main facade shadow glaze**: Crosses from roof into facade, around windows, down to foreground—unifies light and shadow as one structural form
2. **Side plane shadows**: Left and right side planes, subtle, defining the architectural turn
3. **Window recess shadow**: Deep shadow in each arched opening, darkest value after glazes are dry

---

## 6. Wet Passages and Detail Protection

- **Sky**: One continuous wet passage, all at once
- **Facade + side walls**: All connected in one or two wet passes while paper is damp
- **Detail regions**: Windows and mullions kept dry or drying to crisp before shadows are laid; no wet-in-wet bleed into window detail
- **Foreground**: Painted on drier paper to keep figures and signs sharp

---

## 7. Drawing (Opening and Closing)

**Opening drawing**: Sparse pen lines traced from the target
- Roof ridge and peaked forms
- Main arched window outlines (not every mullion, just the arch shape)
- Key structural corners where facade meets side walls
- Chimney stack silhouette if visible

**Closing drawing**: Selective ink restoration on dry paint
- Mullions in central arched windows where they carry structure
- Arch springlines at focal arches
- Corner junctions where they enhance form
- No ink in reserved lights or pale areas

**Ink pigment**: Neutral sepia or black (check the run's pigment log)

---

## 8. What Would Show Failure

1. **Blurry facade**: Indicates wet-in-wet or soft edge where precision is needed. Architecture needs dry-paper edges.
2. **Fragmented facade (pale islands)**: Several separate washes instead of one connected lit plane. Shows in side-by-side compare.
3. **Overmerged sky**: Grading so strong it flattens cloud shapes (failure subject-d). The sky should stay a sky, not a uniform gradient.
4. **Heavy ink over blur**: Indicates the paint's edges need fixing before closing ink added (failure subject-c, 10-06).
5. **Patchwork shadows**: Region-by-region shadow fills instead of one cross-boundary glaze that unifies multiple regions.
6. **Lost structure**: Arches and mullions too soft or absent at focal windows.
7. **Noise on facade**: Parallel bands, many small marks, or scratchy dry brush where broad planes should be (failure subject-b, failure Cape). Facade is washes, not texture.

**Base recipe (applied automatically):**

**Token change:**

**Why:** The base has 4 mid-value layers (`_lay4`), which gives rich gradation but can risk over-rendering and fragmentation on the facade. For precise watercolor with careful hand-planned regions, 2 mid-value layers (`_lay2`) allow finer control over where values transition, reducing the risk of the pale-island fragmentation seen in failure Y1. The plan's regional boundaries and shadow glaze explicitly guide the mid-value placement, so fewer automatic layers work better.

## 10. Fast Proof Check (before full paint)

If running a compile preview:
- Check that the architecture's large shapes read as value planes, not spotted or noisy
- Verify sky is one graded passage, not tiled cloud patches
- Check that façade is one light mass with a soft edge into foliage, hard edge into side walls
- Window shapes should read as dark recesses within the lit wall
- Ensure no unsupported axis-aligned seams (reserves and regions should follow real edges)
