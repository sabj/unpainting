# Painting plan: South France, urban sketch, V5 round 1

## Reference cues
A Tuscan hilltop village under warm afternoon light. Pale blue sky above dark cypress sentinels. A cluster of terracotta buildings on the right with strong cast shadows on the sunlit field. Rolling grass landscape with cypress trees creating a vertical rhythm. The brightest area is the sunlit side of the rightmost buildings; the darkest shapes are tree shadows on the grass and building shadow sides. No pure blacks—warm shadows with violet undertones.

## Value structure
- Lights: pale sky (nearly white), bright grass field, lit building façades
- Half-tones: mid grass with turning planes, tree canopy shapes, building shadow sides
- Darks: cypress tree trunks and shadows, deep building recesses, cast shadows across field
- Focal point: the building cluster on the right with its roofline and façade planes

## Regions and techniques

**sf.grass (bottom half, 0.5–1.0):** Light wash base for the entire field. Pale yellowish-green, wet on dry. Damp with shine gone, few strokes, to avoid bloom. The middle distance stays a wash. Only the foreground (bottom 15%) gets dry-brush flicks for grass texture at the focal zone. Reserve light patches on the sunlit side. Shadow glaze across where terrain turns and cast shadows fall from buildings.

**sf.trees (left/center, tall cypress shapes):** Canopy mass per tree, loaded wet on dry, with sky holes reserved as crown grows. Dark greens (ultramarine, sap green, burnt sienna mix). Charge shadow shapes into the still-wet canopy for soft transitions; do not tile separate passes. Trunks and branches last, tapering from belly to point on dry paper. Foreground cypress lines near the focal point: dry paper, a few shaped marks with one crisp edge.

**sf.buildings (right side, 0.65–1.0 bounds):** Architecture approach. Opening pen: sparse structure, rooflines, building junctions, and door/window hints. Broad pale wall and sky-facing washes on dry paper. Mid values as fold planes and shadow edges (roofs darker than walls, sides darker where they turn). One connected shadow glaze per shadow side, crossing wall and roof boundaries. Dark recesses of windows and doorways as last paint accents. Closing ink restores roofline definition and building corners where they explain form. No filled dark masses—ink is gestural line, never fill.

**sf.sky (top, 0.0–0.4):** One continuous pale wash on dry or evenly damp paper. Graded from pale cream-white at horizon to pale blue-grey at top. Reserved cloud-light edges early if the target shows them. No over-merging—keep the subtle blue-to-white transition visible. Cloud shadows if needed: charged into the moist base at the right sheen, never tiled into separate patches.

## Protected lights (reserves)
- Brightest building façade: [0.75, 0.45, 0.95, 0.65]. Pale tint only, no dark overlay.
- Grass field light patches: [0.2, 0.55, 0.4, 0.75]. Preserve paper white or pale glaze.
- Sky at horizon: pale cream, lifted or reserved where the target shows brightest.

## Drawing plan
**Opening:** Sparse pen lines trace rooflines, building edges, cypress tree profiles, and main junctions. Drawn with light pressure, defining silhouettes and structure only. No full fills or ruled hatches. Drawing should read like a painter's first gestural marks, not traced linework.

**Closing:** Selective ink on dry paint at focal points—roofline definition, building corner junctions where they carry structure, window arches if the reference shows them. Accent dark valleys between tree shapes. No under-edge creases or stipple. Ink pigment: neutral sepia (base recipe _ink4).

## Load and moves
- Grass wash: watery, big brush, few strokes, paper showing through. Damp with shine gone to avoid blooms.
- Trees: medium load, charged into wet or layered on dry per reference.
- Buildings: medium load for walls, controlled for edges. Fold shadows as glazes with one hard edge (crease side) and one soft edge (turn side).
- Pen: light to medium pressure. No tremor, spurs, or uniform flicks. Width follows pressure; runs go dry naturally.

## Edge roles
- Sky–tree boundary: soft where trees blur into sky, hard where rooflines define the silhouette.
- Grass–tree shadow: soft where shadow turns field, hard at the building edge.
- Building sunlit side: hard edge separating from grass, soft edge where it turns into shadow.
- Grass texture: dry-brush or pen strokes only at foreground, middle distance stays a wash.

## Failure signs to watch
- Trees painted as individual separate passes or stippled flecks (fix: one wet mass per tree, shadow glaze if needed).
- Grass "SUPER NOISY" with many small marks (fix: broad wash, texture only foreground and at cast shadow edges).
- Heavy ink over blurry paint (fix: paint edges crisp before closing ink).
- Sky banding or flat blue (fix: one continuous graded passage, no separate cloud patches).
- Buildings as a patchwork of separate rectangles (fix: connected wall plane, shadow glaze across boundaries).

## Tokens and changes
- _lay2: Two mid layers instead of four. Reduces opacity build-up and keeps the work light and sketch-like, matching the urban-sketch style request.
- _dk11: Dark gain 1.1 instead of 1.3. The Tuscan light is warm and golden; shadows are violet-warm, not deep black. Lighter darks preserve the luminosity.
- Base recipe kept: ink4 (full pen drawing), _uncap (many lines), _pgreen (green palette), _dwg200 (pen width 2x), _sky3 (sky color and split), etc.

## Stage intents
1. **Opening drawing:** Rooflines and building edges visible, cypress silhouettes read, minimal lines, no fills.
2. **First light wash (grass):** Entire field is pale yellowish-green, nearly monochrome, sky is pale cream-blue. Paper showing, fresh and loose.
3. **Mid layer 1 (trees and field shading):** Tree canopy shapes appear, shadows across grass begin to read, building walls take warm undertone.
4. **Mid layer 2 (values):** Building shadow sides and roof planes defined, tree shadows deepen, grass field has turning planes.
5. **Shadow glaze:** Connected dark glaze across building shadow side and cast shadow on field, unifying the composition.
6. **Closing ink:** Rooflines crisp, building corners restored, select accent lines in tree valleys, foreground grass flicks (dry brush or pen). Light and selective, never a fill.

## Fast proof
- Test one cypress tree in 30 seconds: wet canopy mass, charge shadow. Does it blob or flow naturally?
- Test the grass-to-shadow transition in 60 seconds: pale wash, then glaze. Is the edge soft or hard as planned?
- Test building edge in 90 seconds: open pen line, pale wash, shadow glaze. Does the form read or blur?
