# Watercolor Plan: Trumbull Cathedral (V5 Try 1)

## Reference Analysis

**Target:** Gothic Revival stone cathedral with warm yellow-tan facade, multiple Gothic windows, blue sky, green foliage on right, street level figures.

**Style:** Precise architectural watercolor — light to dark layering, hard edges on structure, soft transitions on sky and distant elements.

## Value Thumbnail

- **Lights:** Sky white/pale blue, lit wall planes, cloud edges
- **Mid-values:** Sky gradient, wall base color, foliage
- **Focal point:** Central tower and window cluster at ~0.5 horizontally, 0.3–0.6 vertically
- **Shadows:** Window recesses (darkest), street level, wall shadow side (right and bottom)

## Protected Lights (Reserved Paper)

1. **Cloud masses** (upper center, ~0.35–0.65 × 0.03–0.15): reserved as brightest clouds in sky, to be toned selectively
2. **Tower top** (left side, ~0.05–0.2 × 0.1–0.25): pale lit plane on tower
3. **Wall plane catch light** (center-left, ~0.2–0.35 × 0.35–0.45): lit wall plane between windows
4. **Sky holes in foliage** (upper right, ~0.75–0.95 × 0.2–0.35): sky visible through canopy

## Per-Region Technique and Order

### Opening Drawing (Before any wash)
- **Pen/pencil lines traced from target** provide architectural structure:
  - Window mullions, arches, clock face feature
  - Stone coursing lines (horizontal at top of tower)
  - Tower edges and roof peaks
  - Main door outline, entry detail
- **Sparse, confident lines:** silhouettes and defining edges only
- **One dip:** flowing gestures, no tremor or ruled hatch
- **Paper state:** dry, pencil or fine sepia ink brush-pen

### Stage 1: Sky Wash (Wet in Wet)
- **Move:** Wet on wet, one continuous graded passage
- **Paper state:** Evenly damped, glossy sheen (not flooding)
- **Load:** Medium watery, pale blue mixed from cool primaries (ultramarine, cerulean)
- **Execution:**
  1. Wet entire sky area with clean water
  2. Load brush with pale blue, apply from top downward
  3. Lighten toward horizon and clouds (more water, less pigment)
  4. Charge in soft cool shadow on upper left (cooler blue-grey)
  5. Leave reserved cloud masses as white paper or lift while wet
  6. Dry fully — check paper state before next stage

### Stage 2: Mid Wash — Wall and Foliage Base
- **Wall region:** Wet on dry, pale warm wash
  - **Paper state:** Dry, brushed for texture
  - **Load:** Watery, warm tone (yellow ochre + touch of burnt sienna, medium strength)
  - **Edge:** Hard edge at top (below sky), soft at bottom where it transitions to shadow
  - **Execution:** One broad passage from left to right, following the wall plane, connecting all lit areas

- **Foliage region (right):** Wet on dry, separate passage
  - **Paper state:** Dry
  - **Load:** Pale yellow-green wash (cadmium yellow + cerulean, very watery)
  - **Execution:** Irregular canopy shape, wet-in-wet within the region, soften upper edge into sky

### Stage 3: Dry, mid-value refinement (Dry on Dry)
- **Window sills and horizontal coursing:** Mid-grey value, crisp edges
  - **Paper state:** Dry
  - **Load:** Medium, dry brush for stone texture (ultramarine + burnt sienna grey)
  - **Execution:** Short horizontal strokes following architecture, one hard edge (shadow side), soft on light side

- **Tower mid values:** Shadow side of tower
  - **Load:** Warmer grey (raw sienna + ultramarine)
  - **Move:** Dry on dry, controlled edge on tower outline

### Stage 4: Shadow Glaze (Cross-boundary, Dry Base)
- **One unified shadow glaze across the shadow side of the building:**
  - From right tower, across central windows into left side
  - Runs down left wall, across lower wall
  - Extends from foliage onto wall below

- **Paper state:** Completely dry before this glaze
- **Load:** Medium to thick, low saturation (ultramarine + alizarin, muted)
- **Execution:**
  1. One connected passage
  2. Hard edge on lit side (catches light)
  3. Soft edge where it turns into background or merges with foliage
  4. Stays below 60% opacity to show wall color beneath

### Stage 5: Street and Foreground (Darker value)
- **Paper state:** Dry
- **Load:** Thick, mid-dark (ultramarine + alizarin + touch ochre)
- **Move:** Wet on dry, blocky shapes for street and figures
- **Execution:** Define figure and street pavement, darker than wall, lighter than window recesses

### Stage 6: Window Recesses and Architecture Details
- **Paper state:** Dry
- **Load:** Thick, darkest value (black + ultramarine, or drop alizarin for warmth in shadows)
- **Move:** Dry on dry, small, precise marks
- **Execution:**
  - Window interiors (darkest), recessed depth
  - Dark shadow under arches
  - Corner shadows at building junctions
  - NOT a fill: painted shapes with purpose, not solid blocks

### Stage 7: Closing Ink (Last, Focal Point Only)
- **Paper state:** Completely dry
- **Ink:** Sepia or black, not blotchy (tied to base paint quality)
- **Move:** Pen or brush-pen, selective lines only
- **Execution:**
  - Reinforce mullion definition where it clarifies form
  - Enhance window corners and arch lines (already painted, ink adds weight)
  - Clock face or key architectural feature in tower
  - Taper all lines with pressure variation
  - NO outlines of the whole building — structure already exists from paint
  - Gestural scribble only in very dark shadow areas (under eaves, deep recesses)

## Layer Dependencies

1. **Sky wash** dries fully before wall touches it (no bleed)
2. **Wall base** dries before shadow glaze is laid
3. **Windows left unpainted** until stage 6, so stage 4 glaze can cross cleanly
4. **Foliage** same drying checkpoint as wall
5. **Ink only after all paint is dry**

## Wet Passages and Barriers

- **Sky (Stage 1):** One wet passage, isolated from wall below by a dry barrier zone (top 1–2 mm of wall stays dry during sky application)
- **Wall and foliage:** Separate dry-on-dry regions, allow foliage edge to soften where it overlaps into wall space
- **No detail region (eyes, mullions, fold detail) overlaps a wet passage** — all stay dry until opening drawing and final accents

## Resolution Checks

- **Sheet size:** 280 mm = 1200 px output
- **Native density for detail:** Windows and mullions (target spans multiple cells)
- **Brush vs. mark:** Window mullions handled by opening ink and closing ink, not paint alone
- **Cell size at 1200 px:** 0.23 mm/px; a 3 mm feature = ~13 cells (adequate for soft edge control)

## Progression and Value Ladder

| Stage | Content | Value | Intent |
|-------|---------|-------|--------|
| Opening | Pen lines | — | Structure, definition |
| 1 | Sky wash | Very pale (20–40%) | Atmospheric base, sky color and light |
| 2 | Wall + foliage base | Pale (40–50%) | Local color, form through value planes |
| 3 | Midtone coursing | Mid (50–65%) | Architecture form, stone planes |
| 4 | Shadow glaze | Mid-dark (60–70%) | Unified shading, depth, turn of form |
| 5 | Street/foreground | Dark (65–75%) | Ground plane, spatial recession |
| 6 | Windows + deep shadow | Very dark (80–95%) | Focal point depth, recess definition |
| Closing | Ink detail | Black (100%) | Structural clarity, finishing accent |

**Progression:** Light to dark, full value range by Stage 6, focal point (windows) darkest.

## Failure Prevention

- **Blur risk:** All detail (mullions, edges, coursing) painted on dry paper with controlled load. Ink over blurry paint is a failure — paint first, sharp, then ink sparingly.
- **Muddy walls:** One light wash connects the lit plane; shadow glaze is semi-transparent, not opaque. No repeated washes on same area.
- **Lost edges:** Hard edges (window outline, building edge) are built into Stage 2 base wash. Soft edges (cloud, foliage merge) softened on one side only, clear intent.
- **Patchwork:** Shadow glaze crosses regions as one passage, unifying right side, left side, and lower building in value and mood.
- **Window recesses too dark too early:** Windows left unpainted through Stages 1–5, then painted last before ink, preserving paper luminosity and the transition of values.

## Token Changes from V096 Base

- `_lay3` instead of `_lay4`: Fewer mid-value passes (three instead of four) to avoid overworking the wall
- `_sep6` instead of `_sep8`: Smaller separation factor for tighter detail control on windows
- `_ink2` instead of `_ink4`: Lighter ink application (two passes instead of four) to avoid heavy ink over soft paint

**Reason:** This is a precise architectural style requiring hard edges and sharp definition. Fewer mid-passes and lighter ink prevent the blurry, heavy-ink-with-blur failure seen in prior runs. The opening drawing carries structure; ink is selective and light.

## Expected Appearance at Each Checkpoint

- **After opening drawing:** Clean pen lines showing building structure, window grid, tower geometry
- **After Stage 1:** Pale sky with soft cloud suggestion, no building yet
- **After Stage 2:** Sky + pale yellow wall + pale green foliage, building silhouette emerges in local color
- **After Stage 3:** Horizontal stone coursing visible, tower mid-tones, building form clearer
- **After Stage 4:** Shadow side visible, depth emerges, foliage darkens
- **After Stage 5:** Street grounds the composition, figures suggested
- **After Stage 6:** Windows are dark, architecture detailed, focal point clear
- **After closing ink:** Mullions and arches crisp, final architectural clarity

## Reserves Toned (If Overexposed)

- Cloud lights: toned with one pale blue glaze if too bright
- Tower top: kept light; if too stark white, one very pale wash
- Wall catch light: left mostly white; one very pale ochre glaze only if needed

**Plan follows the light-to-dark watercolor technique, precise-watercolor style, with emphasis on hard edges for architecture, soft edges for sky and foliage, and opening pen structure carrying clarity.**
