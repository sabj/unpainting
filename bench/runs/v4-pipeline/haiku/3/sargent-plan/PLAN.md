# Watercolor Plan: White Ships (Sargent) — V4 Try 3

## 1. Value Shapes and Focal Point

**Thumbnail values (3–5 distinct steps):**
- Light (L): pale cream sky (0.85–0.95), white paper foam on water
- Light-mid (LM): pale-to-medium sky-blue, pale water greens, bright hull plane
- Mid (M): water medium blue-green, sail ochre base, figure ochre
- Mid-dark (MD): hull shadow side, water shadows, sail fold shadows
- Dark (D): dark sail accents, figure and rigging silhouettes, deep water

**Focal point:** the boat hull and its surrounding water, center-right of the composition. The interplay of the warm sail ochres against the cool hull-blue and greens.

## 2. Light Reserves and Catches

- **Bare white paper:** bright foam patches on water surface (scattered ~0.35–0.85 horizontal band)
- **Pale painted plane:** sky light area (top third), pale tint allowed but paper shows
- **Boat bright edge:** hull lit plane (center-right), preserved in pale blue-grey wash
- **Water glints:** small toned (not blazing white) highlights on water surface, reserved early

## 3. Layering Order and Technique

**Stage 0: Opening Drawing**
- None. Pure watercolor; minimal graphite structure guides only, no ink.
- Relying on paint shape definition.

**Stage 1: Pale Sky Wash** (Layer 1, Light)
- **Regions:** Sky family (top 35%)
- **Move:** Wet on dry (paper damp, not glossy wet)
- **Paper state:** Evenly damp, shine just gone, to allow soft grading without blooms
- **Load:** Watery, pale ochre + pale blue mix (warm-to-cool grading)
- **Pigments:** Raw sienna, cerulean, touch of ultramarine
- **Stroke pattern:** One big connected passage left-to-right, grading warm-cream on the left to pale blue-grey on the right
- **Edges:** All soft; no crisp edges in this wash
- **Reserve:** Boat bright plane and water foam marked on paper, never touched
- **Check:** Paper state across the whole region before each major stroke; prevent blooms by working at right sheen

**Stage 2: Pale Water Wash** (Layer 1, Light-Mid)
- **Regions:** Sea family (lower 65%)
- **Move:** Wet on dry (dry paper or slightly damp, not wet)
- **Paper state:** Dry or barely damp
- **Load:** Watery, pale blue-green (cerulean + raw sienna pale)
- **Stroke pattern:** One connected large passage, pale and transparent, east-west strokes covering the water band
- **Edges:** Soft at the top (into sky), crisp-soft transition at figure/boat area
- **Reserve:** All white foam patches; boat hull rim untouched
- **Intent:** Establish light base; paper showing through; no value yet

**Stage 3: Dry, Check Paper State**

**Stage 4: Hull and Sail Base Colors** (Layer 2, Light-Mid to Mid)
- **Boat hull:** Blue-grey base, dry paper, one hard edge on the lit plane (left side), soft turn on the shadow side (right). Medium load, blue with hint of grey (ultramarine + sienna grey mix). Keep the lit edge crisp; shadow turns soft into water.
- **Sails:** Ochre and warm brown base, dry paper. Pale ochre-sienna on lit areas, darker brown (burnt sienna) on cooler shadow-side folds. Each sail region keeps its shape edges: hard where cloth folds catch light, soft where it turns away.
- **Figures:** Warm ochre-brown wash on the left, figures abstracted as value shapes, not detail. Preserve some white paper within for light planes.
- **Water mid-tones:** Darker blue-green glazed over the first pale wash on dry paper, connecting water regions but leaving foam reserves untouched. Medium load, blue-green (ultramarine + raw sienna).
- **Edges:** Focal region (hull/sail) gets hard edges on one side, soft on the other. Distant water and sky edges soften.

**Stage 5: Dry, Check Paper State**

**Stage 6: Shadow and Dark Glazes** (Layer 3, Mid-Dark to Dark)
- **Sail shadow folds:** One connected glaze across sail regions on dry paper, rich dark brown-blue (ultramarine + burnt sienna, low water). Hard edge on the crease side (where cloth folds define shadow), soft edge where shadow fades into lit area.
- **Hull shadow side:** Cool dark glaze (ultramarine + touch of sienna) on the right shadow plane, one hard edge on the hull crease, soft edge into water reflection. Preserves water foam below.
- **Water shadow glaze:** Darker blue-green across the water (ultramarine + raw sienna, more pigment) on dry paper, one glaze connecting beneath the boat and figures, unifying the dark areas.
- **Figure and rigging accents:** Dark shapes (ultramarine + burnt sienna very concentrated) on the left side—rigging lines and figure silhouettes, but no detail marks. Keep these gestural and light.

**Stage 7: Final Dry, Check Paper State**

| Sky | Pale warm-to-cool grading, soft edges | Wash | N/A (soft blending) | Wet on dry, blended | Damp, shine gone | Watery, pale ochre + blue | All soft | 1 |
| Sea | Broken mid-blue-green, white foam speckles | Wash | Foam ~2–4 cells | Wet on dry, two passes | Dry, then damp | Watery, medium blue-green | Soft at top, soft-crisp below | 1, 2 |

## 5. Glaze List

1. **Water shadow glaze:** Ultramarine + raw sienna, dark, connects sea and under-hull, dry paper, one passage beneath boat center
2. **Sail shadow glaze:** Ultramarine + burnt sienna, dark brown-blue, connects sail folds across the sail region, dry paper
3. **Hull shadow glaze:** Ultramarine + touch sienna, cool dark, shadow plane right side, dry paper

All glazes cross region boundaries where the reference shows shadow continuity (e.g., shadow under the boat runs across water and hull edge).

## 6. Wet Passages and Dry Barriers

- **Wet passage 1:** Sky alone, no spillover into hull or water
- **Dry barrier:** Between sky and water (dry paper for water base)
- **Wet passage 2 (optional):** Boat hull + immediate water reflection, if charged into damp water wash; else all dry
- **Dry paper:** All mid-value and dark passes (hull base, sails base, glazes)

Water's first pale wash is wet on dry to control where it goes; hull and sails must dry before shadow glazes to avoid mud.

## 7. Ink and Drawing

**Opening:** None (pure watercolor).
**Closing:** None (pure watercolor, no ink accents).

All form and contrast come from paint layers and light-to-dark progression.

## 8. Breaks from Default Light-to-Dark

**No breaks.** The entire painting follows light-to-dark:
1. Pale sky wash
2. Pale water wash
3. Mid-tone hulls and sails on dry paper
4. Dark shadow glazes last

## 9. Stage Intents

| Stage | Checkpoint | Expected Look |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Opening (if any) | Minimal or none |
| 1 | Sky wash | Pale warm-cool grading; soft edges; no detail |
| 2 | Water pale wash + hull/sail base colors | Distinct hull blue, sail ochre; water pale; still mostly light and airy |
| 3 | Shadow glazes + water darks | Dark folds in sails visible; hull shadow appears; water darkens and unifies; foam still reads as light |
| 4 | Final dry | All layers visible; no mud; paper lights preserved; marks gestural and confident |

## 10. Pigment and Material Choices

**Palette:** Raw sienna (warm earth), burnt sienna (dark warm), ultramarine (cool dark blue), cerulean (bright cool blue), raw umber or ultramarine grey (for hull greys)

**Load consistency:**
- Sky: very watery (transparent)
- First water: watery, pale
- Hulls/sails base: medium (more pigment, still flowing)
- Shadow glazes: medium to thick (opaque-looking but still glaze behavior on dry paint)

**Paper:** Cold-press assumed; granulation in water and darks will add character.

## 11. Plan Checks

✓ Light-to-dark layering: sky → water → hulls/sails → shadows
✓ All reserves named (foam, sky light, hull bright edge)
✓ No detail region inside a wet passage by spill
✓ Focal point (hull/sails) has clear hard-soft edge definition
✓ Dry paper for mid and dark layers prevents mud
✓ All glazes justified by reference (shadows crossing regions)
✓ No ink; all contrast from paint transparency and value
✓ Loose Sargent style: few purposeful strokes, big washes, soft edges

## Summary

Pure watercolor rendering of a maritime scene. Light-to-dark layering through washes (sky, water) and glazes (shadows). Focal area (boat and sails) gets the sharpest edges and brightest value contrast. Loose, gestural, with paper lights preserved to give air and luminosity. No ink or closed detail; form carried by value shapes and edges alone.
