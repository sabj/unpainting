## Read the reference

The focal point is the sunlit central Gothic gable and its two large arched windows. The cropped tall chimney, gable apex, second chimney, four upper lancets, paired arches and stacked rectangular openings establish likeness. Cool recesses must remain subordinate to broad golden stone planes. The lower right tree is yellow-green and connected, while the left wing is cooler and darker. The sky is pale blue, with soft white clouds and hard roof silhouettes.

Five-value thumbnail: (1) paper cloud lights, thin sunlit trim and clothing glints; (2) pale blue sky, cream stone and pavement; (3) golden wall planes and yellow-green canopy; (4) blue-grey roofs, cool chimney sides, wall shadows and olive foliage bases; (5) deep window/door recesses, the banner and sparse ground accents. Paint proceeds from 2 through 5, with value 1 protected.

## Regions, scale and technique

Family boxes describe the location of reference shapes. The executor retains its fitted colour/value silhouettes; no family box is a fill rectangle. All families retain precise-watercolor style, so a box cannot impose an atmospheric style seam. After the first run's wet-footprint refusal, the sky's explicitly chosen first move is a connected flowing wet-on-dry wash, with fitted sky lanes; its middle pigment is consolidated with sk1. Own same-passage wet overlap is allowed. This suits the confined sky between hard architectural silhouettes without pre-wetting small fragments. A pale first sky tint may share a cool colour family with glass, but final window detail is always painted after drying.

Native cell width is 0.2333 mm. One source pixel spans four native cells (0.933 mm). A wash needs about four cells or 0.93 mm across. Window masses of 3–10 source pixels span 12–40 cells and belong to paint. Mullions and fine arch rims of approximately 0.4–1 source pixel span 1.6–4 cells, so their fine edges belong to drawing. No density upscaling is substituted for a native inspection.

## Protected lights and glazes

Use the executor's target-derived connected paper-light mask. No rectangular reserves: a rectangle would amputate irregular cloud or stone edges. Bare-paper lights are the brightest cloud openings, narrow white chips at the central left arris, glints on projecting roof trim, and the strongest small shirt highlights. Pale painted planes are the broad front gable, light arch surrounds, roof ledge and pavement; they receive the light local-colour band and are not enlarged into paper-white cutouts. Small mullion gaps are negative-painted openings between dark recess shapes. No white paint or late invented lifts.

After the middle layers dry, the first connected cool glaze joins the left chimney side, left wall and window reveals. A second target-supported shadow system links central arch reveals, lower recesses and adjacent stone; eave shadows cross the low right wall and windows. Ground shadow connects foliage bases to pavement. Preserve the fitted cross-region shadow geometry. Use the base ultramarine/raw-sienna neutral mixture for cool masonry shadows, water .45 with controlled medium pigment, and the fitted mixtures for the darkest recesses. Two transparent complementary shadow steps maximum, followed only by selective accents.

## Drawing and order

Opening: sparse HB graphite, pressure .22, on dry paper. Authored connected reference paths retain the gable roof, the two chimney crowns/arrises, major wall junctions, paired Gothic arches and principal window groups. Fine mullions and leaf texture are omitted here. An opening checkpoint should look like a light architectural setup, with large areas still blank.

Paint: pale connected local colours; dry; three middle value steps; dry; restrained stone variation within broad planes; dry; cross-region shadows; dry; dark recess accents. The base's irregular ashlar hints remain paint on dry paper, not graphite hatching. All passages now start on dry paper; the connected sky wash merges its own wet overlaps. The precise architectural boundaries and confined sky shapes justify this adaptation from the usual pre-wet sky recipe.

Closing: selective target-supported sepia ink on dry paint. Traced fine paths are selected inside window, arch, chimney crown and banner regions; noisy stone/foliage fragments are discarded. A small set of structural roof/junction paths supplements these. Ink pigment is explicitly sepia-ink; pressure is capped at .55, and recipe sep4 halves the base sep8 strength setting. Dark window interiors are painted masses, never ink fills.

For every brush or drawing stroke, the fixed executor must measure the whole footprint and neighbouring paper. Dry means the previous layer passes its physical dry gate. Same-passage overlap may remain wet. If the intended state is absent, the executor dries or waits through its existing physical route. Check actual moisture/technique records when available; never claim a reading that was not exposed.

## Choices relative to the fixed base

Use region_w13. Replace lay4 with lay3: fewer separately dried middle contours while retaining gradual value development in the walls. Add sk1: large sky components receive their middle pigment in the first middle wash rather than acquiring quantile-shaped blue patches. Replace sep8 with sep4 and cap closing pressure at .55: the requested style needs transparent paint and fine supporting structure. Set opening graphite and a light .22 pressure explicitly. Preserve the base darkgain 1.3, warm/green palette, recess pair, irregular stone hints, cold-press paper and raw-sienna shadow mixture. No global water reduction, extra splatter, stroke-plan lanes on facades, or decorative hatching.
