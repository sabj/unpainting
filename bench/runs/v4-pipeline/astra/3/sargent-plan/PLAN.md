## Reference and value design

The principal form is the curved blue-white hull through the centre, opposed to the angular cream-white hull at the right. Warm ropes, spars and rusty fittings cross them, with a pale ochre anchor prominent at lower left. Dark scuppers, the upper-left gunwale, and the dark lower-centre wedge anchor the composition. The small green-blue water area is subordinate. This crop contains no broad open sky.

Five-value thumbnail, relative to the warm paper: (1) off-white upper/right hull lights and narrow rigging gaps; (2) pale cream and pale blue hull planes; (3) blue-grey hull turns, muted teal water and ochre ropes; (4) connected lower-hull shadows and the left gunwale; (5) selective dark ports and rope junctions. The focal relationship is the central curved hull, its dark fitting near source (143, 68), and the warm rope across its lower-left edge. The oval near (252, 46) is a secondary anchor. Do not promote every rust fleck to a focal point.

## Executable choices and their reasons

- `precise-watercolor` throughout: dry placement retains the angular reflections and rope structure. Family boxes describe the reference; target-derived connected shapes determine paint geometry. No rectangular style override or reserve cuts the wash.
- `lay2`: two transparent middle layers instead of four, to avoid repeated nested rims and let broad hull planes remain luminous. Two final connected shadow glazes remain.
- `cg10`: remove the base's 1.3 cool boost; this reference is muted blue-grey, not saturated cobalt.
- `dk12`: retain decisive dark structure with a moderate 1.2 shadow gain rather than the base's 1.3.
- `rg0`: remove the automatic horizontal lower-ground glaze. There is no ground plane here; the ordinary connected target shadow support supplies the lower-hull glazes without a height-based transition.
- Initial `noslane`: fitted connected hull gestures rather than the base's horizontal sky lanes through broad cool shapes. Removed at boundary 17 after the actual wash revealed incomplete coverage and diagonal gaps; see the revision below.
- `acc3`: reserve part of small dark-feature demand for the final dry accents, so ports and rope junctions appear after the hull planes.
- Palette: yellow ochre, burnt sienna, French ultramarine, cerulean and sepia watercolor. Ochre/blue mixtures cover the subdued green water. Sepia is a watercolor pan, not a drawing medium. Both line documents are empty with medium `none`; `--drawing none` explicitly disables opening and closing drawing.
- Keep the physical cold-press paper, ordinary drying, fitted geometry, pigment calibration and guarded reads. No synthetic texture, opaque white or post-render adjustment.

## Paper state and progression

The fixed value executor measures the whole proposed footprint and neighbouring cells before every stroke. Wet-on-dry pigment is admitted only on dry paper, apart from the same passage's own open wet overlap. It dries and remeasures a footprint when required and refuses an unsatisfied gate. Retain this behavior and its `value-commands.jsonl` evidence. Within a large wash, adjacent strokes join while open; every new middle plane or glaze starts on dry paper. The plan deliberately avoids whole-area wet-in-wet because the crop's defining cues are dry-edged hull planes, ropes and fittings; there is no open atmospheric sky requiring it.

Expected checkpoints:

1. Opening: warm paper only, zero drawn lines.
2. First value band: a pale connected cool/warm arrangement, recognizable broad hulls but no prematurely black fittings. Judge wash continuity and light survival, not final contrast.
3. Middle layers: the central curved hull, angular right hull, left rigging and lower-right water become distinct. Still lighter than final in the deepest shadows.
4. First shadow glaze: connected lower-hull depth, dry crisp overlap edges, lighter ropes intact.
5. Final shadows and accents: fittings and dark wedge have weight; pale hull planes still dominate. Closing stage adds no drawing.

No departure from light-to-dark order is planned. Because this task permits painting only through `bench/paint.sh`, the first actual wash checkpoint replaces a separate crop physics proof. ## Acceptance and failure signs

Apply the technique guide's final checklist in order: thumbnail value shapes, surviving correctly placed lights, hard/soft/lost edges, fitting and hull landmarks, purposeful mark character, and credible light-to-dark progression. Check the compiler for unsupported straight seams, absent brush paths and disconnected large planes. Native 1200 px images judge edge and mark character; small whole-sheet views judge values and composition.

Failure means saturated blue replacing grey hulls, broad hull lights going dark, a horizontal stripe at the lower third, nested contour rims, patchy unpainted interiors, blurred ropes or ports, a dark outline around every shape, or many disconnected little dots replacing a wash. A passing tool check is execution admission, not proof of artistic success. Record observations and any revisions here before finishing. Stop once the painting is finished.
