# Mittens V4 painting plan

## Read of the plate

This is a high-key watercolor portrait sketch on white paper: a ginger-and-white cat at left overlaps a person's pale face at right. The useful value thumbnail has four steps: (1) white paper and the catchlights, (2) a very pale warm wash on the cat's lit fur and the person's skin, (3) the orange markings, facial turns, and gray-brown hair lights, and (4) the cat's eyes and deepest warm-black curl valleys. The joint cat-and-person gaze is the focal point; the cat's eye and the person's eyes carry the likeness. The dark hair is a supporting mass, not a solid black shape.

Use precise-watercolor overall for painted planes, reserved lights, and dry focal edges. The only override is mittens.hair in ink-and-wash: its small curls are below wash resolution, so keep one connected dark painted mass and add a few directional lines. The cat's coat stays wash led; its fine whiskers and coat separations come from the existing line drawing rather than stipple or a dark ink fill. The pale skin stays wash led with dry eye and mouth accents. The white ground is mostly untouched paper, not a background wash.

## Regions and resolution choices

- **Cat, mittens.cat** — The face and ear have broad white fur, warm orange patches, soft transitions at the cheek, and thin whiskers. Use one pale, connected wet-on-dry foundation over the cat's soft coat on dry paper; keep the wash watery and broad, with a rounded broken edge. Reserve the eye, nose glint, whisker gaps and brightest muzzle, and keep a dry barrier around the eye and nose. After the foundation dries, shape the orange patches and cheek planes wet-on-dry; soften only the cheek-turning edge with clean water while the paint is still workable. A single light shadow glaze crosses the ear, crown and cheek on dry paper. Texture question: value wash for the coat, ink-and-wash only for the sub-brush whisker and fur boundary lines already in the drawing. The narrowest whisker is about one source pixel, or four output cells at 1200 px; its line is therefore drawn, not painted as a brush mark. The cat eye is roughly 10–14 output cells across. With the sepia nib's 0.12–0.45 mm contact (about 0.6–2.3 cells in radius at 0.195 mm per output cell), eye contours remain several nib radii wide. Pale wash uses a watery load; patch and shadow passes use medium loads. The eye and nose edges stay crisp; the cheek turns softly on one side.
- **Person's skin, mittens.face** — A single broad pale warm wash, wet-on-dry on dry paper or evenly damp only across the skin plane; do not carry it through the cat's eye or into the hair. Dry, then use restrained medium-load planes at the brow/eye sockets, side of the nose, under the nose and lip, and jaw. One pale shadow glaze crosses the temple into the cheek and nose-side plane after the local colors are dry. The eyes, nostrils and mouth corners are ink-and-wash details over the skin plane; eyelid strokes are line features, not painted creases. Human eye lines are about 8–16 output cells long/wide in their local shapes, several times the nib radius; preserve catchlights and keep sclera a pale tint rather than filling it with skin color. Use hard edges for the lids and nostrils, one softened cheek edge, and leave other face edges lost.
- **Hair, mittens.hair** — One connected mixed warm gray-brown foundation, then the two middle layers on dry paper. Let the target's irregular hair silhouette carry form. Keep the curls as a few curved directional accents on the dry mass, varying length and pressure; no uniform hatch or isolated dots. The dark channels can be the darkest value, but the crown and curl shoulders retain lighter paint. The dark-curl override is limited to neutral dark cells inside this family box (--cellfamilies 1), not the orange coat or skin.
- **Paper and overlap** — Leave the white background bare and let the image vignette into it. The cat's white muzzle, cheek, ear edges, whiskers, and the person's forehead/cheek lights are primarily held by the painter's near-paper protection. Four small reserves protect the cat catchlight, both human catchlights, and the narrow nose ridge. Their rectangles are under 0.03 of the target width/height, so they protect local lights without imposing long artificial straight seams.

## Drawing, layers and values

The opening drawing is 12 selected target-traced gestures: cat eye, muzzle/cheek, the cat-person boundary, hair edge, and the person's eye, nose and mouth landmarks. They are fine sepia ink on a dry sheet at the light opening pressure. No traced mass is filled with ink. The closing drawing uses the full 66 thin, target-supported contours and tonal details, with the base sepia ink route; it restores only the lines that remain legible after paint. The eye lights stay outside both drawings where possible and remain protected from every wash.

The sequence is: sparse opening lines; the cat's watery light foundation and the skin's pale local wash, both wet-on-dry; dry; two broad middle passes (lay2) to develop warm fur patches, skin planes and curl shoulders; dry; connected shadow glazes across each subject's adjacent planes; dry; dark curl and eye accents, then selective closing ink. I initially planned wet-in-wet for the cat's soft undercoat, but the measured footprint at the first passage did not meet the moisture gate. The clean-sheet retry uses a broad wet-on-dry wash and softens only the cheek turn with clean water, matching the feathery coat edge without asking a dry sheet to merge pigment. Skin, hair details, and all dark accents stay on dry paper. Before each stroke, the executor must check the measured paper state across its full footprint and neighboring cells: use a dry paper for the planned wet-on-dry and detail work; wait or work elsewhere if the local state is wrong. Keep watery water separate from pigment strength; no invented paper readings.

## What to watch at the passage stops

- After opening lines: both faces are recognizable from placement and proportion; no eye, nose, or mouth has become a heavy outline.
- After the light wash: paper remains dominant, the cat reads as white-and-ginger, and the person's skin remains pale. Catchlights and the nose ridge are still clear.
- After each middle pass: orange markings stay connected to the cat's planes, the face has a cheek/nose turn rather than a patchwork, and hair shows a crown with lighter shoulders rather than one flat block.
- After the shadow glaze: the cat and human face retain separate silhouettes; there are no rectangular family edges, dark rims, or blooms. The eye whites and cat muzzle remain pale.
- Before closing ink: check the actual native 1200 px details. The cat eye, both human eyes, nose and mouth should be in proportion; lines should be fine and selective, not doubled into heavy outlines. If a defect first appears in one layer, revise only the remaining plan to correct that layer's cause.

Failure signs are a missing catchlight, a blank or blurred eye, a flat black hair cap, orange fur broken into disconnected islands, a polygonal face, halo rims, muddy warm shadows, or repetitive parallel lines. Judge value masses, lights, edge roles, landmarks, then mark character; do not use pixel error as the art verdict.

## V4 choices and reasons

This independent try follows the requested light-wash portrait-sketch style: two middle layers instead of the base four, no sky-lane option on a plate with no sky, fine source-traced opening and closing lines, and a localized ink-and-wash hair override. I kept the champion dark range for the cat's eyes and hair valleys, since reducing all darks would flatten the strongest reference landmarks. After pass two, I saw repeated parallel dry-brush tracks and an automatic sap-green pass that the reference does not support. The painter locks its palette after paint reaches the sheet, so I kept the existing sepia, raw sienna and sap green pigments and pinned them explicitly with `--paints`; the green remains a limitation of the already-started sheet. Passage-level brush choice adds variation. The tracks persisted through pass five. A trial to raise the dry-brush threshold failed the planning preview twice with exit 137, so the remaining plan retains the admitted technique settings. Preserve short fur texture in the existing line drawing; let the connected late glazes define the final curl and eye darks, then assess whether those value layers integrate the tracks.
