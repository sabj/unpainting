# Trumbull, V4 independent try 3 — precise architectural watercolor

## Reference reading and value plan

The crop is a close, upward view of a warm sandstone university facade. The central triangular gable and its paired arches are the focal landmark. A taller, darker tower interrupts the left roof; a narrow chimney and lower annex balance the right. The target has a pale blue broken sky, hard roof and window geometry, small green masses at the lower edge, and deep recesses that hold the facade together.

Use five value groups: (1) paper and near-paper cloud gaps / small facade glints; (2) pale warm stone and lit roof faces; (3) ochre middle planes and slate-blue roofs; (4) cool side planes, eaves, and foliage shadows; (5) the deepest windows, sign, and door recesses. The sunlit gable is a pale painted plane, not a blank reserve. The focal point is the paired arched windows and the gable-to-roof junction above them.

At the frozen 1200 × 1052 output on 280 mm paper, a native cell is 0.233 mm. The narrowest useful window mullions and dark frame strokes are about one source pixel, or 4 output cells / 0.93 mm. This is an ink-and-wash resolution question: hold those fine structures with the pen after the wash. With the width-limited lines and adjusted pen gain, the expected nib radius is about 1.3–2 output cells (0.30–0.47 mm), at most half the width of a four-cell mullion. Let paint describe larger arch and wall planes; do not ask a wash to draw a mullion.

## Order, light protection, and paper-state checks

1. **Opening drawing, before washes.** On dry paper, use the 32 target-traced structural spans in opening-lines.json: roof and chimney junctions, selected wall corners, arches, and repeated window structure. Lines are target-supported and width-limited for this sheet. Draw only the paths in the file; no hatch, masonry fill, or invented straight guide. Use the declared neutral sepia ink. The first wash must wait until the opening ink is dry.
2. **Light washes.** Keep the gable and major lit stone faces as pale ochre-tinted planes. Work a connected sky passage only within each actual sky wedge. Apply one broad light passage per connected wall/roof/foliage shape and allow the sheet to dry before the next value step. Do not force separate value islands to merge across a real corner.
3. **Mid values on dry paper.** After checking the complete mark footprint and its neighboring paint is dry, place the cool roof planes, wall turns, tower/annex separation, and window mid-values. Use broad, purposeful wet-on-dry planes, not many small repairs. Keep the primary gable and narrow stone borders visibly lighter than their recesses.
4. **Shadow glazes on dry paper.** A connected cool shadow shape crosses the shaded wall plane, its eaves, and the corresponding roof junctions; the cast recess shadow also passes across window/door borders where the target supports it. Check that the entire glaze footprint is dry before every stroke. Keep the facade-side boundary crisp; soften only the selected turning edge while the neighboring paper state allows it. Let foliage overlap the building only at its actual lower contact.
5. **Deep recesses and closing drawing.** Add the last paint darks to windows, arches, the sign, and door recesses on dry paper. After confirming the full sheet is dry, the filtered target-supported sepia closing lines restore only hard architectural structure. Protect all pale stone rims and cloud gaps from the pen.

Before each stroke the executor must check measured paper state over the full footprint and the neighbor halo, together with water and pigment load. A dry move waits or moves elsewhere if any part is wet; a deliberately wet sky blend proceeds only when the whole intended sky footprint has the required sheen. No fixed wait time substitutes for the state check, and no moisture reading is inferred from the plan.

Protected lights are classified as follows: pale cloud gaps and small reflected glints remain target-relative near-paper light; the large sunlit gable, lit tower face, and pale arch rims are pale local-color planes; small light spaces between foliage remain negative-painted gaps in the fitted segmentation. No broad rectangular reserve is added, so no unsupported straight edge cuts a wash. Tone a near-paper light only if the painted result is materially brighter than its local target plane.

## Edges, ink, and failure signs

Keep the roof silhouette, gable corners, chimney spans, arch rims, and window openings crisp. Soften one chosen wall turn and selected foliage/ground joins; lose only those lower edges that actually merge in the reference. A uniform halo around every plane is a failure. Ink is neutral sepia as selected by the frozen W4 recipe; verify the paint log reports sepia rather than a wash-palette blue. Ink carries mullions and junctions, while dark recess bodies remain paint.

Failure is visible if the gable or paired arches shift, the lit facade becomes a flat dark block, the roof and wall collapse into one value, the side shadow breaks into pale islands or separate tiles, the windows turn into ink fills, foliage becomes equal dots, or closing ink outlines every stone. At each stop judge thumbnail value grouping first, then surviving lights, edge roles, roofline / chimney / arch landmarks, and mark economy. Judge detail at the native 1200 render; a reduced whole-sheet view supports composition only.

## Stage intents

- Opening stop: a light, legible architectural skeleton with the gable, tower/chimneys, selected arches, and repeated openings still readable; it must not look like all-over stone hatching.
- After the first wash: near-paper sky and warm wall planes remain dominant; the main facade is connected and broad, without scratchy islands or dark contour bands.
- Midway: roof planes, tower separation, and side-wall turn are distinct while the gable stays the focal light.
- Before closing drawing: recess values and shadow planes carry the architecture; the lit jambs and cloud gaps survive and the whole sheet is dry.
- Finished: selective sepia structure sharpens windows and roof junctions without overpowering the transparent stone washes.

## Fast proof and revision rule

The trace overlays were reviewed at source scale, and opening paths are limited to target-supported architecture. The first light-wash stop is the smallest physical technique proof in this fixed-sheet task; use it to check pale connected wall coverage and the sky edge before proceeding to mid values. Stop and revise the unpainted plan at the first visible failure; preserve the already-painted sheet and do not use added ink to hide a blurred or fragmented wash.
