# Hills, round V4 try 3 of 3

## Reading the crop

At thumbnail size the plate has four values: warm pale grass and the winding track; yellow-olive lit slopes; middle-value green folds; and deep cool woodland. The track bends from the upper middle through the right-hand middle and back toward the foreground. It is the focal route. The wooded mass is broad and irregular, with dark pockets and a broken boundary against the grass. There is no sky in this crop.

## Region and resolution decisions

- **Grass and rolling slopes:** wash question. The broad slopes read as connected color and value planes; the smallest hill turn is wider than four output cells at 1224 px. Use broad connected passes, with a crisp edge along the track where the source has one and softer or lost edges where the grass runs into woodland.
- **Woodland:** ink-and-wash question for the fine foliage texture, wash question for the mass. At 1224 px, one output cell is about 0.129 mm. Small leaf clusters are roughly 4–12 cells across and individual twigs are often below four cells, so paint the canopy as connected value masses and use only a few broken pen accents. Do not paint each leaf as a dot or fill the darks with ink.
- **Winding track:** wash question. It is broad enough to retain as a pale warm plane rather than outlining it in ink or leaving a bare white channel. Keep its bends legible as the mid and shadow values build.

The broad wash brush radius is about 9.5 mm (74 cells at this scale). The track's narrow stretches are about 32–60 cells wide, or 0.43–0.81 wash radii, so the broad brush must preserve the track as a fitted pale gap instead of trying to draw it. Foliage clusters at 4–12 cells are 0.05–0.16 wash radii; individual twigs below four cells are below the wash threshold and belong to sparse ink. The recipe's pen width range, 0.12–0.45 mm, is about 1–3.5 cells.

The single full-crop family gives all target-derived color shapes the same landscape cue and introduces no internal rectangular boundary. The pale track is a painted light plane, not a white-paper reserve. The initial sheet held it at nearly the paper value, so the revision turns off the broad near-paper holds and lets the warm local wash reach the track; the remaining true white flecks still use the painter's bright-white protection.

## Value and layer plan

1. **Opening drawing on dry paper:** a small number of target-supported, broken contours around the track, hill folds and selected canopy edges. Use the recipe's sepia ink, light pressure and varied width. These are orientation marks, not a traced outline around every color patch.
2. **Light wash on dry paper:** watery-to-medium load (profile water 0.55), transparent yellow ochre, hansa and sap green across the lit slopes, with the pale track kept as a warm tint. Connect neighboring hill planes in broad passages and leave paper showing. The woodland receives a restrained cool green foundation in its existing connected shapes. Let each passage dry before the next.
3. **Middle values on dry paper:** medium load (profile water 0.55), sap green, ochre and ultramarine shape the slope turns and the broad woodland canopy. Keep the grass planes connected, preserve breaks in the canopy edge, and avoid parallel bands. Allow a few hard edges where the track cuts the slope; lose selected edges into the dark trees.
4. **Shadow glaze on dry paper:** controlled, more concentrated load (profile water 0.40) in a transparent cool glaze mixed from sap green, ultramarine and a small amount of sienna or sepia. Deepen the wooded hollows and the shadow side of the slopes. Let the connected shadow cross the grass/woodland boundary where the reference shows one continuous cast form. Retain the light track and pale ridge openings. Do not build the forest from separate dark tiles.
5. **Closing drawing on dry paper:** selective broken sepia lines in the darkest foliage and at a few track or ridge turns. Let the dark mass remain paint; no uniform hatching, closed outlines or ink fill.

Each pigment/value passage begins with the executor's measured paper-state check over its footprint and neighboring cells. The planned paint moves are wet on dry; the sheet is allowed to dry between value steps. If a mark's footprint is not dry, the executor waits or works elsewhere. The line layers also require dry paper. No wet-in-wet break is needed because this crop contains no sky or broad soft cloud transition.

## Recipe choices

- `ink-and-wash` follows the requested loose treatment, with painted land masses and sparse directional ink.
- `_lay2` uses two broad middle-value build steps so the hill folds and wooded mass develop without multiplying small value patches.
- `_sep4` reduces the base recipe's ink strength for a landscape whose fine texture is already dense; the paint must carry the forest shadows.
- The paint set is sap green, ultramarine, ochre, hansa, sienna and sepia: cool greens for the woods, warm yellow-greens for the slopes, and earth color for the track and its turns.
- After looking at the first two light passes, `--paperrel 0` and `--paperlights 0` replace the broad automatic paper hold: it left the focal track too close to bare paper. The remaining near-white flecks stay protected.
- The base recipe's separate granulating ultramarine stays in the palette because blue-gray woodland shadows occupy a substantial part of the crop and show broken texture. This is a deliberate use of the recipe's sky-labeled tube in the cool woods; the plate itself has no sky.

## What would show failure

- The track disappears, becomes a bare white stripe, or gets boxed by a continuous dark outline.
- The forest collapses into one flat green patch, or breaks into tiled dark islands.
- Hill values form parallel stripes, pale interior shards or straight edges unsupported by the terrain.
- Foliage becomes evenly dotted, scratchy or overdrawn; ink takes over the dark mass.
- The light-to-dark progression fails to keep the warm slope lights and track clear against the cool wooded shadows.

At each look, compare the value shapes at thumbnail scale, check that the track and hill openings survive, then check edge variety and the weight of the ink. Keep changes only when they improve those landmarks, restore connected value shapes, or remove one of the listed failures while preserving the planned light-to-dark progression.
