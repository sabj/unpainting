# South France — independent V4 plan

## Reference read and value thumbnail

The crop is filled edge to edge: no open sky or foreground margin. Irregular olive and yellow-green tree crowns overlap across most of the sheet. Dark vertical cypresses and the lower-left trunk break those crowns. Pale ochre stone walls and terracotta roofs are scattered through the upper half, with the clearest focal cluster at centre-right. The houses need crisp plane edges and small dark openings; the foliage should read as connected masses, not leaf-by-leaf stipple.

Four values guide the run:

1. **Reserved light:** scattered leaf glints, pale roof ridges and the sunlit faces of the stone. These are small, irregular target-relative lights, not a white background.
2. **Light wash:** pale olive foliage and warm limestone.
3. **Middle value:** deeper greens, ochre side planes and red-brown roofs.
4. **Dark accents:** cool green canopy pockets, cypress/trunk marks, under-eaves, doors and windows. The focal landmark is the centre-right house group.

## Style and resolution choices

Use **urban-sketch** across the painting: opening black pen, restrained local-colour washes, then selective closing pen on dry paint. The target already carries colour and brush character, so the wash should tint and organize its large values rather than imitate every fleck. No sky override is needed. The compiled ladder has pale target-colour foundations and dry middle planes, followed by two connected shadow glazes. Those glazes follow the target's interlocking dark shapes, crossing adjacent target-derived colour regions where the shadow continues (often foliage into a roof or wall edge); isolated shadow pieces stay local. There is no single cast-shadow silhouette to impose.

At 1216 px on a 280 mm sheet, one native cell is 0.230 mm. House openings and narrow roof details are about 2–3 source pixels, or 8–12 native cells after scaling. The black-ink nib is 0.1–0.4 mm wide (about 0.4–1.7 native cells), leaving at least about 4.7 feature-widths per maximum nib width. Use pen for those narrow edges. Foliage is a **wash question** at crown scale: an individual small leaf is not a separately painted mark; preserve the larger clumps and a few dark branch/cypress accents. Broad wash marks stay outside the small house openings.

## Region technique and order

- **Canopy and distant/foreground trees:** the painter's colour/value segmentation supplies the irregular region shapes; no rectangular family boundary is added. Use a watery, pale olive wash on dry paper in connected crown masses, with visible paper between selected clumps. Add middle green on dry paper only where the reference separates overlapping crowns. Keep edges broken and varied at the crown silhouette; no wet-in-wet spill into the houses, no repeated dots, and no uniform leaf strokes. The cypresses are narrow dark-green value shapes; the lower-left trunk and only a few prominent branch lines are ink-and-wash accents.
- **Stone houses and roofs:** pale, transparent ochre/sienna wash on dry paper, staying inside each already-segmented wall and roof plane. The lit fronts remain broad and light; darker side planes and terracotta roofs receive the next value on dry paper. Hard but slightly irregular wall, roof and eave edges preserve the block shapes. Windows and door recesses are not wash-filled as a group: they remain small dark accents for the closing pen.
- **Protected lights:** no rectangular reserve is declared. The highlights are scattered and follow irregular leaves, roof ridges and wall planes; a box reserve would cut an unsupported seam. Keep the painter's target-relative near-paper lights and do not tone the lightest flecks to match the mid-value wash.
- **Opening drawing:** black ink, dry paper, light controlled pressure. The 28 traced hard-line paths state the roof edges, house junctions and a few strong tree marks before the wash. They are structural gestures rather than a contour around every leaf. Check that the opening stage reads as village architecture with loose trees, not a fully outlined drawing.
- **Wash and value progression:** use the urban-sketch low-load, pale wash over dry paper with a connected broad passage per local mass. Water is watery and pigment light; maintain a continuous body of colour without pooling. Let each wash pass dry fully before the next value. After the dry middle planes, lay the first connected target-shadow glaze over dry paper, then dry again before the second complementary shadow glaze. Both use the simplified connected support chosen by the compiler, cross only where the actual shadow shape crosses segmented colours, and retain the sunlit wall and leaf planes. The two-layer recipe reduces the base's denser stacking while preserving these light-to-dark steps. Do not add a sky wash or wet passage; none is present in the reference.
- **Glaze 1:** on fully dry paint, connect the main cool-green canopy shadow pockets with the attached house-side and roof shadows where the target joins them. Use a transparent medium load; one edge may stay hard at a roof/eave, with the opposite turn softened only where the source does.
- **Glaze 2:** after Glaze 1 is fully dry, deepen only the darkest linked pockets and recess edges over the same target-supported shadow route. Keep it lighter than the closing ink and leave lit walls, roof ridges and leaf glints untouched. Smaller disconnected pockets remain local rather than being bridged across light shapes.
- **Closing drawing:** after the washes are dry, return with black ink at controlled stronger pressure on the selected centre-right house lines only. Emphasize a few eaves, roof junctions and openings. Leave foliage lights untouched and do not use ink to repair soft or muddy paint.

Before every stroke, check the measured paper state across its footprint and neighbours. If a broad passage is still glossy where dry paper is required, wait or work elsewhere; use a damp brush only for a deliberately softened edge. Keep broad washes watery but connected, and the pen controlled on dry paper. The ink pigment is the urban-sketch profile's black ink; the wash palette is fitted to the target's greens and warm stone/roof colours.

## What would show failure

The value thumbnail should still show a broad connected canopy, warm readable houses, and the centre-right focal cluster. Failure signs are leaf-like dots or scratchy texture replacing the tree masses; a flat green blanket that erases the individual crowns; black pen outlining every leaf; thick pen merging window and wall; houses lost beneath dark washes; or the small pale glints disappearing. A long straight seam at a region or reserve boundary is also a failure.

## Recipe changes

This is an independent V4 attempt. The recipe change is `_lay2`: two middle layers instead of the V096 base's four, to keep the requested wash light and reduce repeated darkening while still giving the roofs and overlapping crowns distinct value steps. After the first nine passes showed broad diagonal lanes, `_spl6` was tried for the remaining passes; the first broad 6 mm lane pass introduced finer scalloped repetition, so the plan was returned to the original W13 pathing for the remaining shadow glazes. At pass 12, the cool upper-right distant foliage was still being treated as a tiny sky plane by the color-based passage classifier, although the reference has no open sky. `_noslane` now suppresses the recipe's sky-lane treatment for the remaining cool accents, keeping them as broken landscape shapes. The central bright path remains a target-supported light, not an unpainted seam to cover. The base's black-ink urban-sketch behavior remains in force; its sepia ink token is skipped for this black-pigment style.
