# South France — V5, try 1

## Read of the reference

The crop is a tightly packed, sunlit hillside village: warm ochre roofs and stone walls sit among connected yellow-green and blue-green tree crowns, with darker cypress and canopy shapes behind. There is almost no open sky. The architectural cluster at center-right is the focal point; the foliage makes a broken frame around it. The paper is warm-white, and the target keeps bright roof and wall planes amid much darker tree masses.

At thumbnail size I am using four values: (1) white paper and the palest roof/wall lights; (2) a very light warm/green wash over sunlit planes; (3) the middle values of foliage and turned stone planes; and (4) the deep cypress, tree divisions, roof recesses and windows, carried chiefly by selective pen. The broad foliage shapes must stay connected, and the buildings must remain legible as a few roof planes and wall faces rather than a grid of little marks.

## Style and region choices

Use `urban-sketch` for the whole sheet, as requested. There is no sky passage. The foliage is a wash question at this cell scale: lay a pale, connected green value mass, then let only a few pen marks state major trunks, cypress edges and dark divisions. Do not paint individual leaves or spread wet foliage over the houses. The stone cluster is ink-and-wash: opening pen gives the roof ridges, eaves, outer corners and only the darkest openings; a pale warm wash describes lit roof and wall planes, and the darker target planes remain mostly paper-separated. The distant upper hillside and background trees stay softer and paler, with sparse structural lines. The foreground canopy keeps a few crisp dark branch junctions and loses its tiny leaf texture into the paper.

The crop's regions interlock too closely for broad rectangular family styles or reserves: those would introduce unsupported straight wash seams. `families: []` leaves the painter's own connected color/value segmentation intact. `reserves: []` avoids artificial boxes; the urban-sketch profile and the painter's near-paper target-light protection preserve the bright wall and roof lights throughout the wash.

## Order, moisture, and marks

1. Protect the warm-white paper and the small brightest roof/wall planes. On the dry sheet, draw the traced target-supported architectural contours in the urban-sketch ink. The opening set favors long roof edges, eaves, wall corners and a small number of dark openings; green foliage texture is excluded by the tracer. Widths are scaled down from the tracer's source estimate because the target-to-sheet registration enlarges them fourfold and the frozen recipe doubles contact width. Keep the result near a fine pen/brush-pen line, not a broad outline.
2. Lay the style's single pale flat wash over dry paper with a medium watery load (profile water 0.5). Keep it broad and transparent: light olive/yellow-green in the canopy, pale warm ochre in the sunlit stone, and muted blue-green in the distant trees. Do not tile the crowns into separate wet blobs, and do not fill every white gap. The profile's urban-sketch value floor can still form a few middle-value passages, so use only two mid layers (`_lay2`) and keep their edges inside the target's existing shapes.
3. Let the painter dry the sheet between value passages as configured. Mid values use wet-on-dry with controlled contact, following the major canopy masses and the roof/wall planes. Dark accents come last on dry paper: one connected shadow grouping where the target joins canopy, cypress and building shade; a few window/recess touches; no black fill of foliage or masonry. If a broad dark area turns into a row of detached flecks, stop adding marks.
4. On the fully dry paint, close the darker target-supported architectural lines with a denser trace than the opening. The opening look was very pale and disconnected, so the closing guide uses a lower edge threshold and modestly fuller widths to restore the roof/eave structure after the washes. The painter's closing threshold still selects the darker lines, leaving pale lit roof edges open. Ink is the urban-sketch profile's black ink; no wash is used to restore a line softened by an earlier wet passage.

The reference calls for hard, irregular edges at roof ridges and building junctions, broken edges along foliage crowns, and softer lost edges only within distant foliage. No large wet-in-wet passage is planned: the scene has no broad soft sky and the fine architectural detail must stay dry.

## Plan changes and checks

This first try has no prior critique. I selected `urban-sketch` instead of South France's default precise-watercolor profile because the task explicitly requests pen plus light wash. `region_w13_lay2_dk11` replaces four middle layers with two and lowers dark gain from 1.3 to 1.1; this should preserve the light paper share and avoid over-darkening a scene whose strongest value masses already read in the target. After look 1, I kept the opening drawing already on the paper and made only the final drawing denser and slightly fuller: the opening pen was too pale and disconnected to carry the village structure alone. The frozen recipe's unrelated settings remain in force.

Failure signs: the paper disappears under a broad uniform green fill; pale roofs and wall faces become dark outlined boxes; pen width overwhelms the small houses; foliage turns into stippled dots or a patchwork of separate blobs; or distant tree masses become as sharp and dark as the focal buildings. At the first stop I will check that the opening pen reads as architecture without enclosing every leaf. At later stops I will check the value shapes and whether the light wash leaves the center-right stone cluster distinct from the surrounding canopy.
