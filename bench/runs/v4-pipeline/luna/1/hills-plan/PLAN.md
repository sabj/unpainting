# Hills, V5 try 1 — loose ink and wash

## Read of the reference

This crop is filled edge to edge with a steep, irregular hillside. Dark blue-green tree cover breaks into clumps around bright yellow-green slopes; pale warm tracks wind through both and converge near the center. There is no sky or clean horizon. At thumbnail size I want four visual values: the lightest cream tracks and lime-lit planes; the broader yellow-green hillside; the middle olive and blue-green foliage; and the deepest cool tree pockets. The central track bend and the bright slope around it are the focal point. The dark tree masses frame that light rather than becoming a uniform dark border. The selected ink-and-wash profile resolves these into two active paint bands: a light local-color foundation and a final dark shadow band. The intervening visual values come from the fitted pigment mixtures, coverage, and paper showing through those washes, rather than separate middle-band passes.

The pale tracks are not bare-paper white in the reference. Treat their centers as pale warm painted planes and their edges as narrow negative-painted gaps during the green washes. Let the painter retain only its own near-paper target lights; I am not adding rectangular reserves because the tracks meander and a box would impose an unsupported straight edge.

## Regions and technique choices

- **Tree cover and canopy clumps, across the upper field and along the left and right sides:** The reference has connected dark crowns with broken, sharp leaf and branch marks. Start with a pale connected green wash on dry paper, let it dry, then place connected middle clumps and a final cool shadow glaze on dry paper. Use dry-brush contact for only a few foreground texture breaks; use the ink-and-wash layer for narrow dark branch and crown accents. This is a wash question for the masses and an ink-and-wash question for lines finer than a brush can hold. The smallest visible foliage marks are about 1–2 target pixels, or roughly 4–8 cells at the 1224-pixel painting width; anything finer stays in sparse ink rather than becoming individual dots. Keep the outside canopy edges broken, with crisp accents only against the lit slopes.
- **Lit hillside planes and ridges, chiefly through the middle and lower right:** The bright chartreuse and olive areas read as broad sloping value planes, not individual blades. Lay a pale local-color wash wet on dry with a flowing medium load, then dry fully before the middle values. Preserve the ridge as an irregular hard edge where it meets dark foliage and soften only the turning edge where the same green slope rolls away. Use a small amount of dry brush on the foreground-facing texture, with no parallel bands.
- **Pale winding tracks and narrow light cuts:** Preserve the target's warm cream/ochre color as a pale wash or a negative-painted gap inside the green masses, never as a white outlined road. Paint a clear hard edge on one side and let the other edge break into grass. At 1–3 target pixels wide (about 4–12 cells at output size), they are value shapes first; sparse ink may touch only the dark edge cues already present in the reference. Their warm cores stay free of closing ink.
- **Deep tree pockets and cast shadow groups:** These are the darkest blue-green and charcoal accents, not ink-filled silhouettes. Apply the last glaze after the neighboring green washes are fully dry. Let one connected shadow passage cross adjoining crown and slope shapes where the reference shadow connects; avoid outlining every leaf group. The smallest branches and dark creases are ink-and-wash accents on fully dry paper.

## Layer order and materials

1. **Opening drawing:** On dry paper, use the registered target-supported sepia-ink strokes in `opening-lines.json`: seven loose accents distributed through the upper, middle, and lower crop. Keep them subordinate to the washes; they establish a few crown breaks and track-edge turns, not a complete contour drawing. The opening nib has a small flowing ink load and tapered pressure. Ink pigment is the base recipe's sepia ink; the run uses the base `_sep8` strength and `_dwg200` width gain.
2. **Light wash:** Lay the connected pale yellow-green and warm ochre local colors wet on dry. Use the ink-and-wash profile's moderate water load (0.55) and a light, transparent pigment share. The track centers remain pale and warm. Let the executor dry the sheet between passages; before every stroke it must read the footprint and neighbouring paper state and wait or work elsewhere if they are not dry.
3. **Local-color foundation:** The first value band is split into target-fitted pigment passages, so yellow ochre, raw sienna, sap green, hansa yellow, ultramarine, and sepia build the intermediate olive and blue-green planes over the pale base. Each passage starts on dry paper with the profile's moderate water load (0.55) and medium fitted pigment share; the sheet dries between passages. Vary pressure and direction with the plane. Crisp edges belong at the track cuts and lit ridge, softer edges only on turning foliage. These local-color passages carry the middle values without adding rectangular family boundaries.
4. **Shadows and accents:** On fully dry local color, glaze the connected cool shadow side across adjacent tree and hillside shapes with the stronger fitted blue-green mixture. Keep the light ridges and tracks visible. Use the profile's moderate water load with the stronger fitted pigment share for the deepest passages; the V096 recipe's shadow gain remains unchanged. No broad atmospheric wet-in-wet passage is called for: the reference is a patchwork of firm, irregular edges rather than a soft sky or distant haze.
5. **Closing drawing:** On fully dry paint, use 24 registered, target-supported sepia-ink strokes selected across the crop. Concentrate them on the dark crown breaks and track bends, leave the pale track centers alone, and do not hatch or fill the canopy. The target's smallest traced dark lines are about 4–8 output cells wide. At 158.05 mm over 1224 cells, one cell is 0.129 mm; the base sepia nib after `_dwg200` is about 3–3.6 cells across (roughly 1.5–1.8 cells radius), so these marks retain a body and edge without becoming a broad brush line. Finer detail stays unpainted rather than being enlarged.

## What changed for this try

This is the first learning pass, so there is no prior HILLS critique to repair. I set the subject to the explicit **ink-and-wash** style because dark directional marks are part of the target's foliage language, and supplied a compact local palette of ochre, raw sienna, hansa yellow, sap green, ultramarine, and sepia for the green slopes, warm tracks, and cool tree shadows. The plan keeps the fixed V096 four middle layers, sepia-ink strength, shadow gain, and cold-press paper: the crop needs several distinct green values and a strong dark frame. `region_w13` keeps the champion's existing passage mechanics. I traced target-supported dark lines, then reduced them to seven opening and 24 closing strokes spread through the image so the finish reads as a few purposeful ink accents instead of a traced mesh.

## Checkpoints and failure signs

- **Opening:** a handful of separated, light sepia gestures; if they read as an outline network or cluster in one corner, the line plan is too dense or poorly balanced.
- **After the light wash:** the large green field is present, the warm tracks still read as paths, and the picture is not yet dark. A path painted white, a missing lime plane, or scratchy speckle means the first wash failed.
- **After the middle values:** the upper and side tree masses frame the bright center; the hillside still reads as broad connected slopes. Repeated parallel bands, isolated equal dots, or uniform blur are failures.
- **After the shadows:** deep foliage has depth and connects across the reference's dark groups without swallowing the track bends. Detached dark islands, black-looking fills, or light paths lost under a glaze are failures.
- **Closing:** crisp ink supports a few crown edges and trail turns on dry paint. Heavy tracing, ruled hatch, ink over the warm track centers, or blurred paint rescued with thick ink are failures.

The value progression must remain light to dark. I will judge the complete image at thumbnail scale first, then check that the pale tracks, central bend, broken canopy silhouette, edge character, and loose ink marks remain legible at the painting's native size. No pixel-error score is used to choose a revision.
