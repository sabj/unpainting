# Mittens V4, try 2: watercolor portrait sketch

## Reference read and value map

The crop is a warm, loose portrait: the orange tabby fills the left side and overlaps a pale human face at lower right. The sitter's charcoal blue-grey hair is the largest dark mass. The paper is near-white; the brightest cat muzzle/chest and face planes should remain open. Read the picture in four values: paper white; pale ochre and warm skin washes; mid-value tabby marks, hair turns, brows and facial planes; then the deepest pupils, nostrils, mouth corners, and a few hair valleys. The eyes and cat nose form the focal cluster.

## Region choices and order

- **Cat, left and upper crop:** The tabby stripes and whiskers are narrower than a wash mark at this scale, so use ink-and-wash for this family. Put in a pale, connected ochre/raw-sienna wash on dry paper with a broad, watery load (water about .62, light pigment); leave the white muzzle, chest, whiskers and eye lights open. Let it dry. Add mid-value markings as a few joined, target-shaped passages with moderate load (about .55 water); keep edges broken along fur and crisp at the eyes and nose. After the dry, use one restrained shadow glaze across the tabby's shadow-side cheek, ear and chest transition (about .45 water, fitted pigment). Fur detail follows growth with a few tapered sepia strokes: short around the muzzle, longer down the cheek and chest. No stippling or equal-length flicks.
- **Sitter's hair, upper right:** Ink-and-wash suits the target's connected charcoal-blue mass and fine curl edges. Lay a pale blue-grey underwash wet on dry with a broad load (about .62 water), retaining the forehead and temple lights. Dry, then build the dark curls in a connected mid-value mass with broken outer edges. Keep the darkest marks in a few curl valleys; use short directional lines only where they explain the silhouette. Do not fill the hair with ink.
- **Sitter's face, lower right:** Precise watercolor. Place a pale warm skin wash on dry paper (about .62 water), leaving the eyes dry and keeping the forehead, cheek highlight and nose ridge pale. Dry fully. Set the brow, eye socket, nose turn, cheek and mouth planes with restrained wet-on-dry shapes (about .48 water); the eyelids and mouth corners stay crisp, while one cheek edge can soften with clean water. A final light glaze may cross the shaded cheek into the jaw to unify the face. The eyes, nose and mouth remain dry focal details; no broad wet passage spills into them.
- **Background and crop edges:** The paper remains the background. No enclosing wash or rectangular vignette; let the pale figure edges disappear where the reference dissolves into white.

## Drawing and protected lights

Before washing, use the sparse target-supported opening lines for the cat's eye/nose and ear cues, the hair silhouette, and the sitter's brows, eyes, nose and mouth. Keep the opening gesture light and continuous. After all washes are dry, close with selective fine sepia lines from the target-supported crop: restore eye rims, whiskers, nostril and mouth corners, and only a few hair/fur accents. Ink is warm sepia; the hair's dark body stays blue-grey paint. Reserve the tiny eye catchlights and nose glint as near-paper target lights, along with the muzzle, chest and face lights.

## Paper state, edges, and checks

Every passage begins on fully dry paper unless its description explicitly says otherwise. Check sheen and the full brush footprint plus its neighbors before each stroke; wait if the planned dry state is not present. Broad washes use a flowing load and connected strokes. Focal linework uses a controlled, smaller load on dry paper. The value progression stays pale wash, dry, shaped mid-values, dry, then cross-plane shadow accents and closing ink. Cat shadow can cross cheek, ear and chest as one glaze; the face shadow should unify cheek and jaw rather than tile separate regions.

At native 1200 px, one cell is about 0.195 mm. A two-pixel feature in the 300 px reference spans about eight output cells. Fine whiskers and lid lines therefore use ink-and-wash rather than trying to make individual watercolor strokes thinner than their supported body. Wash edges carry the larger planes; sharp edges belong at eyes, nostrils and selected fur locks. Watch for pale disconnected islands, hard rectangular reserve outlines, muddy orange/blue mixing, blurry eyes, a flat hair cap, scratchy or stippled fur, missing catchlights, or a face that becomes uniformly outlined.

## Recipe changes from the inherited V096 base

`_lay2` replaces four middle-value layers with two, keeping the light washes simple. `_dk11` reduces the inherited 1.3 dark gain to 1.1 so the crop retains its airy paper. `_sep4` moderates the inherited sepia absorption for fine portrait lines, and `_dwg100` removes the inherited doubled drawing width so linework stays delicate. After pass 1, I reduced the five reserve boxes from about 7 target pixels to about 2; the pass-2 look still showed square holes, so I removed the geometric boxes and left the eye catchlights to the painter's near-paper target-light handling. The base cold-press paper, hue separation and other V096 behavior remain available to preserve watercolor texture and the warm cat/cool hair distinction.

## Failure signs at each checkpoint

- Opening drawing: too much tracing, missing eye landmarks, or dark lines already stronger than the target.
- Light wash: patchy/pooling color, tinted white muzzle or face highlights, or color crossing the hairline into skin.
- Mid-values: tabby becomes striped hatch, hair loses its connected shape, or face features blur into one brown plane.
- Shadow and closing: patchwork cheek shadows, hard reserve boxes, over-inked contours, lost catchlights, or details added uniformly across the crop.
