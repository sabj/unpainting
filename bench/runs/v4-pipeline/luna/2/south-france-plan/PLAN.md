# South France — V4 try 2

## Read of the reference

This is a full-bleed painted hillside, with no open sky. Three to five value groups are enough: (1) near-paper sunlit roof and wall planes, (2) pale ochre roofs and distant stone, (3) middle olive canopy and warm facades, (4) cool tree and building-side shadows, and (5) the darkest window and eave recesses plus selective pen. The focal area is the larger village group just right of centre; the large foreground tree is the secondary mass. The target's paper is white, but the pale walls and roof glints are painted lights, not empty framing.

## Style and families

Use \`urban-sketch\` throughout: the small hard architectural edges need pen, while the image's form is already carried by broad color masses. The requested style's black ink profile gives a crisp, dark line. The wash stays light and sparse so it does not bury the target's near-paper walls. The three family boxes only describe where the target's color clusters are most likely to overlap; they do not create rectangular paint edges. No family-specific style override is needed.

- **East village, right centre:** warm tile roofs, pale front walls, cooler side planes and a few dark recesses. Resolution question: wash for the planes, ink-and-wash for eaves, roof ridges and small windows. The smallest mullion/recess is about 1–2 target pixels, or 4–8 cells at 1216 px (0.230 mm per cell); it is near the four-cell threshold, so it belongs in the registered pen lines. Moves: watery, wet on dry light wash over dry paper; medium, wet on dry only for the target's darker planes; dry paper and a controlled pen load for closing accents. Roof edges are crisp but slightly broken; let some wall bases disappear into foliage. Failure: outlined polygon walls, dark windows filled into a single band, or a wash that erases the pale front planes.
- **Upper ridge houses and cypress:** small warm roof and wall shapes between dark vertical cypress and foliage. Resolution question: wash for the distant shapes; ink-and-wash only for target-supported building junctions. Keep these marks lighter and fewer than the focal village. Use a watery load on dry paper with broken edges where branches overlap. Failure: outlining every small roof or turning the cypress into evenly spaced strokes.
- **Olive canopy and foreground trees:** irregular, connected yellow-green, olive and cool-green masses; one large foreground crown anchors the bottom left, smaller crowns join behind the houses. Resolution question: wash for the value mass, not individual leaves; leaf texture is under wash resolution. Lay one watery-to-medium wash on dry paper, allowing connected contact within a crown and leaving the already light flecks exposed. Keep far foliage less contrasted. Do not wet-in-wet across building details: the reference has distinct painted edges and no sky to blend. Failure: isolated dots, repeated scallops, parallel lanes, or hard family-box edges.
- **Terraces, path and ground:** pale warm stone and muted yellow-green ground glimpses weave between tree masses. Resolution question: wash for connected planes; only the tracer-supported architectural breaks receive ink. Watery, wet on dry, with selected edges lost against foliage. Failure: a flat stripe across the hillside or a field of little stone marks.

## Lights, layers and drawing

No explicit rectangular reserve is used: the bright planes are broad parts of buildings rather than isolated glints, and the painter already protects near-paper target lights. Keep the bright roof facets and the sunlit faces of the right-center houses at near-paper or pale tint; do not tone them merely to fill an area. Pale foliage flecks stay open within the canopy. There are no eye-like catchlights or other point glints.

1. Before each mark, check measured paper state over the entire footprint and its neighbours. The opening drawing and wash begin on dry paper; the painter's drying boundary is observed before any later mark. If a selected path is not dry, wait or work elsewhere rather than assume a timer made it dry.
2. **Opening pen:** sparse target-traced building edges, roof ridges/eaves, a few corners and dark openings. Use the black-ink profile on dry paper with a light-to-medium controlled load; let width and pressure follow the source line. No tree hatching and no invented straight lines. Check the opening stage for recognizable house planes and surviving light gaps. Failure: every leaf or stone fleck becomes an outline, or a roof frame closes a light opening.
3. **Light wash:** one light, watery-to-medium pass over the target's connected value shapes. Keep foliage green and variegated, walls pale and roofs warm; preserve the target's value hierarchy rather than filling each family box. Dry fully between any nested value steps. Failure: broad color has become a uniform green-brown sheet, or the light roofs/facades have been lost.
4. **Mid values and shade:** two restrained mid-layer steps from \`_lay2\`, on dry paper. Shape roof planes and cool sides; keep building recesses small. Use a single connected shadow treatment where a target shadow crosses roof, wall and foliage, not separate outlined fills. This scene has no large cast shadow needing a broad cross-region glaze; local value shapes follow the reference. Failure: separate rectangular patches, dark contour rims, or depth that is too weak to separate roofs from walls.
5. **Closing ink:** after dry-down, use only target-supported dark architectural lines, weighted toward the right-center houses. Restore a few eaves, roof intersections and openings; leave light roof facets and most foliage unlined. Ink clarifies the plane construction but never substitutes for painted dark masses. Failure: repeated opening strokes make heavy black borders, or added pen obscures the wash's soft/lost edges.

The expected value progression is light wash, dry, two modest mid/shadow steps, dry, then selective dark accents. No wet-in-wet break is requested because this crop has no broad soft sky or distant atmospheric wash.

## Recipe choices and failure checks

\`_lay2\` replaces four mid layers with two and reduces patchwork risk. \`_dk11\` lowers the base shadow gain so tree and stone shadows keep color instead of becoming black blocks. \`_dwg140\` replaces the doubled base drawing width with a width closer to the traced source edges; the urban-sketch ink share still carries the structural drawing. The base \`_ink4\` keeps the last pen pass on the dedicated \`closingink4\` file. The base sepia token yields to the urban-sketch black-ink profile.

At each look, judge the thumbnail values first, then lights, silhouette/roof landmarks, edge variety and mark character. Stop for revision at the earliest visible failure: opening lines too heavy before the wash; one flat or lost light mass after the wash; dark rectangular boundaries after mid values; or uniform black contours at closing. Judge the actual 1216-pixel sheet, not an enlarged inspection copy.
