# White Ships (Sargent) — V4 try 2

## Reference read and value map

The 300 × 244 reference is a loose, cropped harbor plate on pale paper. At thumbnail size I read four value groups: (1) near-paper cream and blue-white boat planes, paper glints and gaps; (2) pale blue-gray hull and upper-boat washes; (3) middle-value turquoise water, warm rigging and hull turns; (4) the dark connected keel and a few small overlaps and fittings. The focal shape is the broad curved foreground hull, especially its transition into the deep lower shadow. Keep the upper boat edges open and broken so the boats do not become an outlined cutout.

## Region techniques and order

Use precise watercolor throughout, with no opening or closing drawing. The line files have `medium: none` and no paths: mast, rigging and boat structure must be painted, not rescued with ink. The production is 1200 × 976 on a 280 mm sheet; the base cell scale is about 0.233 mm per output pixel. The water glints are below reliable brush-detail scale, so the two tiny explicit reserves stay bare paper; larger white-blue hull sheens are pale painted planes and the planner's near-paper lights, not empty cutouts.

- **Main hull** (roughly x .14–.72, y .03–.99): wash question, not individual scumbles. Lay a broad, transparent cool blue-gray light body wet on dry paper, allowing the irregular fitted edges to stay soft only where the hull dissolves into water. Let it dry fully. On dry paper place the curved middle-value turn and lower keel mass in connected, tapered shapes; retain pale streaks within the body. Add the deepest blue-violet-gray only at the bottom overlap, as paint rather than line. Water is tea-to-coffee for the broad wash and coffee in the last accents; do not wet neighboring pale upper-boat details while this hull passage is open.
- **Pale upper boats and deck** (roughly x .48–1, y 0–.72): wash question. Keep the light cream and cool-white planes largely paper or a very pale local tint. On dry paper shape the pale blue shadows and narrow separations with controlled coffee loads; lose one edge into the adjacent wash and keep the other crisp where a boat edge turns. The small dark fittings are final paint accents, not a repeated outline.
- **Left rigging and boat edge** (roughly x 0–.38, y 0–.77): below four cells for some thin lines, so treat this as a few painted tapered marks rather than trying to draw every cord. Warm, light ochre-brown on the initial plane, dry before a smaller sienna-brown accent. Keep the strokes interrupted and vary their width; no uniform hatch.
- **Right mast** (roughly x .74–.94, y 0–.96): narrow painted structure. Establish its pale warm body with a light wash, dry, then add a few broken sienna-brown shadow segments wet on dry. Preserve small gaps where pale hull or sky shows through. No ruled full-height dark contour.
- **Harbor water** (roughly y .67–1): the bottom strip is a wash question. Begin with a connected pale turquoise/blue-green underwash, slightly stronger toward the lower edge but with an irregular horizon. Keep hull reflections attached to the hull shadow. Once dry, use broken mid-value turquoise and warm reflection marks; short glints remain mostly painted pale values, with only two small bare-paper sparks reserved. Finish with a few short, pointed dark accents. Avoid parallel bands and evenly spaced ripples.

## Passage sequence and paper state

1. Start on dry paper. Before each brush mark, check measured wetness and pigment/water load over the full intended footprint and its neighboring cells. The first large light passages need connected contact and an unbroken wet edge; use a broad brush with a watery, light tea load. If the paper is not evenly receptive, wait or prepare the passage deliberately; do not force the wash onto a patchy damp field.
2. Lay the pale hull, upper-boat and water bodies as large light washes. Keep the water and hull touching only along the reference's actual reflected shadow; do not let wet color flood the near-paper boat planes. The small reserves remain untouched.
3. Let the sheet reach measured dry state. Work the hull turn, boat separations, water variation and mast on dry paper with medium coffee loads. The broad hull shape and water stay connected; small structural shapes get a single clear hard edge and a softer or lost opposite edge.
4. Dry fully again. Add one restrained cross-boundary shadow glaze where the hull's lower shadow enters its reflection, so the boat sits in the water rather than reading as separate region fills. Use a controlled milk-to-coffee pigment load on dry paper. Stop if the pale hull sheen starts to close or the water becomes a flat stripe.
5. On dry paper add only the deepest keel, overlap and fitting accents. These are small paint touches; no ink or graphite follows. Keep warm and cool darks distinct instead of mixing the whole shadow to black.

## Recipe and stage intent

`region_w13_lay2_dk11` keeps the native W13 passage brush and reduces the base's four mid layers to two, limiting repeated glazing on this already busy, loose plate. Dark gain 1.1 instead of 1.3 should keep the keel and fittings deep without turning the blue-gray washes into black. Keep the base cold-press texture and the run's target-fitted wide palette: cerulean blue, quinacridone rose, hansa yellow and the recipe-added granulating cerulean tube. The blue carries the hull and water; rose and yellow support warm rigging, rust and reflection mixtures; their complements and the deepest cool mixture provide painted dark fittings. A proposed French ultramarine/earth palette was refused by the painter because pigments cannot change after a run starts, so the original fitted set remains. The checkpoints should progress from open paper to pale connected boat/water shapes, then legible middle-value hull and mast, and finally a small dark keel and reflection accents.

## Failure signs

At the first light checkpoint, look for a coherent curved hull and pale upper-boat shapes; broad holes, hard rectangular wash edges or water bleeding across hull lights mean the earliest wash failed. At mid-value stages, check that the pale hull sheen survives, the right mast stays narrow and broken, and the water remains a low, mottled band. At the finish, reject an outlined boat, a uniformly dark hull, a horizontal blue stripe, scratchy isolated dabs, lost paper sparks, or dark accents repeated along every edge. The intended looseness comes from a few connected transparent masses and selective broken edges, not from random gaps.
