## Regions, resolution and technique

Native resolution is fixed: one cell is 0.19509 mm; one reference pixel becomes four cells, 0.78036 mm. A four-cell painted body is about 0.78 mm. Broad planes comfortably exceed this. Fine hair, whiskers and tapered lid edges can be 0.3–1 reference px, 1.2–4 native cells; these are an ink-and-wash question. Keep the reference geometry and let the fine pen explain selected lines. Do not paint individual fur strands or raise density through an export resize.

## Lights, drawing, glazes and execution order

1. Protect four small eye glints, each 1.5 × 1.5 reference px (1.17 mm), at the measured reference positions. Cat glints are bare paper; the person's smaller reflected glints are deliberately kept light at this scale. The painter also protects target near-paper lights. White chest and muzzle are bare paper; pale iris/sclera, skin and cheek sheen are painted light planes; thin white whisker gaps rely on the target-fitted negative spaces. No planned lifting or white overlay.
2. Opening drawing: 19 selected paths, not a full trace. Keep the traced cat and human eye geometry, brow, muzzle and smile structure, a few contact lines and three sparse ear contours. Simplify traced polylines by at most 0.45 reference px without moving retained points. Use declared sepia ink at pressure 0.25; allow the executor to drop paths that cross protected lights. Inspect the actual opening stage for both faces before the first wash.
3. Broad pale fitted colour washes. Connect each local plane with the painter's original fitted paths and finite loads. Dry base is measured before each stroke over its footprint and neighbours; same-passage overlap is allowed while the wash is open. Never infer a reading from elapsed time. Work elsewhere or let the fixed dry gate wait when a dry region is not ready.
4. Dry completely; two middle-value layers build the ochre markings, face planes and hair. Details remain on dry paper with controlled load. Do not let a large wet passage spill into an eye. The fixed painter checks actual paper state and records the strokes.
5. Dry; shade last. Preserve connected shadow support across cat head into neck/flank, the cat/portrait contact, and portrait socket into cheek/lower face. These use the original cross-region fitted shadow shapes rather than separate box fills. Actual connected-support/cross-region decisions are checked in the compiled value plan. Keep shadow water at the original controlled 0.45. No dark-first exception.
6. Fully dry; inspect before closing. Closing drawing has 35 selected candidate paths: upper lids, small pupil/nostril and mouth accents, a handful of directional cat lines and contact edges. The painter selects only supported dark edges, never protected lights. Sepia ink is declared explicitly, pressure capped at 0.42, edge gate 0.18. Never add heavy ink to rescue a blurred wash. Inspect and finish.

## Changes from V096 and their reasons

`region_w13_lay2_dk11_cg10_sep3_dwg139`: retain the established target-fitted W13 brush paths. Two middle layers replace four so the requested light sketch avoids needless repetition. Dark gain 1.1 replaces 1.3 to keep high-key skin and coat transparent while preserving the darkest hair and pupils. Cool gain 1.0 replaces 1.3 to avoid blue cast on the warm cat/face. Sepia scale 3 replaces 8 and drawing width gain 1.39 replaces 2 for fine lines; the material model ultimately sets the physical nib. Opening pressure is 0.25; closing is 0.42 instead of the base's 1.0, and supported-edge threshold is 0.18 instead of 0. The explicit palette is ochre, raw sienna, sienna, ultramarine and sepia: warm local washes with a complementary neutral dark, avoiding unnecessary cyan/green in the hair. All settings must be read by the painter.

## Checkpoints and acceptance

Run `bench/check.sh` before physics; require ADMITTED, coherent fitted shapes, no unsupported straight seams, no bands or box edges, and a light-to-dark ladder. The allowed fast proof is the actual opening/first-light checkpoint in this run; do not launch a separate physics render.

Opening should be sparse but contain cat and human facial landmarks. First light stage should establish pale warm masses with large paper areas and all glints intact; it is not expected to look finished. Middle stage should connect tabby bands, readable sockets and a developing hair mass without hard contour rings. Before closing, the wash must already carry likeness and the value groups; the pen adds only a few focal accents.

Judge in the skill's order: thumbnail value shapes; lights and their positions; chosen hard/soft/lost edges at native scale; eye/nose/mouth/ear landmarks; purposeful tapered marks; visible light-to-dark progression; untouched paper and concentrated focal detail. Reject lost catchlights, swollen irises, under-eye ink creases, global blur, noisy stipple, disconnected wash islands, uniform stripes, rectangular seams, over-dark skin, or ink-filled hair. Material/execution success and artistic quality are separate conclusions.
