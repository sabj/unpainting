# Trumbull, V4 try 3 — painting plan

Reference: `bench/targets/trumbull-crop.png`, 300 × 263 target pixels. Painting: 1200 × 1052 native pixels, 280 × 245.47 mm, seed 0. Requested style: precise architectural watercolor. Independent plan; no other tries or scores consulted.

The focal point is the warm central gable and its paired pointed arches. The tallest left chimney, roof pitch, vertical front corner and right chimney establish the building's proportions. The front is sunlit golden stone, the receding left wall is grey ochre, and the openings are cool blue-black. Yellow-green foliage overlaps the front without competing with the arches. The sky is cool, pale and irregularly clouded, not a hard blue rectangle.

## Value thumbnail and protected lights

Five value groups, built light to dark: (1) paper/cloud lights and tiny cornice glints; (2) pale sky and warm sunlit front; (3) ochre side walls, slate roof and canopy; (4) connected cool-brown chimney, eave and wall shadows; (5) small window and arch recesses, banner and signal accents. The sky remains lighter than the major architectural shadow. The deepest paint is confined to openings and a few lower accents.

Bare paper: the executor's target-relative cloud and stone lights, plus two small measured reserves at the bright annex roof edge and central arch rim. Pale painted planes: the central facade, larger arch surrounds, window sills, chimney's lit side and pavement. Negative-painted gaps: the reference's irregular wall/sky holes in the tree and narrow separations between openings. Window mullions are pale local stone, not broad white masks. No rectangular facade reserve or continuous white border. No planned lift is needed: preserve the lights before the darks. A reserved rim should read cream beside the warm wash, not as an enlarged white patch.

## Resolution and mark choice

Native cell width is 0.2333 mm. One target pixel is four native cells and 0.9333 mm. The four-cell wash threshold is about 0.93 mm. Broad wall, roof and sky features range from 8–60 mm (34–257 cells): the wash question. Most dark window bodies are about 3–8 mm wide (13–34 cells), enough for a fitted flowing brush body. Their narrow mullions and roof seams are about 0.5–1 target pixel, or 0.47–0.93 mm (2–4 cells): the ink-and-wash question. Do not paint these as tiny wash fragments. Fine foliage is below the threshold; it belongs to connected canopy groups and a few pointed edges, not individual dots.

## Region techniques, loads, edges and order

All regions use precise-watercolor so no style box cuts a facade or creates a seam. Per-region technique decisions below follow the reference. After the first attempt's moisture-gate refusal, every sky-family contact starts on a measured dry base: a flowing transparent wet-on-dry wash whose own overlapping strokes merge. The family also contains cool roof/glass colors, so it must not require a moist whole footprint. Later sky color is one graded blue middle layer (`sk1`), with no separately quantiled dark sky patch. Keep the gable and roof silhouettes sharp; the cloud lights and pigment variation supply the softer internal sky character.

Stone texture is sparse pale glazing, not a dry-brush first wash: irregular 4 mm block hints over dried masonry, 40% coverage at 14% of local demand. Most stone tone comes from the broad connected planes. Do not introduce a course grid or uniform scratches. Cold-press tooth and pigment granulation provide the remaining surface character.

## Cross-boundary shadow glazes

After the middle colors are measured dry, keep the fitted connected shadow support independent of family ownership. One cool-brown glaze sequence crosses the left chimney, receding wall, roof/eaves and window surrounds. Another follows the central/right turning edge through the arches, sill and lower wall behind the tree. The annex eave shadow crosses wall and window tops. The grounding glaze connects lower masonry and street contact. Pigments are French ultramarine plus raw sienna with the base violet modulation; the palette deliberately has no burnt sienna, so `shs` retains a warm component. Window recesses keep their own fitted dark mix, in two dry layers; they are paint masses, never ink fills. Water 0.45, watery-to-medium pigment, flowing load. Keep the base darkgain 1.3 to establish dark openings without darkening sunlit planes.

1. Opening stop: sparse neutral grey structure on light paper; focal landmarks present without a finished line-art sheet.
2. Light passes complete: large pale connected washes; paper and cloud lights intact; structure begins to read but dark openings are intentionally unfinished.
3. First two mid layers: warm facade and cooler side planes separate; sky stays pale with cloud variation; no mosaic, parallel stripes or patchy coverage.

6. Finished: selective fine sepia restores the central arches, mullions and roof/chimney junctions; no blanket outline or dark canopy.
