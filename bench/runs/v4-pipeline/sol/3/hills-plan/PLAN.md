Reference: `hills-crop.png`, 306 × 242 source pixels; final native sheet 1224 × 968, 158.048 × 124.992 mm. Independent V4 try 3, 2026-10-09. Recipe: `region_w13_noslane_dk11_cg10_sep4_dwg100_opp30`, layered on frozen V096.

## Value thumbnail and focal point

Read four values: warm cream path and tiny paper lights; luminous yellow-green slopes; middle olive/blue-grey crowns; deep cool woodland underneath crowns and around branching trunks. The focal point is the spreading tree beside the lower path bend, around source (103, 145). The pale path connects the foreground meadow to the upper-left slope. Its shape must remain readable through every stage. There is no sky in this crop.

## Style and palette

All regions use ink-and-wash: large transparent local-color foundations on dry paper, complete drying, then connected shadow glazes, complete drying, then selective sepia structure. The six paints are yellow ochre, hansa yellow, French ultramarine, cerulean blue, sepia, and sap green. Ink uses the base's explicit sepia absorption profile, scaled to 4 rather than 8; width gain 1 rather than 2, opening pressure .30. Sparse opening structure is 22 reviewed gestures; closing structure is 38 gestures, predominantly foreground trunks and branches. Paint carries the dark masses. Ink does not fill crowns or outline every patch.

The ink-and-wash profile distributes dark supports between a 42% foundation and a 58% shadow portion. Outside shadow supports it joins the color into the foundation. This means sunny meadow planes reach their light local color early, while woodland deepens later. That is a deliberate departure from making every region pale at the same rate: the reference has finished-looking bright slopes adjacent to layered dark groves. No dark-first reorder. The inherited middle-layer setting does not demand extra painted layers in this two-wash profile.

## Protected lights and cross-boundary glazes

The near-paper path flecks and the tiny light breaks on slopes and crown tops are bare-paper lights selected by the painter's fitted target-light mask before the first wash. The whole path is a pale painted plane, not a rectangular reserve. There are no explicit rectangles: they would cut unsupported straight edges through irregular terrain. The yellow slopes are painted lights, not bare white. Pale cream path tones come from the ochre foundation; no opaque white is added later.

The shared dry shadow support crosses adjacent woodland color families, crowns, trunks and shaded ground. The glazes must unify the grove rather than fill each tree as a separate patch. At the lower bend, shade connects tree undersides with the darker meadow edge; the central path light stays protected. Across the central hillside, cool crown shadows bridge olive and blue-grey undercolors. A second unrelated complementary glaze is unnecessary in this loose crop; different pigments within the single planned shadow stage build the mixed dark physically.

## Drawing audit and progression checkpoints

Opening paths contain the main tree's rising trunk and forks, a few left/right foreground trunks, short broken path edges, and ten simplified fitted woodland gestures. Lines use source-coordinate registration (306 × 242), tapered per-point widths, and lighter opening weights. The closing set selects thirty traced thin dark spans, simplifies tiny tracing zigzags, and adds eight connected reference-fitted trunk gestures. No ruled hatch, no repeated leaf discs, no ink in paper lights. Check the actual opening and pigment log before the first wash; closing line admission may intentionally omit low-contrast gestures.

1. Opening stop: paper dominates; sparse confident structure fixes the bend and a few trunks. It must not resemble complete traced lineart.
2. Foundation complete: warm path and yellow-green slopes are already readable; woodland is transparent and lighter than its final shadow. Broad masses, not isolated stamps.
3. Shadow stage: woods deepen across crown boundaries while bright slope and path survive. There must be several values inside each dark grove, not one black slab.
4. Before closing: judge the painting's value shapes and edge roles first. Do not repair blurred paint by making ink heavy.
5. Finish: selective dry sepia near the bend and foreground trees, lighter and quieter distance. Native paper tooth supplies texture; stop after the completed painting.

## Choices relative to V096 and acceptance

Remove sky lanes because cool woods are irregular tree masses, not a horizontal sky. Reduce dark gain from 1.3 to 1.1 and cool gain from 1.3 to 1.0 so the forest stays blue-grey with visible middle values. Reduce ink strength, width and opening pressure because this reference calls for a loose light drawing. Add an explicit landscape palette because Y1 palette tokens do not apply to Hills. Keep target-fitted shape geometry and physical cold-press paper.

Run `bash bench/check.sh` and require ADMITTED before `bash bench/paint.sh start`. No local physics render. The admission compile is the permitted fast planning proof; inspect actual first passages through the required cloud painter rather than invoke an extra crop render.

Accept changes only against the watercolor guide's final judging list: correct thumbnail value shapes; lights present and toned; intended hard/soft/lost edges; accurate path bend and tree placement; purposeful tapered marks without stipple, stamps, scratches, noise or tiled patches; credible light-to-dark progress; and detail concentrated at the focal point. Failure signs are a lost cream path, fluorescent flat meadow, solid black woodland, horizontal lanes in crowns, rectangular seams, cloudy blurred focal trunks, heavy outlines, or equal little leaf stamps.
