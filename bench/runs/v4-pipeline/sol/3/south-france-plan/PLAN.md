# South France — urban sketch, black pen and light wash

Reference: the fixed 304 × 249 crop, on a 280 × 229.34 mm sheet at 1216 × 996 native cells. This is an independent V4 plan. The focal point is the terracotta house cluster on the right; the large branching tree anchors the foreground. Narrow cypresses link the houses to the high terraces. There is no open sky: the cool top-right shape is distant land.

## Value thumbnail and style balance

Five values: paper flecks and very pale limestone/path lights; pale ochre walls and yellow-green crown tops; middle olive/blue-grey groves and terracotta roofs; connected cool canopy undersides and shaded house faces; a few deepest cypresses, branch junctions and window recesses. Pen carries structural lines; dark foliage bodies stay paint. Pale first washes precede dried shadow glazes. The executor's urban-sketch value floor provides a foundation plus one later shadow passage rather than leaving the tree masses nearly blank. Overall style gain is 0.80; shadow and cool gains are 1.0. Keep local colour legible and paper luminous. No added stone texture or individual painted leaves.

## Opening drawing and protected lights

Opening black ink: reviewed connected gestures for the focal roofs, eaves, wall corners, distant roof junctions, terrace doorway, the foreground tree's tapering trunk and forks, and a few cypress sides. Draw on measured fully dry paper at restrained pressure 0.45. No hatching and no ink fill. The actual opening checkpoint must contain the right-hand roof group and the foreground tree; short distant window marks are deliberately deferred to the closing drawing. The native black-ink material sets the physical nib (roughly 0.2 mm at opening pressure), rather than enlarging widths from the tiny source image.

Bare-paper reserves are three small measured limestone/path glints: the left doorway jamb, the path beside the main house group, and the low foreground path. They are only 1.5–2 mm across and cannot become rectangular holes across a facade. Other paths, lit walls and crown lights are pale painted planes, not large masked whites. Natural gaps between canopy masses remain negative-painted light. Near-paper target lights are also protected by the painter. There are no eyes or catchlights in this reference. Do not add a broad paper vignette across this tightly cropped landscape.

## Per-region techniques and order

All families use urban-sketch without rectangular style overrides. Their boxes label the target's own connected colour/value segmentation; they are not fill rectangles. Every first mark starts on measured dry paper, with only same-passage wet overlap permitted. Before each stroke the executor checks its footprint and neighbours; wait/dry or work elsewhere if the required state is absent. Never claim an unmeasured reading.

1. **Upper-left terraces.** Wash question. Limestone planes are 10–35 mm wide (43–152 cells); the thin doorway jamb is an ink question, about 1–2 source px = 4–8 native cells. First, watery pale ochre/raw-sienna wash on dry paper, broad belly contact, connected across each real wall plane. Dry. Give the doorway and retaining wall their darker accents; retain hard structural edges and lose some low-contrast ground edges. Do not dry-brush the first facade wash.
2. **Upper village.** Ink-and-wash question. Roof planes are 8–15 source px (32–60 cells); distant windows are 1–2 source px (4–8 cells). Thin ochre wall and sienna roof washes on dry paper, watery pigment, controlled water 0.5, broad contact for planes. Dry before the connected cool shadow. Pen retains just roof/corner junctions; distant detail stays sparse and lighter than the focal houses.
3. **Distant top-right hillside.** Wash question. Broad blue-grey shapes, 12–25 source px (48–100 cells), not individual vegetation. Watery pale cerulean/ultramarine/ochre wash on dry paper, 0.5 water, low pigment load. Keep contrast low and edges less emphatic; no atmospheric-wet override because there is no large continuous sky gradient in this crop. No additional ink there.
4. **Cypresses.** Wash body plus ink silhouette. Width 3–8 source px = 12–32 cells, 2.8–7.4 mm, comfortably wider than a four-cell wash body; tip and slender trunk are ink questions. A pale connected green foundation, fully dry, then medium-strength sepia/ultramarine/sap-green shadow glaze within the tapered body. Paper must be dry, water 0.5, controlled load. Keep pointed tops and some crisp outer edges; never make the whole mass an ink fill.
5. **Middle olive groves.** Wash question. Canopy masses span 8–25 source px = 32–100 cells; individual leaves below 1 source px are not painted separately. Watery yellow-olive and blue-grey foundation masses on dry paper; connected strokes merge inside a passage. Dry. Medium pigment and controlled water 0.5 form one connected glaze per cool underside across neighbouring canopy groups and ground shadows. Lit tops stay light; some turning edges are lost, focal edges remain firm. No equal leaf disks, stipple or scratching.
6. **Focal house group.** Plane washes plus structural ink. Wall/roof planes are 15–40 source px = 60–160 cells; eaves/mullions are 1–2 source px = 4–8 cells (0.9–1.8 mm). Watery pale ochre/raw-sienna walls and sienna roofs on dry paper, broad contact; preserve lit facing walls. Full dry. A medium cool-neutral glaze crosses shaded wall, adjacent eave and doorstep, rather than shading each labelled family separately. Dry. Tiny window recesses are dark paint accents where the fitted shape is at least four cells; thin frames and junctions are black ink. Crisp roof geometry and a few lost light-wall edges keep the focus.
7. **Foreground tree.** Wash crown; ink branches. Crown groups are 12–35 source px = 48–140 cells. Twig widths are 0.3–1 source px = 1.2–4 cells, so ink carries those. Watery yellow-green crown foundation on dry paper, 0.5 water, no speckled dry-brush texture. Dry. One connected olive shadow glaze follows the lower/interior crown and overlaps the middle-grove shadow. Opening trunk and forks show through; closing pen strengthens only branch junctions, changing pressure toward tips. Leave bright crown patches and path holes open.
8. **Lower gardens and paths.** Wash/negative-painting question. Paths are 5–12 source px (20–48 cells), shrubs 8–20 source px (32–80 cells), little trunks sub-four-cell ink. Pale warm ground wash with negative gaps around connected bushes. Dry before the common cool shadow glaze across bush undersides, terrace wall bases and nearby ground. Watery to medium pigment, controlled 0.5 water. Keep a broken irregular edge and broad light paths; only two small branching trees receive pen.

The four-cell body threshold here is 0.92 mm. Typical small ink contact is about 0.2–0.3 mm (0.9–1.3 cells). The fixed fitted brush chooses contact from each plane's support width; compile instructions record estimated support widths. A broad plane is a wash even if its edge has paper texture. Branches, eaves and thin window structure are never enlarged into brush blobs to meet the threshold.

## Glazes, accents and closing pen

Cross-boundary glazes: cool canopy shade runs across neighbouring middle groves into cypress bases and ground; focal house shade crosses wall/eave/doorstep; foreground crown shade connects to nearby garden shadow. Target-fitted shadow support ignores family ownership. Each goes on fully dry paint with medium pigment and 0.5 water, retaining lit planes. No wet-in-wet dark charge is planned: the crowded architecture and separate leaves call for controlled dry placement. This is a deliberate dry interpretation of the reference's softly connected canopy.

Closing drawing is a separate selective file: focal architecture, foreground branch junctions, a few little olive trunks, and small window recesses/frames. Do not repeat every distant contour. Draw only on dry paint at pressure capped at 0.70, in the declared non-rewetting **black ink** material. Its physical pigment must be present in the executed log. The executor's value/contrast selector may omit low-contrast lines. If structure is absent at the opening stop, revise before washing; closing ink cannot repair blurry paint.

## Checkpoint intents and acceptance

Before GPU work, run `bench/check.sh` and inspect the value-plan report for dry first washes, pale-to-dark sequence, correct family cues, and no unsupported straight seams. The first physical opening and first wash serve as the fixed painter's representative proof; do not run an independent physics test.

Opening: recognizable roof cluster and foreground branching skeleton, sparse distant pen, no filled dark bodies. Foundation: pale connected hillside, warm walls and roofs distinguishable, light paths and reserved flecks survive. Shadow stage: coherent depth under trees and on house side planes, with dry focal edges. Before closing: painting reads at thumbnail scale; pen should only clarify junctions. Final: correct roofline proportions, warm light faces against cool shaded faces, foreground branches legible through light yellow-green washes.

Accept changes by the watercolor skill's ordered criteria: value shapes, correctly placed/toned lights, hard/soft/lost edge roles, roof/window/tree landmarks, purposeful tapered marks without stamps/scratches/tiled patches, then human light-to-dark progression. Failure signs: a nearly blank forest, dark first fills, blue rectangles on lit walls, missing main roof junctions, cypresses as thick ink outlines, scratchy facades, leaf confetti, broad blur, or a repeated all-over closing trace. If a failure appears, fix the earliest still-unpainted passage at a pause and record the reason here. Once the painting is finished, stop.
