# Trumbull V5 try 1 — precise architectural watercolor

## Reference read and value plan

The crop is already a watercolor plate on pale paper. The focal point is the central sunlit gable and its two tall arches, held between the taller left tower and the receding right wing. The tree and street furniture overlap the lower-right architecture and should stay subordinate.

Use five values: (1) bare paper in near-paper target cloud flecks and other target lights; (2) pale blue sky and transparent warm stone foundations; (3) ochre wall planes, blue-grey roof planes, and the tree's light green mass; (4) the turned facade planes, roof shadows, tree underside, and pavement; (5) window recesses, the sign and signal, and the deepest arch interiors. Build these in order, with the warm stone remaining luminous. The stone's strongest dark/light separation belongs around the gable arches and deep openings.

## Regions and technique

- **Sky:** one connected pale blue wash, wet-on-dry on dry paper, with an irregular edge that follows the broken, craggy cloud forms. The target has small cloud fragments rather than a broad soft cloud bank, so retain near-paper centers through the painter's target-light mask and let the blue wash's edge stay broken; do not paint a soft wash over architectural details. Keep the roof silhouette clean and avoid separate tiled blue patches or a hard horizontal sky boundary. The sky's blue-grey cloud turns stay light enough for the roofs to remain the focus.
- **Main stone (gable and tower):** broad, watery ochre and raw-sienna foundation on dry paper, followed after drying by the V096 middle-value washes. At this scale, the large facade reads as value planes; the arched openings, roof edges, and tower corners carry the structure. Soften only a turning edge on a sunlit plane, leaving the gable outline and window edges firm.
- **Right wing and side planes:** transparent pale foundation followed by dry mid-value planes. Keep these cooler and a step darker than the front-facing gable so the right side recedes. Preserve the target's irregular roof silhouette and avoid rectangular value blocks at the family bounds; these bounds only describe target color groups.
- **Tree:** treat the crown as an irregular connected green mass with small paper gaps, then let the existing value layers darken its lower and right side. Do not paint individual leaf disks or send wet green over the dry window detail. The tree edge is broken against the facade, with a few crisp leaf clumps at the silhouette.
- **Street and foreground:** the signpost, signal heads, and very dark street marks are compact accents on dry paper. Keep their silhouettes legible and use the fitted blue sign shapes as paint; the stone paving remains a few connected planes with sparse irregular texture.
- **Windows, arches, and fine stone lines:** wash question for the wall planes; ink-and-wash for narrow mullions and the finest drawn contours. At 1200 px, one source pixel is about four native cells (0.93 mm on this sheet). Those four-cell mullions sit at the detail threshold, so keep them in the registered pen drawing rather than trying to paint a smaller line with a wash. The fine line's pen contact is about 0.5–1.9 native cells wide across its pressure range, leaving roughly 2–8 feature widths across a four-cell mullion. Broader arch and facade features exceed this and are painted as planes.

## Order, moisture, and ink

1. **Opening drawing:** the 63 registered target traces in `opening-lines.json` establish the gable and tower silhouettes, roof junctions, arches, window grids, signpost, and signal. They are sparse sepia ink on dry paper. The source lines are already uneven; keep their taper and variable pressure rather than adding a second outline from the tonal edge.
2. **Light washes:** sky first as a single connected wet-on-dry passage, then pale local-color foundations on dry paper for the stone, roofs, foliage, and ground. The painter retains near-paper cloud and stone lights from the target itself; there are no rectangular cloud reserves. Sunlit stone is a pale painted plane, not a white reserve.
3. **Middle values:** after the light layers dry, follow the V096 four-step middle ladder on dry paper. Broad facade washes carry the stone; roof planes, shadow-side masonry, and the tree mass get firmer edges where they define a turn. Use the source's broken warm-stone texture only as sparse, irregular surface variation, never as parallel courses or evenly spaced marks.
4. **Shadows and recesses:** let each pass dry fully. The two final connected shadow glazes share the target's shadow support across facade, roof, tree, and ground boundaries, so the building does not become a patchwork of region fills. Keep warm/cool shadow mixtures transparent over the ochre. Window and arch recesses deepen last as paint accents.
5. **Closing drawing:** the 178 target-supported paths in `closing-lines.json` restore only the dark architectural lines, mullions, arch edges, signal, and street details that remain visible in the source. Use the recipe's sepia ink on fully dry paint. Do not outline every stone or use ink to repair a soft wash.

Before each stroke, the executor must check the measured paper state across the full footprint and its neighbours. The sky and facade washes start on dry paper; middle, shadow, and pen work also wait for dry paper. Water and pigment load follow the passage: watery pale foundations, medium fitted middle washes, and a controlled medium-to-thick recess accent. If the footprint is not in that state, wait or work elsewhere.

## V096 choices and this try's changes

The target is the Y1/Trumbull plate the fixed V096 recipe was tuned on, and its base palette and dark window treatment suit this reference. Keep the recipe's four middle-value steps, sepia ink, cold-press paper, warm/cool shadow split, and irregular stone hints unchanged. This try adds target-specific descriptive region cues and separate sparse opening and fuller closing traces. The first run reached a measured whole-footprint paper-state refusal on the sky's wet-in-wet pass (0.779 moist coverage against the executor's load limit); preceding pigment passes had dried. The sky move is now wet-on-dry. At pass 16, the upper-right rectangular reserve also read as a box against the irregular cloud shapes, so the explicit reserves are removed; the painter's source-shaped near-paper light mask keeps the cloud flecks instead. No pigment or other painter setting changed.

## Stage intents and failure signs

- After the opening drawing: the building should read from its roofline, tower, arches, and window grid, while the street lines remain small. If the opening looks like dense tracing, dark texture has entered the drawing.
- After the light washes: pale sky and warm stone should connect over large planes while retaining near-paper cloud lights, lit stone, and a clear roof silhouette. Sky bands, rectangular cutoffs, scratchy facade fills, or a blue wash over the windows indicate a failed first passage.
- After the middle ladder: the gable should turn forward from the darker right wing; the tower and stone blocks should still read as one building. Repeated parallel courses, disconnected color islands, or weak arch and window landmarks indicate failure.
- After the shadow glazes: one coherent shadow side should cross the architecture and tree without a visible seam at family descriptions. Muddy grey stone, lost lit faces, or dark rims at wet edges indicate a failed glaze.
- At finish: the central arches, roofline, window grid, green crown, and dark street furniture should be recognizable at thumbnail size. Heavy uniform ink, missing deep recesses, vanished cloud lights, or equally dark front and side walls are the main defects to watch.
