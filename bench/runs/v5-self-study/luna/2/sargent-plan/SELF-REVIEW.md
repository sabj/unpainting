# Try 1 self-review

I judged the previous sheet against the target before using the external critique. The central sail and lower hull are recognizable, the warm spars survive, and the dark lower contact gives the vessel some weight. The large pale opening at upper right is also present. The main failure is that the blue-grey masses broke into too many small, pale-edged islands. The hull curve loses continuity, the turquoise water does not read as one quiet field, and several white holes look like accidental geometric reserves. The sheet feels over-segmented and washed out next to the target.

The trouble starts in the early wash: too much of the whole composition appears as separated low-contrast patches instead of a few broad connected planes. Later shadow and correction marks add useful depth in places, but they intensify the fragmentation, especially along the hull base and around the left overlap. I score the finished painting 4/10 and the plausibility of the painting process 2/5.

## External critique

The judges gave the finished image a very low score and the process about 1.1/5. I agree that the result is grey, fragmented, too pale in important hull passages, and weakened by the square reserve and many late corrections. I also agree that the early stages should establish larger, clearer value groups before small accents arrive.

I partly disagree with calling the progress frames static: the frames do change as deeper values and edges are added. But the first frame already contains too many pieces of the whole scene, so that disagreement does not change the practical fix. I will reduce the number of small passages, raise the cool passages' colour strength, keep more warm paper visible, avoid explicit rectangular reserves, and use only one low-gain correction round because the painter requires a read of `--touchgain` when touch-up rounds are enabled.
