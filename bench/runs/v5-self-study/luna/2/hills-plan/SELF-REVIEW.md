# Self-review of try 1 (before reading external feedback)

## What works

- The overall color family is close: cool blue-green tree masses against olive and yellow-green slopes, with a warm trail through the center.
- The central winding trail is still recognizable, and its position gives the image a focal path.
- The broad dark canopy distribution loosely frames a lighter center. The painting does not collapse into a single flat value.
- At the opening and first light stage, the sheet remains airy and the green base is restrained.

## What is wrong, and where it went wrong

- The target's hillside is made of coherent lit planes interrupted by tree crowns. The final painting turns much of that structure into a dense mosaic of small, hard, jagged patches. This is the main failure: the foliage reads as repeated angular chips, while the bright grass slopes lose their broad connected shape.
- The trails are overexposed white cutouts in many places, rather than pale warm paths with broken grassy edges. The large pale gap near the center-bottom is too broad and makes the path geometry less faithful.
- The finished image has excessive contrast and dark coverage. Several tree pockets become nearly black and the shadows compete with the center instead of framing it. The subtle lime lights from the reference are mostly missing.
- The ink is too dark and too sparse in a few disconnected spots to unify the marks; some accents look like stray contour fragments. It does not establish useful tree structure.
- The first 10% stage is an acceptable pale underpainting. By the 25% stage, the canopy and slope are already breaking into many separate specks and shards. The strongest loss happens as the later middle and dark passages accumulate (roughly 25–75%): they reinforce the fragmented segmentation rather than connecting the larger tree and hillside shapes. The last quarter adds little correction to the value design, and the closing marks cannot repair those earlier shapes.

## Scores

- Finished painting: **4/10**. The palette, broad light/dark arrangement, and central trail survive, but the shapes are too fragmented and the lights too white for a convincing loose ink-and-wash rendering.
- Plausibility of painting process: **2/5**. It progresses through pale washes and darker additions, so the order is broadly watercolor-like, but the repeated chip-like marks and abrupt value jumps look more like algorithmic stamping than purposeful passages.

## Comparison with the external feedback

- I agree with the low finished-image assessment. The vision panel's 2–3 ratings and its low marks/texture score match my view that the tree shapes became repetitive chips. Its stronger classic edge score makes sense: edges are present, but they do not describe the landscape well.
- I agree with the process panel that the painting reads as a whole-sheet fade-in. Looking at the progress strip, I had focused on the light-to-dark order and underweighted the fact that the whole composition is already legible at 10%, then mostly gains density everywhere together. My process score of 2/5 is consistent with the panel mean of 2.0.
- I do not treat “dark before light” as a general diagnosis: the strip starts pale and darkens later, as the written plan intended. The issue is that this sensible overall order does not create a convincing sequence of locally built forms. I also give less weight to the isolated “region arriving finished while its neighbour is untouched” tell; the repeated whole-field fade-in is much clearer in the strip.

## Changes for try 2

- I will retain a loose ink-and-wash appearance through a small, deliberate sepia drawing layer, while using the precise-watercolor value profile for the broad hills. Try 1's ink-and-wash profile puts a pale foundation over almost the whole crop, which made the earliest progress frame look like a faint completed composition. The hillside is chiefly broad value planes; fine foliage marks are below wash resolution, so shape and value should carry most of it.
- I will use the W15 passage brush selection and fewer, smoother mid washes to favor connected planes over the prior jagged mosaics. I will reduce shadow gain and cool gain to restore the chartreuse lights and stop the tree pockets from becoming nearly black, and widen the paint palette with cerulean and sienna for more varied blue-greens and warm paths.
- I accept the critique of texture and tonal buildup. I reject a broad increase in ink as the fix: the lines already read as disconnected accents, and more tracing would intensify the outline effect rather than repair the painted masses.
