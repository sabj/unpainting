# Try 1: my review before reading the judges

## What works

- The placement reads: the cat occupies the left, the person sits low on the right, and the dark hair mass joins the two silhouettes without filling the white background.
- The warm ochre and umber for the cat, pale face, and cool dark hair keep the main value families distinct. Several white fur and skin lights remain open.
- The cat's eye and the person's two eyes land near the right places. The broad hair silhouette and the cat's upright ears survive into the final image.
- The progress frames move from pale shapes toward stronger values. The early stages leave open paper and the later stages add facial and coat darks.

## What is wrong, and when it began

- The first wash already breaks the cat and face into disconnected pale islands. This is the main early failure: I wanted separate recognizable markings, but the light passages did not first establish a few connected planes. Later passes darken those islands rather than joining them.
- From roughly the middle stages onward, the cat's coat becomes a collection of hard mottles and short fragments instead of directional tabby locks. The forehead, cheeks and chest lose the target's tapering fur marks; the central overlap also becomes an overly strong dark divider.
- The hair reads as a dark mass, but the later dark additions make it chunky and blue-grey rather than a soft charcoal curl mass with a few controlled dry marks. The light gaps are less selective than the target's.
- The face retains its eye placement but loses the target's fine structure around the nose and mouth. Its broad pale center is under-described while small hard patches around the eyes and cheek compete for attention. The closing drawing does not restore enough delicate, purposeful landmarks to fix that balance.
- At the final stage, stronger values improve separation but do not repair the fragmented first wash. The biggest quality loss therefore began in the first wash and accumulated through the middle value passes; the closing stage only had a limited effect.

## Scores

- Finished painting: **6/10**. The composition, palette family and likeness cues are recognizable, but the coat, face and hair marks are less controlled and less fine than the requested light watercolor sketch.
- How plausibly it was painted: **3/5**. The pale-to-dark sequence and retained lights feel plausible, though the progress jumps add many similarly hard patches instead of clearly moving from connected washes to selective dry detail.

## After reading the try 1 critique

- I agree with the process judges that facial features were already legible too early and that the middle frames add contrast across the whole sheet without enough visible change in what is being painted. I saw both in the strip. The early opening details and global light pass made the later image feel like a gradual strengthening of an already-present picture.
- I agree with the low marks and detail scores: the fur is mottled, the hair is chunky, and the face loses its nose and mouth structure. I am changing the middle planes to follow value instead of every color cluster, reserving the small eye and mouth accents for the last paint band, and keeping opening ink to silhouettes.
- I do not accept the isolated note that one region arrives finished while its neighbour is untouched. Across the displayed stages, both subjects develop together; the main timing problem is that both are present too faintly too soon. I also read the late darkening as actual new paint and accents rather than a literal digital dissolve, although the similar-looking early frames make the dissolve comparison understandable.
- The final-progress score of 5/5 is plausible for the finished frame, but it does not describe the whole sequence: the first four stages visibly show the early-detail and contrast-build issues above. The spread between the vision panel's overall 3.0 and the classic score of 7.47 reinforces my choice to use the visible specific notes, not either aggregate alone.
