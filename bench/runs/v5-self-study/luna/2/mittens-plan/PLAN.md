# Mittens portrait crop — round V5, try 2

## Reference read and values

The 300 × 259 target is a loose watercolor portrait sketch on a mostly white sheet. The ginger-and-white cat is cropped by the left and lower edges. A pale human face sits low on the right under a soft, irregular charcoal curl mass. The principal landmarks are the paired human eyes and the cat's two eyes; the cat's nose and whiskers are secondary. The background is unpainted paper.

Read the sheet in four values:

1. **Paper:** open background, cat blaze and muzzle, face forehead and lit cheek, plus all four eye glints.
2. **Pale wash:** warm ochre and raw-sienna underpainting in the cat; a very light warm neutral over the skin.
3. **Middle planes:** ginger tabby markings, eye sockets, cheek turns and the softened body of the hair mass.
4. **Deep accents:** hair interior, pupils, nose and only the darkest tabby marks. The closing sepia pen then restores the target's fine facial edges and whiskers.

The source is light and line-led. I will leave the sheet and near-white planes open rather than tinting the whole crop. Cat markings use warm earth pigments; ultramarine mixed with sepia gives the hair its cool charcoal. I removed rose from the palette because it added a separate color pass without a clear large rose shape in this reference.

## Resolution, moves and paper checks

The sheet is 1200 cells wide on 234.107 mm, so one output cell is **0.195 mm**. The 300 px target maps to 1200 output cells: one target pixel is about **4 output cells (0.78 mm)**. A one-pixel whisker or lid is below a useful watercolor-brush body and belongs to ink-and-wash. The eye openings and broad fur patches span many more cells and read as value planes.

At this scale the physical detail rigger is 1.2 mm wide, a radius of about **3.1 cells**. A 3–4 target-pixel iris is about **12–16 cells** across, giving a radius-to-feature ratio of **0.19–0.26**; it can hold a painted plane with several cells of edge on either side. The smallest one-pixel whisker is only 4 cells wide, so it is drawn with the pen instead: the 0.5 mm nib radius is about **1.3 cells**, or **0.32** of that line width. The broad mop radius is about 97 cells and is reserved for connected masses, not eye detail.

The pale base passages use a watery-to-medium load on **dry** paper, with the fitted large brush and a few connected value shapes. Mid planes use a medium load on fully dry paint. Deep glazes use a controlled medium load after the middle planes have dried. Eye details and lines are dry work. Before each passage the executor checks the measured state over the whole footprint and its neighbours; if the paper is still damp, it waits or works elsewhere. The face remains behind a dry barrier while the hair is painted. No wet-in-wet departure is needed: the reference shows a white sheet and crisp facial landmarks, while softness is limited to selected turning edges.

## Glazes and passage checks

The value plan builds from pale to dark and restores the darkest eye and mouth accents in its last paint band. Mid planes follow target value across nearby color clusters, reducing the posterized islands seen in try 1. One restrained hair shadow glaze follows the hair mass into the hairline; the face shadow glaze follows the shaded cheek and jaw. The cat's own shadow stays within the cat: the cat and person are separate forms, so I do not force a glaze across their overlap. The edge fade is limited to 1.2 mm at dark-to-paper contours, where curls and fur lose into the sheet.

The opening drawing has four low-pressure contour gestures only: cat ear/crown, hair silhouette and face/jaw. It omits the cat and human eyes, nose, mouth and whiskers. The target-registered closing paths carry those details once the paint is dry. This is a deliberate paint-shapes-first choice: the try 1 progress strip showed fine facial detail before the washes had built the forms.

## What changed after try 1, and what I rejected

- I agree that the cat and face were already faintly detailed in the first quarter, that the early sheet changed too little between snapshots, and that the midtone marks became mottled. I reduced opening ink to four silhouettes, restored the base four middle layers, combined midtone domains, and moved small dark landmarks toward the final paint band. The closing lines retain their accurate target registration and now use full share for the requested fine sketch lines.
- I agree with the judges' low marks/detail assessment: fur direction, human nose/mouth and hair curls need clearer, fewer marks. I disagree with the isolated “one region finished while its neighbour is untouched” note; the displayed strip develops both subjects together. The more visible timing error was both subjects appearing too faintly too early.
- I also reject the claim that the entire sequence is simply a literal dissolve. New paint and marks do accumulate at each stage, and the final image reads as a hand-painted sheet. Still, the resemblance to an opacity fade is strong in the early frames, so this plan changes when fine detail arrives and how the mid planes connect.

## Checkpoint intents

- **Opening:** only the light silhouette scaffold is present; the face has no eyes or mouth lines.
- **First pale band:** broad pale cat and skin masses emerge, with paper still dominant and all eye glints open.
- **Middle bands:** cat markings and face turns become distinct as planes, not clusters of equal hard flecks; the hair remains one connected mass.
- **Last paint band:** hair interior and small dark features strengthen after the pale and middle planes have dried.
- **Closing:** selective sepia lines restore the eyes, nose, mouth and whisker directions without enclosing either face in a full outline.

At each stop I will compare the stage intent with the sheet. The first correction target is disconnected pale massing; the second is a dark central seam or patchy fur. I will revise only work that has not yet been painted.
