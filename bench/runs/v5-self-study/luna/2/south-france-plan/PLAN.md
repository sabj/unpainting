# South France hillside village — V5 try 2

## Reference read and value plan

At thumbnail size the crop has four value groups: bare paper and sunlit roof/wall flecks; pale warm stone and yellow-green canopy; middle green tree crowns and turned wall planes; then the cypress, connected canopy shadows, roof undersides and window recesses. The main focal area is the close stone cluster at center-right, with the smaller ridge village repeating it in the distance. There is no open sky passage.

The paper lights are the small gaps between crowns and the palest roof and wall planes. Keep target-near-paper lights out of the washes; do not add rectangular reserves, which would carve artificial seams through the foliage. The lit faces are pale painted planes rather than blank white boxes. The deepest values belong to a few cypress and canopy divisions and the darkest roof recesses; keep them as paint shapes, not black ink fills.

## Region techniques and resolution

- **Upper ridge and distant houses:** wash question. Use a pale, slightly cool green mass with soft or lost edges in the trees. The tiny roofs and walls are pale warm planes. At this run density individual leaves are below useful wash-mark scale, so keep this area as a value mass and use only a few opening roof lines.
- **Left and foreground canopy:** wash question. Lay connected yellow-green and blue-green masses, leaving only the larger target sky gaps as paper. Dry, add a few grouped middle-value crown shapes; dry again before the shadow glaze. Do not make separate equal leaf dots. Small leaf texture is below the roughly four-cell body threshold and should resolve as value and edge, not as individual marks.
- **Cypress and tree divisions:** wash question for the dark forms, ink-and-wash only for a few structural branch or trunk cues. Build cypress from a dark green value shape with a softened or broken edge where it merges into the canopy. Preserve the target's narrow lighter separations; do not turn them into black spikes.
- **Stone cluster at center-right:** ink-and-wash. The opening pen states selected roof ridges, eaves and facade junctions on dry paper. Lay pale ochre and terracotta roof/wall planes as broad washes. After drying, place the middle planes, then the shadow glaze across connected wall and roof sides. Window openings are dark paint accents; closing ink only reinforces a few target-supported eaves and recess edges. Roof junctions and corners need crisp edges; one turning edge may soften into foliage.
- **Lower hillside and foreground:** wash question. One light ground/canopy transition, then a dry shadow passage that links the foreground foliage to the shaded side of the buildings. Keep small ground flecks subordinate to the broad slope.

The sheet is 280 mm wide at 1216 px, about 0.23 mm per output cell. Roof and facade junctions several source pixels wide have a body of multiple output cells, so the broad plane is paint and the finer ridge is pen. Individual canopy flecks are too small to paint distinctly; their grouping and broken edge carry the texture.

## Order, moisture, load and edges

1. **Opening drawing on dry paper:** 12 target-traced architectural lines, ordered around the upper ridge and the close village. They state a few roofs and facade breaks, not every outline. Black ink is explicit and pressure is held below a heavy marker. At each stroke, the executor checks the measured paper state over the stroke footprint and its neighbours; proceed only on dry paper.
2. **Light wash:** broad, transparent, medium-watery washes on dry paper. Keep the green canopy connected and the warm stone light. Avoid painting every paper gap. The run uses the target-fit pigment palette; there is no extra palette substitution.
3. **Middle values:** after each pass is dry, work wet-on-dry with a controlled medium load. Increase the value in grouped tree crowns and turned roof/wall planes. Use one slightly softened edge within distant foliage and crisp edges at the close roof junctions. The plan uses three middle layers rather than the prior two, to build the value change in smaller steps.
4. **Shadow glazes and paint accents:** on fully dry paper, place a connected dark-green glaze across the linked canopy/cypress shadows and the shaded building sides. Keep the darkest passages small and let the roof, wall and foliage shadows relate across their boundaries instead of filling each region separately. Dark windows and recesses are the last paint accents. The dark gain is reduced to 1.0 so the cypress does not overwhelm the stone cluster.
5. **Closing drawing on dry paper:** eight selective target-supported lines, mainly at roof and facade junctions. This is a light structural restatement, not a second tracing of the whole scene. No pen in the pale lights and no foliage scribble.

No wet-in-wet break is planned: this tightly interlocked crop has no broad sky or soft cloud transition, and a wet passage could blur its small architecture. The painter dries between passes as configured. Before every mark, check the actual paper state over the full brush footprint and adjacent cells; if it is wetter than the chosen dry state, wait or work elsewhere. Do not infer a paper reading from elapsed time.

## Try 2 changes and critique decisions

I keep the global `urban-sketch` choice, connected foliage masses, warm village colors and empty family/reserve geometry from try 1. Empty families avoid unsupported rectangular seams; the engine already protects near-paper target lights. I change the middle ladder from two to three layers and lower dark gain from 1.1 to 1.0, so middle planes build more gradually and dark foliage stays subordinate. `layersmooth` rises slightly to 2.4 mm to join small value fragments without erasing the house edges.

Try 1 used 40 opening lines and a much denser closing set. Its stroke count rose from 1,038 just before the closing boundary to 8,700 there. I replace that with 12 opening architecture lines and 8 closing accents, and keep the measured widths and pressure controlled. This addresses the judges' early slab/reveal and late texture-dump concerns while keeping the finished pen selective.

I agree with the low early-stage scores, the slab-like pale start, and the weak foliage marks. I also agree that the close pass added too much texture at once, given the large closing-boundary stroke jump. I reject the process judge's claim that the finished frame is a convincing sketch: the village remains recognizable, but the prior marks do not read as deliberate pen structure to me. I give little weight to the classic edge score of 9.11 because it conflicts with both the visible fragmentation and the vision panel's 1.67 edges/detail score, and the task warns that those judgments are noisy. I do not treat one judge's “region arriving finished while its neighbour is untouched” tell as a proven diagnosis; the strip shows broad coverage, and the main planned correction is the early architectural drawing and slower value progression.

## Stage checks and failure signs

- **Opening:** roofs and facade junctions should read before any wash, while most of the sheet remains bare. Failure: a dark, continuous outline around every building.
- **First light passages:** pale, connected greens and warm stone should emerge without a straight wipe front or a finished building beside untouched paper. Failure: broad opaque slabs, isolated white chips, or the whole sheet appearing to fade in uniformly.
- **Middle passes:** tree crowns should separate into a few value groups and the center-right roofs should retain clear planes. Failure: speckled/dotted trails, disconnected fragments, or the foliage becoming as dark and sharp as the village.
- **Shadow passes:** the cypress and linked canopy shadows should add depth while leaving the lit roofs visible. Failure: black spikes, a uniform dark field, or a patchwork of isolated shadow fills.
- **Closing:** only a few roof and facade junctions should sharpen. Failure: a second full outline, dense texture dump, or pen entering the paper lights.
