# Trumbull V5 try 3 — precise architectural watercolor

## Reference and value map

The crop is an existing watercolor plate on white paper. Its landmarks are the tall left tower, broad sunlit central gable and paired arches, cooler right wing and roof, grouped window recesses, small yellow-green tree, and the street sign and signal. The gable is the focal plane. At thumbnail size use five values: (1) bare-paper cloud gaps and the brightest stone glints; (2) pale blue sky and luminous sunlit stone; (3) warm wall planes, cool roofs and the light tree mass; (4) turned facades, eaves, tree underside and ground shadows; (5) a few deep window and arch recesses, sign, signal and post.

The broad walls must read as connected planes, not as a grid of separate stones. Value 4 is a connected family of architectural and cast shadows. Value 5 stays confined to the actual recesses and street accents. The target is the review source for color and shape; no pixel-level target reads are requested from the painter.

## Style, regions and resolution

Use `precise-watercolor` throughout: connected transparent washes carry the building planes, crisp but irregular edges carry architecture, and selective sepia ink restores only the lines too fine for the paint. The family bounds below describe overlapping areas used to label the painter's own segmentation; they are not painted rectangular panels. No separate family style overrides or rectangular reserves are added.

- **Sky** (`Y1.sky`, upper field): pale blue, values 1–2. Wash question: one connected, watery wet-on-dry graded passage over dry paper, with cloud openings left as bare paper and a crisp roof boundary. The target's broken cloud lights and sharply legible roof silhouettes support this controlled dry start. No dry-brush texture or tiled cloud patches.
- **Left tower and neighboring facade** (`Y1.left-tower`): ochre and raw-sienna light plane, then middle ochres and a cooler/darker return, values 2–4. Wash question: broad, watery-to-medium wet-on-dry passages on dry paper. Edges stay firm at the roof, tower corners and openings; soften only the turning plane. Keep any stone texture as sparse, irregular hints within the continuous wall plane.
- **Central gable** (`Y1.central-gable`): luminous warm ochre face, values 2–4, with the ridge, paired arches and windows as landmarks. Wash question for the broad plane; small recesses are painted late, and the thin arch/window divisions are ink-and-wash. Keep the lit face continuous and reserve its strongest glints as bare paper. The reduced stone-block overlay (`_blk15_bka12`) is only a low-strength accent; stop if it starts to tile the wall.
- **Right wing and roofs** (`Y1.right-wing`): cooler blue-grey roof and receding wall planes, values 3–4. Wash question, wet-on-dry after the light base dries. Keep ridge, eave and wall junctions crisp; distinguish the receding mass by color and value, without a family-boundary outline.
- **Foliage** (`Y1.foliage`): a connected pale yellow-green crown, then a darker underside/right-side shadow, values 3–4. Wash question rather than individual leaves: one irregular mass with a broken silhouette against the wall, followed by connected shadow clumps on dry paper. Do not stipple or blur the nearby window details.
- **Street and ground** (`Y1.street`): a few pale-to-middle connected pavement planes, values 2–4; sign, signal and post are late value-5 accents. Wash question for ground, controlled small shapes for the street objects. Use broken ground edges and keep the narrow sign and signal edges crisp.
- **Windows and arches across the building families**: paint recess bodies after the surrounding wall is dry, mostly values 4–5. The smallest important mullion is about one source pixel, or 4 cells at the 1200 px native output (0.933 mm on this sheet). Use the traced ink-and-wash lines for such features, not a wash brush. The measured trace widths are about 0.96–1.35 source pixels; with `_dwg100` and the 0.42-cell width margin this gives roughly 2.2–3.7 output cells of contact (radius about 1.1–1.9 cells), so a 4-cell mullion is about 2.1–3.6 times the nib radius. Larger arch strokes get a broad painted recess plus selective ink on dry paint.

The sheet is 280 mm wide at 1200 px: one output cell is 0.233 mm. The 300 px source is enlarged fourfold. Detail below that four-cell feature scale is not assigned to wash marks. Opening and closing path files contain 17 and 115 target-supported line paths respectively; the opening set is only the long structural drawing, while the closing set restores selected window grids, arches, roof junctions and street details. Their measured widths span 0.88–1.40 source pixels, which scale to 3.52–5.60 cells; with `_dwg100` and the 0.42-cell margin the pen contact is about 3.10–5.18 cells (radius 1.55–2.59). A 4-cell mullion is therefore about 1.5–2.6 times the nib radius. The ink is sepia (`_sep4`), and the reduced width gain keeps it subordinate to the painted planes.

## Protected lights, paper state and order

Protect cloud openings and small, near-paper stone glints as bare paper with the target's near-paper mask. The bright central wall is a pale painted plane, not a white reserve. No eye catchlights or other portrait lights are present. If a stone glint is too sharp at the finish, tone only that small plane with a watery pale glaze; do not glaze the cloud openings.

1. **Opening drawing, dry paper:** use the 17 sparse structural paths for the tower/gable/roof breaks, major arches and sign/post silhouette. Keep the drawing light and target-supported; it should guide the broad masses, not outline every window or stone.
2. **Light washes, then dry:** lay the pale sky and broad local light planes in watery loads. Paint each broad region as a connected passage, leaving the cloud and stone reserves visible. The sheet should still show abundant white paper and only a partial value structure. The executor must check measured moisture on the whole footprint and its neighbours before each mark; wait or work elsewhere if the passage is not at its planned state.
3. **Middle planes, then dry:** build the tower and gable ochres, cool right wing/roof, foliage mass and ground with medium loads on dry paper. Use the four middle steps already in the base ladder, from light to stronger value. Keep large walls connected; do not add texture to fill unused paper.
4. **Sparse wall hints:** allow the planes to dry, then use the irregular 15% stone-block coverage at 12% strength. Keep this below the plane value and irregular in spacing; reject a grid, repeated chips or isolated pale islands.
5. **Shadow glazes, then dry:** apply the last connected shadow family on dry paper where the reference turns the tower, crosses the gable/roof junctions, darkens the receding wing, and shades the tree and ground. Let a shadow pass cross adjacent architectural labels where the target shows one continuous form. Keep one chosen soft turning edge and crisp edges at the target's roof, arch and opening boundaries. Paint the deepest window/arch recesses and street accents last; do not spread value 5 across a whole facade.
6. **Closing drawing, dry paint:** lay selective measured sepia lines from the 115-path file, focused on mullions, arch contours and structural junctions. Use tapered connected gestures; no hatching, stone outlines, or ink used to rescue a blurred wash.

## Try 3 changes and critique decisions

Try 2's saved plan passed admission, but the render failed before producing a finished painting because its four extra ordering options (`--ordersubject`, `--ordersubjmax`, `--ordersubjmin`, `--orderregions`) were reported as unread by the painter. I agree with the critique's actionable diagnosis and remove all four. The supported `--drawwant 0.8` remains; the painter's normal passage order now controls the washes. I keep the earlier choices for the broad facade wash, sparse opening structure, restrained stone hints, reduced sepia width and target-shaped lights because the failed render supplied no painted result to justify changing those art decisions. There were no visual judge notes to accept or reject; I reject any inference that this validation failure demonstrates an artistic defect.

## Stage intents and failure signs

- Before the first wash, a few light structural lines identify the gable, tower and major junctions.
- Early light frames show the pale connected wash building across the sheet while preserving white cloud gaps and a luminous gable. A fully colored, detailed scene at the first light stage, scalloped white holes through a wall, or hard rectangular family edges indicate failure.
- The middle ladder should make the central gable the broadest warm light plane against the cooler roofs and right wing. A tiled facade, equal-value chips, blurred window openings or a mottled tree of separate dots indicate failure.
- After shadow glazes, the building should turn in connected value groups. Dark rims around every region, rectangular seams, muddy front walls, or lost cloud/gable lights indicate failure.
- At finish, the tower, gable ridge, paired arches, window groups, roofline, tree silhouette and street sign should read at thumbnail size. Ink remains selective and lighter than the deepest painted recesses.
