# Self-review — previous White Ships painting

## My read before the judges' notes

The previous painting keeps the target's cool blue-gray and warm ochre palette, and the central blue hull, its diagonal orange seam, the two dark round forms at upper right, and the dark lower water are still broadly recognizable. The pale unpainted areas also preserve some of the target's light.

The likeness is weak. The target is organized around a large, rounded blue hull against a pale, connected background, with a few clear rigging lines and a low band of water. The result breaks the hull, background, and water into jagged, similarly busy fragments. It loses the hull's smooth curved silhouette and readable light-to-shadow planes; the foreground has too many holes and unrelated hard edges. Its dark mass is much too large and uniform, while the pale background is cut into disconnected white shapes. The left support/rigging and small boat details are muddy rather than structural.

I score the finished painting **3/10**. Some color and landmark placement survive, but the major value shapes and hull contour do not. I score the painting's plausibility **2/5**: pigment transparency and granulation suggest watercolor, but repeated cell-like edges, abrupt patch boundaries, and the overworked dark areas do not read as deliberate hand-painted passages.

## Where the progression failed

At 10% of strokes, the broad composition is already present, but the hull is pale and indistinct and the large background and water masses are weakly connected. By 25%, the surface has become a patchwork of hard-edged pieces instead of resolving into broad planes. The 50% stage pushes the hull and foreground too dark; later stages mostly repeat that fragmented structure rather than clarify the silhouette. The main failure therefore starts in the first broad wash and the early mid-value passes, then is amplified by later darks. This is a plan/segmentation and value-shape problem, not a missing final outline.

## Changes to consider

For this try I should simplify the plan around the target's large connected planes: a light warm-gray background, the rounded blue hull with a pale central plane and one coherent shadow side, the narrow ochre seam and supports, and a restrained dark water band. I should protect only the brightest hull accents and rigging gaps, keep the foreground water connected, and avoid asking late passes to repair an over-fragmented first wash. Since the requested style is pure watercolor, I should use no opening or closing ink; the small structural marks need to come from controlled dry-paper paint where they are large enough to survive at this scale.

## External critique and decisions

The judges' overall scores (2, 3, 3) support my own low score. I agree with their process diagnosis: the earliest frame already shows the whole scene as a faint image, and later frames add scalloped wash edges and round holes. That matches what I see in the stage strip. My own read places the first structural failure in the broad wash and early mid-value passages; the judges' description of a fade-in is a useful account of how that failure appears to a viewer. I also agree that the late stages do not recover the broad hull form.

I reject the high classic edge score (9.13) and marks score (6.62) as useful guidance for this painting: on direct comparison, those edges are jagged and fragmented, and the marks obscure the hull curve instead of explaining it. The classic value score (5.15) is closer to my view: some value masses and the dark contact survive, but the pale background and blue hull are not grouped well enough. I also reject the implication that simply adding more outlines or detail would help; the task requests pure watercolor, and the target's form needs broader paint shapes.

For try 3, I will restore a four-step middle build and remove the explicit high paper threshold that contributed to oversized white holes. I will keep the no-ink decision, use fewer warm accents, and keep one low-gain correction round only. I will watch for the hull as one curved plane in the first wash and for later values to deepen that plane rather than reveal many disconnected islands.
