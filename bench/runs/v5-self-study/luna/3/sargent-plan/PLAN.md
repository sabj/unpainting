# White Ships (Sargent), V5 try 3

## Reference read and value plan

This is a loose pure-watercolor crop on a warm sheet. The main read is the rounded blue-grey hull descending through the centre into a dark lower contact. Pale sail and sheet shapes sit behind it, especially at upper right. At left, a cropped cool vessel is crossed by ochre spars and rigging. A turquoise water plane opens at right and joins the darker lower edge. The focal point is the hull's curved turn and the warm/dark junction at left; small rigging and upper-right fittings are secondary.

Four values, from light to dark:

1. Warm near-paper sheet and pale sail shapes as thin transparent tints; no explicit light reserves are planned.
2. A light blue hull body and a pale turquoise water field.
3. Blue-grey turning planes on the hull and left overlap, plus the mid-value water turn.
4. A connected lower-hull and water contact, with compact dark paint at the real spar junctions and upper-right fittings.

Keep the large hull curve readable before its accents. No black outline or ink is needed: form and structure come from paint edges, value changes, and reserved paper.

## What changed and why

I agree with the previous judges that the early image looked like a faint whole-scene fade, and that scalloped wash edges and holes undermined the form. My own review also found that the hull and water fractured during the early broad wash and mid-value steps, then grew too dark without regaining the hull silhouette. I reject the unusually high classic edge and mark scores: the visible edges are jagged and the marks interrupt rather than describe the hull. I also reject adding more outlines or detail as a remedy; this is a pure-watercolor task.

- I restore the base four middle layers after the previous try replaced them with two. The planner's broad first middle plane and progressively darker dry layers should make the curved hull build more gradually.
- I first raised the shape floor from 500 to 800 cells. At the 16-pass look, the hull and background still broke into too many scalloped pieces, so I revised the unpainted passes to a 1400-cell floor and 3 mm value smoothing. The later middle plan had fewer passages per layer. The finished look has a clearer hull silhouette, but the revision did not remove all the hard fragments.
- I remove the previous explicit `paperdelta 30`, which protected too many near-paper cells and made oversized white holes. The default threshold keeps the target's pale sheet while allowing its cream-tinted planes to read.
- I keep W15's brush choice per passage for broad hull and water strokes with smaller dry-paper accents, but reduce dark gain from 1.3 to 1.1 because the previous lower hull became a nearly uniform dark slab.
- I keep cold-press texture, the blue/ochre palette, cool gain 1.6, and one low-gain correction round. I disable sky blue pigment for this non-sky subject and explicitly disable drawing. Opening and closing lines are empty: no ink is used.
- The first four light passes and the first middle layer are already on the sheet; the revision applies only to the remaining passes.
- There are no explicit rectangular reserves. The admitted value plan holds 0% of cells as bare paper, so the pale hull sheen, sail lights, and water glints are painted as very light tints rather than blank holes. If the first wash darkens them, that is a plan failure to correct before adding detail.

## Regions and technique

At 1200 px, a cell is 0.233 mm. The smallest useful hull contour is a broad shape edge. The narrow ochre rigging is about 4–8 cells (0.93–1.87 mm) where it is visible; that is near the minimum width for a stable watercolor mark, so finer strands are simplified to a few broken paint accents. Nothing is drawn in the opening stage.

Before each stroke, the executor checks measured moisture over the full footprint and its neighboring paper. The load and moisture must fit the chosen move. If the paper state is wrong, it waits or works elsewhere; it does not substitute an assumed reading.

## Layer order and checkpoint intents

1. **Opening:** empty drawing on the warm sheet. There must be no ink, tracing, or early fine rigging.
2. **Large light washes:** a connected pale sheet/sail tint, a light hull foundation, and a pale turquoise water field. The first painted look should still be mostly light; the hull curve should begin to read as one shape. Stop and revise if the surface is already tiled with small islands or round holes.
3. **Four middle layers:** each pass dries before the next. The broad hull plane appears first; later passes narrow to the turning plane and lower side. The left cool mass and water field stay distinct but connected where their silhouettes meet. Stop and revise if the hull has become a dark slab or lost its pale centre.
4. **Shared shadow glaze:** on dry paint, one connected cool glaze crosses the lower hull and adjacent water only at the target's shared contact. It must not trace every segmentation edge. Compact cool and ochre paint accents follow on dry paper at the true junctions.
5. **Finish:** no closing drawing. The result should read at thumbnail size as warm pale sheet, rounded blue hull, restrained left overlap, turquoise right water, and one dark lower contact. Inspect the 1200 px sheet for separated paint bodies and jagged holes; do not use an upscaled half-width image to judge fine details.

The final comparison retains the curved hull and lower contact, but the hull is greyer than the reference and the pale sail and water are still broken by hard shapes. The remaining mismatch is visible in the final stage; I finished the planned dark values and touch-up rather than add more detail or ink.

## Acceptance and revision criteria

Accept a revision only when a visible failure matches one of these criteria: (1) if the first wash is broken into small islands or round holes, increase the shape floor and check the resulting plan before continuing; (2) if the hull is an even dark slab, reduce the dark gain or narrow the shadow layer; (3) if the turquoise water separates into bands, replan it as one connected field and keep later reflections sparse; (4) if a broad hull or water shape is missing, correct that value plane before adding detail. Keep pure watercolor, no ink, and preserve the target's four-value order. The paper-state check applies before every revised or unrevised mark.
