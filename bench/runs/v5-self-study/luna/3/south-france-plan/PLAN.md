# South France hillside village — V5 try 3

## Reference read and value thumbnail

At thumbnail size the target has four useful value groups: (1) paper lights in the winding path, lit roof planes and a few gaps between crowns; (2) pale ochre stone and light sage foliage; (3) olive and blue-green crowns with the turned, cooler sides of the houses; (4) a few cypress and canopy valleys, roof undersides and small dark openings. The focal point is the close village at center-right; the upper ridge houses repeat it at smaller scale. There is no open sky region in this crop.

Keep the light path and the brightest roof planes as pale painted shapes or paper where the target is nearly white. The value planner already protects target-near-paper lights, so I use no rectangular reserves and no family boxes that could make false straight seams. These lights are not blank rectangular holes: their irregular path and roof edges carry the form. The darkest values stay limited to connected foliage shadows, cypress shapes and a few recesses; black ink carries architecture, never a painted dark mass.

## Region decisions and resolution

The sheet is 280 mm wide at 1216 px: one native cell is 0.230 mm. The target is 304 px wide, so one target pixel spans four native cells. A roof ridge or tiny opening around one target pixel wide is therefore about four cells across, at the lower edge of the useful watercolor-mark threshold. In this plan it is an ink-and-wash feature. Broader roof and wall planes are washes. With the measured pen widths in the line files and the recipe's 2x width gain, the typical contact is about 3–4 native cells across (radius about 1.5–2 cells). An 8-cell window feature is roughly 4–5 times that radius; the thinnest 4-cell mullion is only about 2–3 times the radius, so keep it as a pen line and do not ask the wash to resolve it.

## Drawing, pigment and layers

The style is `urban-sketch`. Ink is explicitly **black ink** (`black-ink` profile), at medium pressure and measured width. The opening file holds 18 selected target-supported architectural paths; it gives the ridge and close village a visible framework without tracing every edge. The closing file holds 15 other selected roof and facade accents. No pen enters the paper lights or scribbles the foliage. The painter checks measured paper state over the full stroke footprint and its neighbors before every mark; if a stroke needs dry paper, it waits or moves elsewhere rather than inferring dryness from elapsed time.

1. **Opening drawing — dry paper, low-water black ink, controlled medium load.** Draw the selected ridge/eave and close-building junctions as connected gestures. Width and pressure taper with the line. Stage intent: the two house clusters read before any wash, with most foliage and ground still bare. Failure: a continuous outline around every house or lines too faint to survive the first wash.
2. **Light washes — glossy or evenly damp paper only for the distant foliage underwash; otherwise dry paper.** Use a watery, pale pigment load and broad connected strokes. Keep the distant wet-in-wet move inside the green mass with a dry barrier beside the buildings. The nearer canopy, path, roofs and walls use wet-on-dry. Stage intent: pale masses and light planes appear in distinct connected shapes, not as a sheet-wide faint tone. Failure: a straight travelling front, a uniform veil over the whole sheet, or isolated white chips inside a mass.
3. **Middle values — fully dry, wet-on-dry, medium water and medium pigment.** Add a few grouped green crown shapes and the warmer/cooler roof and wall turns. The outer foliage may lose one edge; the close roofs retain a crisp edge. Stage intent: village planes and crown groups separate while the path stays legible. Failure: equal-value speckle, parallel bands, or every crown receiving the same texture.
4. **Shadow glazes — fully dry, wet-on-dry, medium water with a stronger but transparent pigment load.** Glaze one connects canopy shadows across the lower foliage and path edge. Glaze two crosses the shaded roof undersides, facades and adjoining tree contact in the close village. These are two linked shadow shapes, not separate region fills. Stage intent: depth increases without burying pale roofs or breaking the hillside into tiles. Failure: isolated dark chips, a black cypress overpowering the focal buildings, or a shadow boundary that stops artificially at a region edge.
5. **Paint accents, then closing pen — dry paper, low-water controlled load.** Place a few dark window/recess paint accents last. Add only the selected closing roof and facade strokes with black ink at moderated pressure. Stage intent: the close village sharpens at its corners while the foliage remains mostly wash. Failure: a second tracing, pen over lit planes, or a late mass of tiny marks.

The wet-in-wet underwash in the distant foliage is the one named break from dry layering: the target has soft merging in those distant tree masses. Architecture, foreground crown details, path edges and every pen mark stay out of that wet footprint. The painter dries between value passes. Before every mark it checks paper state across the complete footprint and neighboring cells; glossy paper is for the distant merge, sheen for controlled softening, and dry paper for roofs, windows, glazes and ink. A wetter brush on merely damp paper is not used to force a soft edge.

## Try 3 changes and critique decisions

I keep the global urban-sketch style and the prior decision to avoid rectangular reserves and family boundaries. My own review found a readable composition and plausible warm/cool palette, but the first wash already split the canopy and path into disconnected pieces; later stages amplified those chips, and the close village lacked a visible pen hierarchy. I agree with the external progression critique about the faint sheet-wide reveal, straight front and dotted trails. The checkpoint record also shows 1,020 strokes before the closing boundary and 8,748 at the next look, so the ending was dominated by touch-ups rather than a few deliberate closing lines.

The new 18-line opening makes the architectural sketch visible before washing, and the 15-line closing set adds selected structure only. I lower texture variety from 0.8 to 0.3 so mark direction and spacing vary less chaotically; reduce touchpoints from 2 to 1 to limit the late texture dump; reduce dark gain from 1.0 to 0.9 so the dark tree accents do not fragment the focal village; and use two middle layers rather than three so the pale wash, grouped mid values and final shadows read as clearer value steps. The black ink and target palette remain appropriate to the requested style. I do not accept the external overall score as a complete description: the target placement and warm building group remain legible to my eye, and the judges are noisy. I also reject the isolated high classic edge score as proof that the last painting's architecture was sufficiently clear; it conflicts with my visual review and the low vision edge/detail ratings.

At the first pre-wash look, the opening paths were in the right architectural locations but were too faint to make the village read at target scale. That opening drawing is fixed on the sheet, so I am not trying to repair it with a full outline. For the remaining work I restore the selected black pen to the recipe's full line pressure and widen only the 15 dry-paper closing accents slightly; those strokes can clarify the roof and facade junctions after the washes.

After pass 3, I saw a second failure in the broad yellow underwash: it made two diagonal sweep bands across the page, which read as a wipe rather than a connected watercolor mass. The dry target has no sky, so the sky lanes and sky-specific layer split are not justified here. For the unpainted passes I switch to `region_w15`'s brush choice per passage, remove the sky lanes, and turn off sky split and layer hue. I also tried to remove the sky blue tube, but the executor refused because that would change the pigment set after painting began. That revision changed nothing. I keep the base blue tube and its flocculation at their original settings, preserve the fixed palette, and limit the next revision to the brush choice, lane behavior, and value-layer assignment.

## Plan checks and acceptance

Before physics, the admission check and half-width planning preview are the fast proof: verify no unsupported straight reserve or family seam, no parallel band plan, a visible line layer, protected target lights, connected mass shapes, and a viable stroke budget. At every paint stop, compare the sheet at target scale to that stage's intent above. Stop or revise at the first visible failure rather than asking later ink to repair a blurred wash.

I accept this plan's result against the watercolor judging list: (1) value shapes read at thumbnail size; (2) the path and lit roof shapes remain in place and toned rather than glaring; (3) hard, soft and lost edges match their regions; (4) ridge houses, close rooflines and the path keep their landmarks and proportions; (5) marks are purposeful with no stipple, stamps, scratches, uniform noise or tiled patches; (6) progress moves from a visible sketch to pale wash, grouped mid values and shadows; (7) the untouched paper is limited to the target's lights and irregular gaps, since this reference is a full hillside crop rather than a vignetted plate.
