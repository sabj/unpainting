# Try 2 self-review (written before reading external feedback)

The target reads as a compact hillside village: warm pale roofs and walls sit in a few clear clusters, with dark cypress accents and a large, connected green canopy. The lightest path and building planes separate those masses. The finished painting keeps the main placement of the village and the bright winding path, and its warm/cool palette is plausible. The right-hand building cluster remains recognizable.

The result is too uniformly broken into small angular flecks. The foliage has many bright holes and isolated dark chips, so the canopy loses the target's larger connected tree shapes and depth. The central path is over-bright and jagged. The houses have some correct warm planes, but their rooflines and openings are not stated clearly enough to carry the architecture. The target asks for an urban sketch with pen and light wash; this reads as a busy mosaic wash with little visible, selective pen structure.

The error begins in the first wash: the large foliage and path masses are already split into many small disconnected pieces instead of one or two connected light passages with protected, target-shaped openings. Later values amplify that fragmentation, while the closing stage does not add enough deliberate roof junctions, eaves, and window marks to establish the buildings. The mid and dark stages also distribute marks across the foliage too evenly; the dark accents should be concentrated under canopy masses and around the focal village.

My scores: **5/10** for the finished painting and **2/5** for how plausibly it was painted. The composition and color relationships provide a useful base, but the mark pattern and missing line hierarchy make the result feel more mechanically tiled than like a person sketching and glazing.

## Comparison with external feedback

I agree with the progression judges that the 10% and 25% frames look too much like a uniform fade or straight travelling front, and that the later scattered marks do not read as drawn edges. The visible stage strip supports those specific criticisms. I also agree that the painting needs a clearer sequence of deliberate structural marks before the washes and a smaller, more purposeful texture vocabulary.

I give the external overall score less weight than the diagnosis: the panel's 2–3 ratings are harsher than my 5/10, because the village placement, central path, warm stone notes, and right-hand building group remain legible to me. The classic edge score of 9.1 conflicts with both the visible broken foliage and the vision panel's low edge/detail ratings, so I do not use it as evidence that the architecture is already sharp. The classic mark score also does not resolve whether the repeated flecks look hand-made at this scale. I reject any conclusion that the high classic edge number alone justifies keeping the prior technique unchanged.
