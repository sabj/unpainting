# Self-review: try 2

## My read before the judges' notes

The previous sheet gets the large composition mostly right: the dark tree canopy frames the lighter grassy slope, and the pale winding trail occupies the right route and broad bends. Its light, middle and dark groups are legible at thumbnail size.

The trail is too white and wide in places, especially at the large lower bend, where the target is a warm ochre path with a crisp edge. The hillside is too uniformly green and covered in similarly sized angular patches; the target varies more in hue and has softer, broken transitions between sunlit grass and brush. Several canopy edges are also too hard and tiled. Dark foliage does not reach the target's depth consistently, while the upper left gains a large dark mass that competes with the path. Thin dark opening marks cross the scene without reading as useful trunks or branches. The bottom edge is left as an abrupt unpainted strip.

The main shape and color problems begin in the first wash and persist through later passes: the progress strip already shows an over-regular, green segmentation and an over-white trail. Later darks add contrast but do not unify the masses; closing lines make the stray marks more visible. For this try I should preserve the trail as a warm light rather than blank paper, group grass into fewer broad planes, and reserve ink for a few purposeful tree gestures. I would paint the canopy as connected masses with soft internal darks and let only selected nearer edges stay crisp.

**My scores:** finished painting 5/10; plausibility of the painting process 2/5. The sequence has a credible pale-to-dark progression, but its early marks and later repeated polygon patches look more like a segmentation being filled than a person making connected washes.

## Comparison with try 2 feedback

I agree with the judges' direction: the two vision judges scored the result 4/10 and 3/10, and the classic rubric was weaker on value (5.66) than edges (8.63) or marks (7.41). That matches my read that the value masses and color grouping need more work than simply sharpening the edges. The judges did not provide written, region-specific notes, and the progression stage scores were all missing, so I cannot use their report to claim a particular passage or mark caused the low score. I disagree with treating the strong classic edge score as evidence that the painting is already well resolved: the visible repeated hard patches are a defect at the whole-sheet scale even if their contours score well. I will address connected value masses and the warm trail while retaining the few clear silhouette edges.
