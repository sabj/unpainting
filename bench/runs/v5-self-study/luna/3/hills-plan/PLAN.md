# Hills — V5 try 3 plan

## Read of the target

The 306 × 242 crop is filled edge to edge with a steep sunlit hillside and broken tree canopy. At thumbnail size I want four value groups: a few paper-bright flecks; pale ochre-green grass and warm sandy tracks; connected olive and yellow-green planes; then blue-green canopy shadows with a handful of deep cores. The focal route is the warm trail as it bends through the center and toward the lower edge. Its center is a pale painted plane, not a white cut-out. The crop has no sky, architecture or paper border.

The main canopy shapes and the diagonal sunny openings carry the likeness. Small leaf flecks are only about 1–2 target pixels, or 4–8 output cells at 1224 px; they sit at the density threshold and are too small for repeated isolated watercolor marks. I treat foliage as connected value masses with broken silhouettes. Larger canopy clumps and trail widths are roughly 16–32 output cells across; W15's selected broad brush should stay around a 4–8 cell radius there (about 0.25–0.5 of a clump or trail width). The sparse closing lines are about 2–3 output cells wide against a roughly 4-cell target branch, enough to carry a few tree forks without tracing the leaf edges.

## Region decisions and techniques

I use the **precise-watercolor value profile** to give this landscape a pale foundation, shaped middle planes and cross-region shadow glazes. The loose ink-and-wash character comes from broad irregular washes, broken canopy edges and selective sepia branch marks. At the first stop, the ink-and-wash profile's stage report showed only a light band and a dark band, leaving no middle values; I changed profiles before the first wash so the planned progression matches the required landscape forms. There are no manual family rectangles or paper reserves: the canopy, grass and tracks interleave across the crop, and rectangular style boundaries would make unsupported seams. The painter's target segmentation assigns the irregular shapes. A full-crop family would add no useful boundary, so `families` and `styleSelection.regions` remain empty.

- **Canopy throughout the crop:** wash question for the broad value masses. Start with a watery, light olive wash on dry paper; on dry paper build connected mid-value clumps with a medium load, then glaze the shadow side with a thicker, cool blue-green mix. Use sap green, ultramarine and cerulean for cool darks, with sienna to keep some shadow groups warm. Preserve a few small paper flecks inside the masses. Break the outside edge where foliage meets grass; keep selected tree-to-slope contacts crisp and allow adjacent darks to join where the target joins them. No leaf stamps, dots or all-over ink texture.
- **Sunny slopes and ridge planes:** wash question. Put down a broad, watery yellow-green and ochre foundation, then use two medium-load dry-paper passages to shape the lit ridges and their olive turns. The open grass remains a plane; individual grass blades are below useful resolution. Keep the ridge edge clear against dark canopy, soften one turning edge with clean water where the hillside rolls away, and let shadows connect across adjacent slope and canopy shapes only where the reference shows a joined mass.
- **Winding tracks and narrow light cuts:** wash question for their warm local color. Hold each track as a pale ochre/raw-sienna painted shape while green strokes work around it. Preserve only tiny cream glints as bare paper; do not reserve the broad route, outline its center with ink, or let it become a stark white ribbon. Keep one side of a turn crisp and break the other into the hillside.

The palette adds ochre to hansa, sap green, ultramarine and cerulean. This should restore the warm route while keeping the sunny yellow-green and cool canopy families. `_relonly` keeps the near-paper relative highlights but removes the separate broad paper estimate that can flatten a crop with no actual paper margin. The base cold-press paper remains. `_noslane` disables sky lanes, and the final options disable sky-blue loading, sky splitting and layer-hue shifts because the crop has no sky; this also avoids giving the ground a special sky pigment.

## Order, paper state and drawing

1. **Opening:** no pen guide. This crop has no linear structure that needs a contour drawing; the first connected washes can establish the hill and trail shapes directly. This is a named mass-first choice. It also avoids the previous try's long guide strokes, which crossed the image without reading as branches or trail edges.
2. **Light foundation:** watery, light pigment, wet on dry over dry paper. Make the first stage a loose connected foundation for the sunny field and canopy rather than one even veil. Keep the route warm and visible, and leave the brightest small flecks alone.
3. **Two middle layers (`_lay2`):** after the foundation dries, use medium watery loads on dry paper to separate hill planes from foliage and strengthen the turns in the trail. These are broad connected shapes, not a repeated set of similarly sized patches. Keep the foliage silhouette irregular; reserve detail rather than filling unused paper with marks.
4. **Shadow and recess glazes:** after the middle layers dry, lay the cooler, thicker shadow glazes on dry paper. The base recipe's two recess layers may cross canopy and hillside boundaries when a shadow connects. Keep them away from the warm path and pale ridge. `_dk9` keeps the deepest values strong but below the previous overly patchy, blue-green contrast; `_cg10` returns the cool gain to neutral. `_ls10` reduces the smoothing that made marks read as uniform patches. `_efade` gives only the darkest mass edges a short, broken transition.
5. **Closing ink:** on dry paint, add four short sepia gestures only: a central trunk and two forks, then one small edge-tree fork. Keep them inside shadowed canopy, vary contact and taper, and leave both trails and open grass untouched. The line paths are narrow enough to read as branches; they do not trace the canopy or rescue paint edges. No ink fill or hatch field.

Before every mark the executor checks measured paper state over the stroke footprint and its neighbors. Washes and glazes require dry paper at their start; any soft merging is confined to neighbors inside the same connected mass. If the measured state is damp where a crisp ridge, track edge or branch needs to land, wait or work elsewhere. The specified watercolor consistency and paper state travel together: watery for the first broad wash, medium for the shaping layers, thicker for shadow cores, and controlled low-load ink on dry paint.

## What I changed after try 2

My pre-feedback review was 5/10 for the finished painting and 2/5 for the process. The target and sheet show a recognizable composition, but the trail became too white, the field too uniformly green, and both field and canopy broke into similar hard patches. Five long opening marks also remained visible as stray lines. I agree with the external scores' direction: the vision judges gave 4/10 and 3/10, while the classic value score (5.66) was lower than edge (8.63) and mark (7.41). Their report has no written regional diagnosis and no progression scores, so I do not infer which stage or passage they disliked. I reject reading the strong aggregate edge score as a reason to preserve the patchy hard-edge treatment; my visual comparison shows that edge repetition is part of the problem.

For this try I first selected the ink-and-wash profile to match the requested style directly, while keeping ink to four closing gestures rather than opening trail guides. At the opening stop, the resolved passage list showed that profile had only a light band and a dark band. Before paint was laid, I switched to the precise-watercolor value profile so the hills and canopy can form middle planes before the shadow glazes; the washes and selected sepia marks still carry the loose ink-and-wash character. I added ochre to the warm and cool pigment set, removed the broad paper estimate while retaining relative near-paper lights, disabled irrelevant sky-specific pigment and hue handling as well as sky lanes, restored neutral cool gain, reduced layer smoothing, and set dark gain just below neutral. W15 retains brush choice per passage; two middle layers remain so the field and canopy can separate before the final glaze. I kept the crop free of hand-drawn family boxes and reserved-light rectangles because the forms cross the crop irregularly. I rejected adding more path tracing: color edges already carry the route, and the previous extra guides made the progress image less convincing.

## Checkpoints and failure signs

- **Opening:** blank, ready for the first wash. Any unexpected drawn route or contour means the empty opening file was not used.
- **First wash:** broad hill and canopy shapes begin to separate; the trail reads warm, not white. A uniform veil, green-only color, or path-shaped gaps means the palette or light handling needs revision before adding shadows.
- **Middle layers:** sunny slope and canopy read as connected masses with different edge character. Repeated equal polygons, hard rings on every clump, noisy leaf dots or a lost warm trail are failures; revise the remaining layers rather than adding more texture.
- **Shadow glazes:** cool connected darks frame the bright trail and the tree groups have a clear light-to-dark structure. Blue-black islands, a blanket of dark paint, or a dark glaze crossing the trail are failures.
- **Closing:** the few sepia marks clarify tree structure and remain subordinate to paint. A line crossing open grass or path, uniform dark tracing, or ink used to repair a blurry edge is a failure.

Judge the finished sheet first at thumbnail size for the four value groups, the warm central trail and connected canopy masses, then at native 1224 px for broken edges and the restrained branch lines. Do not judge small foliage by individual leaf fidelity; it is a mass-and-edge question at this crop's resolution.
