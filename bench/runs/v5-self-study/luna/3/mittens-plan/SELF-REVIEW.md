# Self review — try 2

## My read before the judges' notes

The previous painting gets the broad arrangement across: the cat sits left, the person is on the right, and the large paper-white gap between them keeps the two profiles distinct. The warm cat against the cooler dark hair is also a useful value and color contrast. The eyes and nose are identifiable at a glance, and the untouched highlights help the face feel light.

The likeness and watercolor character are only partly successful. The cat face has become a collection of isolated orange and grey-brown patches instead of a few connected fur masses with directional edges. Its long white muzzle and the white area through the center of the sheet dominate; some necessary planes around the nose and cheeks are too faint. The person's hair is a dark, lumpy chain of marks with hard polygonal holes and a few stray line artifacts, rather than connected curls with varied edges. The human features have some definition, but the brows and eyes are too heavy and the face reads more like a set of outlined parts than pale planes with selective linework. Overall, the image is much more fragmented and contrasty than the requested fine-line, light-wash portrait sketch.

I think the failure began in the first color passes: the large fur and hair masses were divided into small cells and dried as separate marks, so later values reinforced the patchwork rather than unifying it. Later dark passes made the hair and eye edges too graphic. Closing marks did not restore the broad pale planes or fix the shapes that had already fragmented.

**My scores:** finished painting **4.5/10**; plausibility of the painting process **2.5/5**. The light-to-dark buildup is visible, but repeated discrete dabs, fragmented edges and the abrupt dark clusters do not convincingly read as a person laying connected washes and then following fur and curl direction.

## After reading the judges' notes

I agree with their main process critique. The progress strip supports the “faint sheet” reading at 10%, and the similarity between the 25%, 50% and 75% images shows that the modeling arrives too gradually to read as purposeful value building. At closing, the stroke count jumps from 803 to 4,091; the sudden sharpening is real. Their notes about scalloped edges and round holes also match my read of the cat and hair masses.

I disagree that a subject region finishes while its neighbor is untouched: both figures develop together in the strip. I also would not call the entire run a literal fade or filter. New marks accumulate and the paint does make broad shapes, but the very pale global start and the large closing-line dump make that impression understandable. The judges' final overall 3/10 is harsher than my 4.5/10: the likeness and value arrangement are recognizable, though the edge/detail and mark texture weaknesses are substantial. I put more weight on that recognizable structure; their process notes correctly identify a serious defect my final-image read underweighted.

For this try I will put the key eye, nose and mouth landmarks into a light opening sketch, leave the closing drawing with a smaller set of whisker and contour marks, remove the explicit edge fade that may be contributing to scallops, and filter very small paint shapes. I retain the four-step value ladder and paper-white reserves; I do not accept the claim that drawing should be omitted altogether, since the requested style and the target both rely on fine linework.
