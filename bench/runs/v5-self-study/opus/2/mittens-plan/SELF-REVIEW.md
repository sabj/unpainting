# Mittens V5 try 1: self-review (written before reading FEEDBACK.md)

Looked at: `bench/feedback/compare.png` (target | painting), `stages.jpg`, `previous-plan/`, `previous-checkpoints.jsonl`.

## What works

- Composition and landmarks: the cat's head, ears and both eyes, and the man's head, eyes, brows, nose and hairline sit
  where the target has them. The two gazes read.
- Hair: the blue-grey/Payne's mass with broken edges and some white flecks is close to the target in hue and value and is
  the most "watercolour" passage on the sheet.
- The cat's warm sienna patches (brow, cheek, the fur behind the man's cheek) are the right hue family.
- The man's eyes and brows are firm and dark enough; the progression (pale washes, then mids, then the hair darks late)
  does go light to dark.

## What is wrong

1. **Far too much paint: the "light washes" sketch became a full-coverage patchwork.** The target is mostly white paper:
   the cat's muzzle, chin, chest and lower body, the man's forehead, nose bridge, cheeks and chin are paper or a
   whisper of tint. In my painting nearly every one of these got a beige/grey-brown wash, so the white of the paper only
   survives as left-over gaps between hard-edged patches. This is the biggest fault; it kills the "sketch" character.
2. **Hard-edged, nested, posterized patches.** Skin and cat fur are built from many small separately dried islands with
   hard rims (cheeks, forehead border, cat chest), reading like a colour-separated print rather than washes. Visible
   already at 10% strokes.
3. **A heavy dark-brown vertical band down the cat's neck / the man's left cheek.** In the target this is a soft mid
   sienna and the man's cheek edge is a light sepia; mine is the darkest warm value on the sheet and splits the picture.
4. **Square catchlights.** The four rectangular reserves read as little white boxes pasted on, especially in the cat's
   eyes. The cat's irises are brown in mine, pale green-yellow in the target.
5. **The drawing reads as traced lineart, and it is black.** The opening lines are near-black, uniform and include
   spurious contours (a long line down the cat/man boundary, stray lines across the cat's cheek and neck). The target's
   lines are fine, light, sepia/graphite and sparse; the cat's whiskers are the most important lines and are mostly
   missing in mine.
6. Man's lower face: the smile (mouth, lips) is lost in blotchy stubble patches; the chin and jaw are heavy.
7. The sheet has a cream cast overall; the target's paper is near white (the paper colour is probably fixed, but any
   pale tint over the background makes it worse).

## Where it went wrong

- At the plan: three mid layers plus `_dk22` and the painter's own segmentation assigned paint to almost every target
  cluster, including near-white ones; the plan relied on "the painter keeps near-paper lights" and that did not hold
  for the pale-tint clusters (cat chest, face planes). No reserves protected the big lights.
- First wash (10%): already covers the cat and the face with a flat beige; the white was lost here and could not come
  back.
- Mid passes (25%): the dark sienna neck band and the patchwork in the face formed here.
- Opening drawing: line weight and colour too strong for the requested "fine lines".

## Scores

- Finished painting: **4 / 10** (good likeness and placement, wrong value key and surface: heavy, patchy, not a light
  sketch).
- Process plausibility: **3 / 5** (light-to-dark order is believable and the hair arrives late as a person would; but the
  first wash is a flat full-coverage fill and the patch-by-patch mids look mechanical).

## After reading FEEDBACK.md: agreement and disagreement

Judges: composite 5.63; vision overall 3.67 (Kimi 3, GLM 4, Qwen 4); composition/values 6.0, edges/detail 3.33,
marks/texture 2.33; classic value 6.69, edge 9.09, marks 7.02. Progress judge: 2.0, 2.0, 2.5, 2.5, 5.0.

Agree:
- **Marks/texture is the weakest criterion (2.33).** It matches my faults 1, 2 and 5: hard-rimmed beige patches over the
  lights and dark traced lines are not the "fine lines and light washes" asked for.
- **Edges/detail low (3.33).** Agree for the surface (rims on every patch, the dark neck band, square catchlights), less so
  for the eyes and brows, which were firm.
- **Composition/values middling (6.0).** The placement is right; the value key is too dark and too covered. My 4/10 sits
  close to the vision panel's 3.7.
- **"The whole sheet at one faint value, as if faded in" (10% and 25%).** Agree with the cause: the first light band put
  one flat beige over nearly every non-white cell of both subjects at once. A painter's first wash also goes everywhere,
  but it leaves most of this sheet white and differs per region. Covering less in band 1 is the fix I control.
- **"Hair fills in as grey speckle clusters."** Agree for the 25 to 50% frames. The hair arrived as many small grey
  islands before it joined into a mass. The finished hair is good, but its middle stages look generated.

Disagree or discount:
- **100% at 5.0 / 5.** Too generous. The finished surface has the same patchwork tells as the 75% frame, which scored
  2.5. I read it as the judge's noise (about ±0.5) plus a finished picture just looking like a painting. It is not
  evidence that the late stages were fine.
- **"Cat half-formed at 10% while the figure is barely sketched."** This contradicts the same panel's "whole sheet at one
  faint value". At 10% both subjects have their first wash; the cat only looks further along because its sienna is more
  saturated than pale skin. I do not change the order of work for this note.
- **"Speckle or dotted trails instead of drawn lines."** Partly. The pen lines were continuous but too dark. The speckle
  is in the hair and touch-up passes, not in the drawing, so I keep a drawing and make it lighter and sparser.
- **Classic edge 9.09 / marks 7.02.** These pixel-level metrics reward exactly the hard-edged patchwork I want gone. I
  do not optimise for them and accept a possible drop.
- My process score (3/5) is above the judge's mean of 2.8 because the order of work (drawing, light band, mids, hair
  darks late, closing ink) is a real painter's order. The tells are in coverage and texture, not in the order.
