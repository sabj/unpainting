# Self-review: White Ships (Sargent), V5 try 1

Written from `bench/feedback/previous-painting.png`, `compare.png` and `stages.jpg` before reading `FEEDBACK.md`.

## What works

- Composition and landmarks are in place: the bow silhouette, the hawse hole, the two round blocks at the upper
  right, the diagonal orange spar, the masts at the left, the anchor and the rigging diagonals all sit where the target
  has them.
- The cream paper lights at the upper right between the rigging stay open; declaring the cream paper (`--paperrgb`)
  avoided the khaki flood.
- Focal darks land at about the right values (hawse hole, blocks, top fittings).
- The progression runs light to dark: a pale first tint at 10%, shapes at 25 to 50%, darks last.

## What is wrong

1. **The hull is a patchwork, not one luminous form.** In the target the bow is one airy pale cobalt plane with a
   strong cobalt left edge, a soft darker right side and pale streaks. Mine is a mottled set of grey and navy islands,
   each with its own hard rim. A heavy navy band runs down the right side of the hull, far darker and more saturated
   than the target's soft blue. This is the biggest fault.
2. **Too grey and muddy overall.** Sepia and sienna glazed over the blues kill Sargent's clean colour. The sea has
   lost its teal and yellow-green and reads grey-blue; the boat at the lower left is a dull slate mass.
3. **Mark character is posterised.** Every shape is a flat cut-out with a hard, frayed rim and pale halos between
   neighbours. Few shapes read as a brushstroke with a soft side. Rigging lines fray into shards.
4. **Straight seams.** A horizontal edge at about 62% height across the left half and faint straight diagonals
   through the hull, laid before `_ew8`.
5. **Lost small accents.** Most orange rivet dots below the hawse hole are gone, as are the hull's light streaks.
6. **Hull foot shadow too black and too hard**: a flat black wedge where the target has a soft dark blue-green.

## Where it went wrong

- Already at the light-wash stage (25% frame): the first wash was split into many separate pale shapes with rims,
  so the hull was never one connected wash. Everything later inherited that patchwork.
- At the mids (50%): the navy band on the hull's right and the seams appeared; four mid layers per pigment and hue
  group added too many separate glazes.
- At 75 to 100%: the warm pigments glazed over the blues greyed the colour, and the late dark gain made the foot black.

## My scores

- Finished painting: **3.5 / 10**. Readable composition and values, but patchy, grey and cut-out; it does not have
  the fresh, transparent look of a Sargent watercolour.
- Process plausibility: **2.5 / 5**. Light to dark, but 128 passes of flat shapes with rims look like a machine
  layering a segmentation, not a painter laying a few washes and then strokes.

## After reading FEEDBACK.md: where I agree and disagree with the judges

Scores: composite 5.08; vision overall 2.67 (composition and values 5.33, edges and detail 2.67, marks and texture
1.67); classic metrics 7.49; progression 3.0 / 3.0 / 3.5 / 4.0 / 5.0 at 10 to 100%.

Agree:
- **Marks and texture are the weakest part (1.67).** This matches my main complaint: flat cut-out shapes with rims,
  a patchwork hull, frayed rigging. It is the first thing to fix.
- **Edges and detail low (2.67).** Agreed: every edge is the same hard frayed rim, with no chosen soft side, and the
  rivets and streaks are lost.
- **Composition and values are the strongest (5.33).** Agreed; the layout and darks landed. Keep what placed them
  (cream paper colour, `_dk11`, touch-up gain 0.8).
- **Tell "flat evenly-toned slabs standing in for a form".** Agreed: that is the hull and the boat at the lower left.

Disagree or only partly agree:
- **"The whole sheet at one faint value, as if faded in" (10% and 25%).** Partly. A pale first wash over most of
  the sheet is what a watercolourist does, so a faint early sheet is not wrong in itself. What makes it read as a fade
  is that the wash has one greyish hue everywhere instead of pale blue on the hull, pale ochre on the masts and
  bare cream at the upper right. I will try to make the first stage more varied (fewer greying tubes) rather than
  remove the first wash.
- **"A finished image fading in via opacity" (one judge).** I disagree. The other judge described a correct
  light-to-dark sequence, and the stages show shapes being added, not a global fade. The two judges contradict each
  other, so I give this note little weight.
- **100% stage scored 5/5 for plausibility.** Too generous; the finished sheet still shows segmentation cut-outs.
  I don't treat the late stages as solved.
- **Classic edge 8.92.** Pixel metrics reward this kind of posterisation, and the technique notes warn against
  judging by pixel error. I ignore it.
