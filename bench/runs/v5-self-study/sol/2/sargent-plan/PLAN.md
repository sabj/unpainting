## Own review and critique decisions

The independent review is in `SELF-REVIEW.md`, written before reading the feedback. Try 1 carried the main composition but fragmented the luminous central bow into hard grey-blue islands, left conspicuous interior gaps, dulled the blue/green color and overexpanded the foreground shadow. The failure started with the underwash and was reinforced by the middle layers. I agree with the judges about weak mark character and detail. I reject the automated high edge score as an artistic verdict, the claim that all development was a mere opacity ramp, the isolated wipe diagnosis and the final process stage's perfect score. Their conflicting notes do not justify changing the basic light-to-dark order.

Changes from the previous final plan:

- Use W15 brush selection from the first stroke, rather than repairing W13 coverage after the foundation. Keep fitted geometry; broad passages need flowing bodies with connected contact, not stronger color over empty interiors.
- First light passages use loaded wet-on-dry washes with their own wet contacts allowed to merge. W15 supplies broad contact from the first stroke. The initial wet-in-wet choice failed its physical moisture gate and was replaced before the clean restart, as recorded below.
- One middle layer (`lay1`) replaces two. It has no second value-quantile silhouette, reducing nested rims and keeping the large hull readable as one value plane. Retain `hue` so the middle wash distinguishes warm, blue and green families, while the first foundation is not split into those families. No sky lanes: the bow curves, and this crop contains no open sky.
- Increase cool gain from 1.0 to 1.2 for the blue hull; reduce dark gain from 1.2 to 1.0. This raises the local-color body without inflating the dark reflection. Five transparent pans—ochre, burnt sienna, ultramarine, cerulean and sap green—replace six. Blue and burnt sienna supply mixed darks; remove the sepia pan that encouraged neutral grey patching. This is a material choice before painting, not a recoloring.
- Retain overlap-charge accounting (`cover 1`). One finishing correction round (`tp1`), gain .55, replaces two rounds at .9. Broad shape means may request a restrained positive-deficit glaze. The previous stronger corrective finish did not repair clipped boundaries and enlarged the foreground mass.
- Remove the two tiny rectangular extra reserves. The fitted connected paper-light mask already protects the broad right hull. Neither sample is a uniquely white glint in the target, and extra isolated bare spots are undesirable. No opaque paint or late fabricated lights.
- Keep cream sheet RGB 230/224/208, cold-press paper, empty opening/closing lines, drawing none, no extra sky pigment and no pavement/ground glaze. These choices worked in try 1.

## Paper gates and glaze crossings

The executor measures the whole actual stroke footprint and neighboring paper before every pigment or clean-water command. The foundation, middle and shadow passes must establish measured dryness before their first contact; only the current passage's own wet-edge overlap is allowed. A fresh watery accent never enters damp earlier paint. No reading is invented and no paper-state gate is bypassed. The rejected wet foundation is recorded below as a physical failure, and its clean restart uses the dry-state gate.

The lower blue shadow glaze crosses central bow, left boat and water color families. The upper rim glaze may cross hull and structural families. The right reflected shadow crosses only its fitted local support and keeps the sunlit hull out. Glazes cross descriptive boundaries to unify form, not to tile separate family boxes. Every glazing base is dry. Pigment strength is the fixed painter's calibrated transparent demand; water load and pigment strength are separate choices.
