# Independent review of try 1

Written after viewing `feedback/compare.png` and `feedback/stages.jpg`, before opening the external feedback.

The large composition is recognizable: the central blue-white bow, the pale right ship, the dark upper-left rail, the anchor at lower left, and the warm diagonal rope survive. The sheet remains light, and the progression adds darks late rather than beginning with a finished dark image. There is no intrusive ink tracing.

The central hull is too fragmented. White holes and hard nested grey-blue islands replace the target's broad, luminous blue plane. The target has brush texture inside connected washes; the result has contours around many little value steps. Several boundaries look like cut paper. The darkest foreground shape becomes a swollen blue-grey mound, losing the anchor and keel's purposeful silhouettes. The anchor flukes are broad, blunt and orange rather than a few pale warm planes with dark structural turns. The orange diagonal rope loses the target's thin patterned rhythm. The right hull is too empty, with excessively sharp broken grey shapes beside it, and the sea loses the target's coherent turquoise strip and horizontal movement. Overall the mixtures are duller and greyer than the target.

The failure begins in the first light wash: even the 10% frame already has separately bounded patches rather than one connected hull underwash. It becomes unmistakable at 25–50%, when the same patches darken independently and internal paper holes remain. The 75–100% darks magnify the segmentation and pool across the foreground. Late accents cannot repair a fragmented base. The light-to-dark sequence is plausible, but the simultaneous development of many scattered patches and repeated contouring does not resemble economical brush passages.

My finished-painting score is **4.8/10**. My process-plausibility score is **3.2/5**: credible transparent layering and late darks, weakened by patchwise construction and too little connected wet paint.

For try 2 I want a more continuous blue hull, a clearer separation between blue hull shadow and dark foreground accents, and stronger cool-blue/turquoise colour. I will retain the fitted geometry and empty drawing layers. I will investigate whether fewer mid layers, fuller wash contact and a smaller, cooler palette can improve the first failing layer without making the focal hard edges blurry.

## After reading the external feedback

I agree with the modest composition score and the low edge/detail and mark/texture assessments. The boats' arrangement survives, but the clipped patches and contour rims are readily visible. I disagree with the high automated edge score (8.73): locating boundaries near the reference does not make their watercolor character convincing. I also do not adopt the final process stage's 5/5; the finished surface still has a conspicuous patchwise tell.

The early-stage complaint about a uniformly faint sheet is useful: the first passage should read as a deliberately chosen underwash, followed by local-color masses and later focal accents. However, I reject the categorical claim that this is just an opacity ramp. The stages show new dark shapes and different developments in different areas, and the other process judge explicitly sees a watercolor sequence. The one report of a travelling wipe is not a reliable diagnosis from this strip. My independent early coverage/fragmentation diagnosis is more concrete, and the previous plan's own observations support it. I will change the physical foundation and simplify the middle construction, not try to optimize for a noisy process number.
