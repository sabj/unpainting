# Independent review, before reading FEEDBACK.md

I inspected compare.png and stages.jpg before opening the external feedback.

The principal roof locations and a few warm wall fragments survive, and the distant upper-right blue is in roughly the right place. The opening structure is recognisable when the sheet is pale. These are useful anchors to retain.

The finished painting loses the subject's main value structure. The reference has distinct olive crowns, narrow dark cypresses, pale terraced stone on the left, and a legible cluster of terracotta roofs and warm walls on the right. The painting instead joins almost all vegetation into one very dark green blanket. It buries the drawing, flattens depth, and makes the buildings look like disconnected pale cutouts. Broad diagonal overlapping brush lanes dominate the scene. White gaps are too abrupt and isolated; they do not read as sunlit paths or thoughtfully reserved paper. The roofs need more red-brown colour and the walls need clearer planes. The scene needs a much lighter wash and a stronger, selective structural drawing to meet the requested urban-sketch style.

The failure starts between the 25% and 50% progress frames. The 25% sheet is broadly yellow rather than a differentiated pale olive/stone underwash. By 50%, the foliage has jumped to a near-black continuous field. Subsequent frames barely develop the image. The later dark passages compound an earlier value/load mistake; closing ink cannot repair it. The first drawing also needs more useful contrast, especially the roofs, cypresses and foreground trunk, without adding traced leaf outlines.

My scores: finished painting **2/10**; plausible human painting progression **2/5**. Physical strokes are visible and the drawing precedes paint, but the huge dark jump, repeated diagonal lanes and static second half do not resemble deliberate pen plus light wash.

For try 2 I would preserve geometry and architectural anchors, reduce overlapping foliage layers and dark loading, distinguish broad light washes from selective shadow groups, and make pen structure readable. I would judge checkpoints by their intended value stage and intervene before the pale underwash becomes an indiscriminate dark mass.

## Response after reading external feedback

I agree with the low vision scores and the named problems: flat slabs, scalloped white holes, repeated directional bands, and little development after halfway. My independent review identified the same dark jump and loss of form. Their progression mean of 2.1 is close to my 2/5. I reject the classic mark score of 8.35 and the implication of the edge score of 7.13: the marks and edges visible in the painting do not articulate crowns, paths or walls. Strong material boundaries are not necessarily the right edges. I also would not call the sequence an actual digital reveal: strokes are visibly deposited, but their repetitive scheduling creates that impression. The panel is incomplete, so its absent composite/landmark values are not evidence either way.

The prior plan and checkpoint history refine my diagnosis. Most of the failing light-color work was painted without overlap load normalization; coverage normalization arrived only at pass 19, after the forest was already dark. The urban style was supplied an ink-and-wash value floor, and the early broad shaped sweeps were replaced after paint had landed. Try 2 must start with the corrected geometry and load accounting. In addition, this crop has no exposed paper: inferred near-paper masks confuse bright yellow stone with paper and explain the conspicuous cutout holes. Keep only tiny deliberate glints and tint the broad lit planes.
