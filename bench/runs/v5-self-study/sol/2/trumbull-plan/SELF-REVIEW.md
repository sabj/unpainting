# Independent review before reading FEEDBACK.md

Reviewed the target beside previous-painting.png and stages.jpg on 2026-10-09, using the watercolor-technique value, light, edge, landmark, mark and progression criteria. These are my qualitative scores, not a calibrated judge's scores.

The composition and principal silhouettes work: central gable, two tall chimneys, overlapping roof slopes, paired Gothic windows, annex and tree are recognizable and correctly placed. The front wall is lighter than the receding left wall, the window masses are dark, and the early stages are appropriately pale. Coverage is substantially better than the diagonal gaps described in the previous plan. The sparse opening is a credible structural lay-in.

The finished building loses the reference's luminous golden stone. The central face is too uniformly beige and its lit corner and pale arch surrounds do not separate enough from the broad facade. Side walls and chimney tops have excessive scattered grey-brown islands, making stone read as camouflage rather than masonry. The facade still shows regular parallel contact bands. This began in the broad foundation and became more conspicuous in the middle-value and shadow stages; the 50% frame already contains patchy side planes before the dark openings arrive. More closing ink cannot repair that wash character.

The sky is conspicuously wrong: large white cutout clouds have hard torn edges, where the reference is predominantly a soft pale blue clouded wash. This is already present at 25% and comes from the early sky support and its connected-light exclusions, not from the closing drawing. The roofs also have too many bright flecks.

Fine architecture is under-described. The focal paired arches have the right position but their tracery, mullions, inner ribs and light stone piers largely collapse into dark filled holes. The circular gable crest is missing. The chimney flues need selective structural divisions. Some long thin vertical lines descend through light facade planes without a visible junction in the reference. These are drawing selection faults, visible in the opening or insufficiently resolved at closing, rather than a reason for stronger ink everywhere.

The tree and lower-left planting are disconnected yellow/green and dark flecks, with little coherent canopy body. Tiny white holes inside dark windows draw attention away from the main arches. The annex roof is overly stark against its wall. These develop late, with the recess, shadow and texture passages.

Finished painting: **6.0/10**. It is a coherent recognizable architectural watercolor, with good placement but weak light, cloudy sky, masonry texture and focal detail.

Plausibility of painting process: **3.0/5**. The pale foundation, dry value development and late recesses follow a believable order. The systematic stripes, simultaneous dispersed fragmentation over several walls, and near-uniform late texture do not look like a person choosing connected planes and a few structural accents. The good light-to-dark order should be preserved while the marks and detail selection change.

## Comparison with external critique

The external composite is 5.33; its vision panel rates marks/texture 2.0 and edges/detail 2.67. I agree with the low texture assessment: the stripes, grey camouflage and white cloud cutouts are visible defects. I also agree that the facade is initially too flat and the middle stage deposits scattered texture over too much of the sheet at once. The progression mean 2.8 is close to my 3.0.

I do not accept the classic edge score 9.5 as artistic evidence: sharp outlines alone do not recover the arch tracery or make the clouds appropriate. Conversely the vision overall 3.0 seems harsher than the painting warrants for composition and broad value order; the principal masses and landmarks are recognizable and well placed. One progression judge describes a credible human workflow, and that agrees with the actual pale foundation and late recesses. The other calls it opacity-layer simulation: I agree with its observation of flatness and simultaneous sharpening, but not with rejecting the whole light-to-dark order. Changing to dark-first or adding random marks would not address the visible causes. I reject chasing the numeric classic marks score 7.16, which conflicts with the visible repetitive bands and fragmented masonry.

Try 2 therefore preserves fitted shapes and the light-to-dark order, changes physical broad-brush contact, reduces stone hints and repeated middle layering, tightens the automatic light selection, and rebuilds the selective drawing around the missing architectural landmarks. Numerical judges do not override the region-by-region visual acceptance checks.
