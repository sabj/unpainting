# Mittens V5 try 1: independent review

Written after viewing `feedback/compare.png` and `feedback/stages.jpg`, before opening the external feedback.

The composition and likeness survive: the cat's large upward-looking eyes, tall ears, white chest and orange flank are placed correctly, as are the man's hairline, brows and smile. The sheet stays light and the warm/cool distinction between orange coat and dark hair is useful. The forehead and chest remain open paper, which suits the requested portrait sketch.

The painting is much more like a collection of flat cut-out shapes than the target's fine-line watercolor sketch. The warm areas have too many separately edged islands and too little translucent, connected wash. The hair has chunky blue/brown tiles rather than a coherent charcoal mass with a few curl gestures. The cat loses whiskers, fine muzzle contours and the small directional marks that explain fur. Its large eye has a heavy brown surround and a weak, warm iris; the target's greenish iris and restrained upper lid would make the gaze clearer. The man's lower lids and sockets are too strongly outlined and angular, and the moustache and mouth have fragmented shapes instead of a light, connected gesture. Both subjects need finer drawing with substantially less weight than the present paint edges.

The earliest visible 10% frame already establishes nearly all the small white holes and angular boundaries. That is where the disconnected wash character starts. The 25% frame puts in strong eye and hair darks; later stages mostly reinforce those same islands. Thus the late stage has little sense of selecting just a few accents after a broad underpainting. Closing drawing fails to supply the whiskers, gentle face lines and curl gestures that the reference needs. A late ink fill would not solve the early wash problem.

My finished-painting score: **5.5/10**. My plausibility-of-painting score: **2.5/5**. It proceeds broadly from pale to dark, but the repeated island geometry and abrupt local darks do not resemble a person's economical wash-and-line progression.

## External feedback comparison

The finished composite of 5.42 is close to my own 5.5, although the low visual scores (3–4) and high automatic edge score (8.78) show why I will not optimize the numerical edge score. An edge can be accurately located and still have the wrong watercolor character. I agree with the low marks/texture verdict and the complaint about flat slabs.

I agree that the first frames contain too much of the final small-shape geometry, and that global darkening does not make an economical painting sequence. The early stage scores of 1.5 and 2 are harsher than my whole-process score but identify the same earliest failure. I will reduce the paint ladder, strengthen the separate drawing stage and keep final accents local.

I partly reject “everything sharpening at once late”: the visibly hard island boundaries are already in the first frame, so this is not mainly a late sharpening problem. The change has to start at the first wash and its shape grouping. I also reject the final plausibility score of 5 as an artistic verdict: the final sheet still has conspicuous tiled wash and hair shapes. The phrase “whiskers-last” describes a plausible habit, but the actual previous finished painting scarcely shows the fine whiskers; it is not evidence that their execution succeeded. I will judge the executed lines and wash edges in the self-views.
