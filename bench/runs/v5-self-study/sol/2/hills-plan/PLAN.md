# Hills, V5 try 2 — loose ink and wash

Fixed target: 306 × 242 pixels; painting: 1224 × 968 cells on 158.048 × 124.992 mm cold-press paper. V096 fixed executor, seed 0, gentle target guard. This plan uses the watercolor-technique reference and the painting-planner procedure. No painter code or physics is changed.

## Reference and value intention

The focal point is the dark forked tree at the central cream-road bend, about (106,143) in target pixels. The path links the small upper clearing, left woodland, central bend and bright foreground slope. No sky is present. Four values organize the picture: warm cream road; bright yellow-green meadow; muted olive/blue-grey crown tops; connected deep cool-green woodland interiors. The path is light paint, not a bare-paper ribbon. The foreground slope must remain luminous and quieter than the adjoining trees.

## Changes from try 1 and feedback decisions

My independent review is in SELF-REVIEW.md, written before reading FEEDBACK.md. I scored the previous painting 5.0/10 and its process 3.0/5. I accept the critique's flat early slabs, diagonal sweep, weak edges/detail, and procedural patch texture. I reject its late 5/5 process ratings and the high classic edge/mark scores as evidence of convincing watercolor. I do not accept more dense ink or late texture as a remedy.

1. Classify the full-bleed photographic crop correctly before the first wash. Use neutral paper with no relative-paper subtraction and disable broad inferred-paper detection. The cream road is not the sheet. Automatic genuinely neutral near-white target flecks remain eligible for paper protection; no rectangular reserves are added.
2. Replace the ochre/sap-green palette with raw sienna, hansa yellow, French ultramarine, cerulean, sepia and phthalo green. Hansa plus green can keep the meadow yellow-green without an ochre-heavy base; dilute raw sienna carries the warm road. Cool blue/green and sepia mix the woodland. These are physical pans, not export adjustments.
3. Keep forest ink-and-wash and meadow/road precise-watercolor value planes, with target-fitted irregular segmentation. Set woodland style gain to .85 for a lighter base, and cool/dark gain to 1 rather than 1.1/1.3. The final dark should explain the connected shade, not form black holes. Lower the connected shadow-support threshold to .32 and raise the fragment floor to 300 native cells, simplifying very small patches while retaining trees.
4. Use one connected meadow middle layer rather than two nested layers. The reference asks for a clear sloping ground plane, not contour islands. Its own fitted terrain shadow follows after drying. Keep first-wash styles separate (skysplit=0), so warm bright grass is not averaged with grey crown tops.
5. Use W15 brush choice for meadow/road value planes and the existing fitted connected-plane paths for woodland. Broad contact follows the actual irregular plane, with a flowing watery load and finite-load joins feathered over 1.5 mm. The initial 6 mm lane / 20 mm stroke plan produced a regular grid in its first physical wash, so that splitter is removed at pass 1 before the meadow and remaining pigments. No reach extension, splatter, stipple, added texture pass or disabled physical effect is requested. Later transparent foundation pigments should unify the pale first sienna layer; its existing marks remain on the paper.
6. Strengthen the structural drawing while keeping it sparse: 16 opening gestures, central forks at .48 pressure, other trunks at about .32–.34, two short broken road turns at .27. Black-ink material is explicit. Closing lines are target-supported, with useful .65 focal pressure and .46 elsewhere, rather than the previous almost invisible drawing. The value/edge selection remains active. Ink never fills woodland darkness.

## Region choices and sequence

Every stroke requires a measured paper-state check over its full footprint and neighboring cells by the fixed executor. Dry glazes require fully dry underpaint; wait/dry/work elsewhere if it is not ready. Wet overlap within a connected wash is intentional. Moisture readings are not inferred from elapsed time. Where the fixed tree-interior physical schedule prepares a local wet passage, clean water precedes a medium charge at measured sheen; that local soft shadow is justified by merging crown interiors. Keep road edges, focal forks and meadow boundaries dry. There is no whole-sheet wet blur.

## Lights, wet passages and glazes

The path, meadow sunlit planes and crown tops are pale painted planes. Tiny truly neutral target lights can remain bare paper; foliage openings are negative-painted gaps. No broad masking, rectangle, invented glint or lift is needed. The crop is full bleed, so an artificial white vignette would contradict its composition. Small tooth flecks and a few broken lights are enough.

The light woodland base connects crowns within their fitted mass, leaving warm terrain and road separate. Meadow mixes stay distinct from that foundation. Dry shadow groups cross the central tree belt's blue/green/olive colors and trunks, the focal tree into its neighbors, and the right near-wood group. Meadow turning shade crosses its green/yellow planes but does not cover the cream road. These are medium-to-strong transparent glazes over fully dry paint, with dry barriers at the focal branches. Any locally prepared soft charge in a tree interior is the sole named departure from dry layering and is required to show its measured moisture gate.

Opening black ink establishes sparse forks, tree scale and the two short road turns on dry paper. Check the opening image before washing. Closing black ink follows full drying; restore the focal fork and a few supported dark spans, with tapered pressure. No universal crown outline, ruled hatch, or ink fill is allowed. Audit the actual pigment and executed gestures rather than trusting the file alone.
