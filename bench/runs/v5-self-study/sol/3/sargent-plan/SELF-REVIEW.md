# Independent review of try 2

Written after viewing `compare.png` and `stages.jpg`, before reading the judges' feedback.

The composition is recognisable: the looming blue hull occupies the right amount of space, the two upper-right openings and the central dark fitting are in place, and the bottom triangular shadow gives the ship weight. The pale-to-dark sequence is believable at the broad level. No ink outline was needed to make the large forms read.

The main failure is the organisation of the light blue hull. It becomes a collection of jagged, flat grey-blue patches and cream gaps rather than one luminous, blue, curved plane. The large pale vertical gap through its centre is too dominant. The source has varied paint, but its variations sit inside a continuous form. Here the patch boundaries compete with that form. The early warm wash is already a little muddy; by the 25% stage the separate colour islands and exaggerated cream gaps are established, and the later darks only reinforce them.

The right water is beige and olive, losing the target's fresh turquoise. The blue hull is also insufficiently clear and cool. The warm anchor in the middle-left has become a large brown patch with a round dark dot; its curved bar and the long diagonal rigging have largely disappeared. The fine orange marks along the hull edge are a useful likeness cue but have thickened and merged. The lower-left flecks retain some liveliness, yet the painting lacks the source's long, confident strokes. The final darks give useful depth, but the top diagonal and bottom keel look filled and soft instead of varied in contact and edge.

Finished painting: **5.2 / 10**. Material and broad composition are convincing enough; the connected value planes, colour relationships and key rigging gestures are weak.

Plausibility of painting process: **3.4 / 5**. The increasingly dark stages make sense, but successive stages add many unrelated jagged patches across the whole page. The hull should become a coherent pale blue wash early, with a few larger shadows and selective fittings following it.

For try 3 I want a connected cool base, fewer disconnected mid-value fills, fresh blue-green water, smaller selected warm accents, and dry, firm dark accents. I will inspect the early wash before proceeding to mid-values. Any setting change must correspond to an option the fixed painter actually reads.

## Response to the external critique

The judges' low marks/texture and detail scores agree with my observation of jagged patchwork and lost rigging. The higher composition/value assessment also agrees: the ship's placement and major darks survive. I do not accept the very high classic edge score as evidence that the edges are artistically successful; there are many strong local edges, but too many are meaningless internal patch rims.

I agree that the 10% stage is too evenly faint across the page. A first wash should commit to a few connected planes, with a visibly cooler hull and untouched lights. I partly disagree with the claim that the entire sequence is a low-opacity reveal: the central fitting, top diagonal and keel dark visibly arrive late, which is reasonable watercolor order. Nevertheless the fixed global ladder can produce a uniform build, so I will use fewer mid layers and inspect the early passes for actual local wash development.

I retain my independent scores rather than replacing them with the panel's 5.72 composite or 2.8 process mean. The panel's broad agreement on weak purposeful marks is more useful than its noisy absolute numbers. I reject spatter or more linework as a response: this crop calls for connected washes, selective paint edges, and pure watercolor without ink.
