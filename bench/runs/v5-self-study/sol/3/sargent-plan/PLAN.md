## Review and changes

Independent review and the response to the judges are in `SELF-REVIEW.md`. Try 2 retained composition and the large dark landmarks, but jagged grey-blue patch rims destroyed the luminous bow, its centre stayed too pale, the water dulled to olive/beige and the rigging gestures weakened. The problem began in the foundation and middle planes, before the strong final correction.

Keep one connected middle layer (`lay1`) with three broad local-color groups (`hue`), rather than separate fills for six fine color clusters or extra nested value silhouettes. The single layer carries all middle demand. Its first foundation remains shared across groups, and the blue pigment gives that foundation its cool body. The proposed `lay0/onedom` alternative was rejected at the CPU preview: each pan joined nearly the entire sheet and its broad parallel contacts crossed unrelated forms. Connected passages must still respect the subject's local colors.

Add `softs`: selected clean damp brush arcs, radius .7 mm, soften only weak, source-supported turning boundaries. Hard-edge overlaps are excluded. This is physical edge handling for the bow's soft internal turns and reflected water, with no global image blur. Keep the main hull curve, porthole rims, spars and anchor fittings hard.

Keep W15's brush selection and fitted target geometry. Cool gain remains 1.2. Middle gain becomes 1.2, dark gain 1.2 rather than 1.0; these calibrated transparent loads should carry the main blue body and dark fittings before any correction. Move 30% of eligible small dark demand to late accents (`acc3`). `dp70` builds only the very darkest target-supported fitting/rim cores in two final dry accents, leaving broad light and middle planes alone. No expanded dark support is requested.

Palette: ochre, burnt sienna, French ultramarine, cerulean, phthalo green. Replace yellow-biased sap green with blue-shade phthalo green for the turquoise water; mixed blues/sienna supply neutral darks. Keep cream paper RGB 230/224/208. No separate sky pigment, ground glaze, ink, graphite, opaque white or spatter. Keep overlap-charge accounting (`cover 1`). One optional finishing round is limited to half of positive broad shape-mean deficits, twelve families smoothed over 2 mm; inspect before executing it. This replaces try 2's strong .85 correction over eight families. `nowow 1` keeps that legacy finishing route on a fully dry glazed base.

I accept the critique of weak purposeful marks and an evenly ghosted first stage. I reject the high automatic edge score as proof of good edges, the idea that every stage is merely opacity reveal, and added spatter or linework as a remedy. The rigid global ladder constrains process order; the proposed changes should improve the actual paint shapes, not manufacture progress frames.

## Regional technique and order

Every family in `config.json` explicitly chooses wet on dry. Bounds only label overlapping target-derived color/value geometry; they never act as painted rectangles. All families use precise-watercolor, avoiding style-box seams. Paper moisture is measured over the full stroke footprint and its neighbors before every mark. The base must be fully dry; current-passage wet-edge overlap may merge. If the physical paper gate fails, wait/dry or stop: do not disable it or invent readings. Full drying separates foundation, middle paint, shadow glazes and finishing paint.

At native density one cell is .2333 mm; one source pixel is four cells (.9333 mm). Four cells across is the starting minimum wash body. Bow planes are hundreds of cells wide. Spar/rope and anchor-arm bodies are typically 8–24 cells (1.9–5.6 mm), porthole bodies 48–88 cells, essential rims 8–16 cells. Details below four cells are simplified through the fitted paint plane; the no-ink instruction takes priority over the guide's usual fine-line remedy. No density change or hand-traced replacement geometry is requested.

W15 kit widths are 1.2, 3, 5, 7, 25.4 and 38 mm; native radii 2.57, 6.43, 10.71, 15, 54.43 and 81.43 cells. The actual chosen radius/support ratio is recorded by compilation. Narrow bodies use round or rigger contact; broad planes use belly or flat-side contact. Native sheet inspection is required for narrow fittings.
