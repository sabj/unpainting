## Reference, focal point and values

The pale winding track links the upper clearing to the large lower yellow-green slope. The forked tree beside the lower central bend, near (105,143), is the focal point. The tall cool trees in the middle-right belt supply a second landmark. There is no sky. Five values organize the painting: warm cream road; luminous yellow-green meadow; light olive crown tops; connected middle blue-green woodland shade; small deepest cool interiors and black branch junctions. The lights are pale paint, with only tiny neutral flecks of bare paper. This is a full-bleed crop, so the margin is painted.

## Changes and critique decisions

`SELF-REVIEW.md` records the independent image review and then comparison with the external judges. My initial finished score was 4.5/10 and process 3.5/5, revised to 3.0/5 after considering the visible early grid. I accept the weak woodland grouping, patch/dot distribution, mustard meadow, excessive road whiteness, and global progression tells. I reject the high classic edge/mark scores as proof of good painting and the claim that any pale first wash is inherently a filter reveal. I also reject dense all-over ink or a late texture dump as a cure.

1. The previous woodland used ink-and-wash's two-value paint ladder: a 42% foundation and 58% final shade, with no middle band. That split produced a light grey matrix and black islands. Retain the overall ink-and-wash intention and drawing, but explicitly select precise-watercolor painted planes in every family, including the woods. The reference needs crown tops, middle shadow sides and small deep interiors. The fixed precise value ladder is value-dependent, rather than a constant per-style share.
2. Final middle schedule: use the native local-color middle planes (lay0, the default middle-domain setting) for all families. The main middle band carries 88% of the middle demand and a smaller second band carries 12%, dried between. These are fitted local-color regions, followed by cross-region final shade. The initial two quantile layers grouped broad hue families, averaging much of the wooded crown color with the meadow. The foundation inspection showed why the remaining middle work must follow more specific local-color planes. No artificial region rectangle is a painted boundary: the executor fits irregular regions to the reference, and every family selects the same painted-plane style.
3. Keep native W15 brush choice and fitted connected paths from the beginning. Do not request the stroke-lane splitter that left a grid in the prior first wash. Feather finite-load joins by 1.5 mm. The fragment floor is 220 cells, rather than 300, to retain useful irregular tree forms without promoting leaf speckle. Final shadow strength is .9 rather than 1.0; its threshold is .36, with the precise ladder's gradual shadow share. This changes the distribution before painting, not a late attempted repair.
4. Palette, in painting order: hansa yellow, sap green, French ultramarine, cerulean, raw sienna, sepia. Sap green is a physical yellow/green mixture that offers the hillside a cleaner green than the previous raw-sienna-led box. Hansa and green arrive before the warm road tint. Cool blues and sepia carry woodland; dilute raw sienna carries the road and warmer crown tops. Paper is neutral cream-white with broad inferred-paper detection disabled, since the road is painted ground, not the sheet.
5. Opening drawing: 20 sparse tapering tree gestures, no road rails, stronger at the central fork. Closing: 57 selected tree/fork/short shadow gestures; simplify the previous staircase traces, omit a long looping crown contour, remove duplicate fork strokes, and add several supported branching gestures. Material is explicitly black-ink. Opening pressure is capped at .58, closing at .85; nonfocal lines are lighter. Ink explains narrow structure, never fills a dark canopy.
6. Permit only one modest automatic touch-up round (.4 gain) rather than two .6 rounds, to reduce repetitive late patch accumulation. This is a fixed painter correction pass, not a substitute for the authored middle layers.

## Region techniques

For every mark the fixed executor must check measured moisture over the whole footprint and neighbors. Begin each layer on fully dry paper; own-pass overlaps may remain wet and join. Dry glazes require dry underpaint. Wait/dry/work elsewhere if the state is wrong; never infer a moisture reading from a timer. The planned wash load is watery (.62 water) with light to medium pigment; shadow glazes use controlled medium load (.45 water), and ink uses the dry pen gate. No whole-sheet wet-in-wet or added blur is requested.

## Lights, connections and order

The cream road, sunlit slopes and lit crown tops are pale painted planes. Small foliage openings are negative-painted gaps. Tiny neutral near-white target flecks may remain paper; no rectangular reserve, invented glint or lift is added. A reserve crossing the route would sever the compositional link, so the road remains eligible for every appropriate pale wash.

Order: sparse opening tree ink on dry paper; large light local-color passages with intentional wet joins; full dry; connected middle planes; full dry; darker middle restatement on its fitted support; full dry; connected shading glazes across crown colors and trunks; full dry; small corrections; selective closing black ink. Shadow glazes cross the central tree belt's olive/blue/green boundaries, focal tree into neighboring wood, and the right near group. Meadow terrain shade crosses yellow/green local colors but holds off the pale road. No departure from light-to-dark is planned. Paper-tooth edge breakup belongs to existing physical contact, not an added dot texture layer.
