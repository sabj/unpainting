# Hills V5 try 3: independent review of try 2

Written after viewing `feedback/compare.png` and `feedback/stages.jpg`, before opening the external feedback.

The winding pale road and the large division between woodland and open hills remain recognizable. The first stage has connected pale masses, and later stages add stronger values in a credible broad sequence. There is some transparent wash and uneven pigment deposition. The drawing does not overwhelm the landscape.

The main failure is the woodland's value structure. The reference has connected deep blue-green shadow masses, lighter crown tops, and clear separation between overlapping trees. The painting has a pale grey-green matrix punctured by repeated near-black islands. Those islands read as holes or camouflage rather than trees. The mid-values do not sufficiently connect them into shadow sides. The important trees beside the road have lost their trunks, branch gestures, and crown hierarchy.

The open hillside is too ochre and flat. The reference is luminous yellow-green, with cooler turning planes and sparse oblique marks. The road is too close to bare white, narrower and less continuous in places, and its warm local colour is largely missing. It should remain the lightest connected route while still being a pale painted plane. The road/tree junction in the lower middle should be the focal point; the rendered sheet has similarly sharp small blobs everywhere instead.

The error starts between the 10% and 25% frames: the woodland becomes many separate mid-value patches while the meadow fills with mustard colour. By 50%, dark accents are already isolated and excessively strong relative to their surrounding midtone. From 50% to 100% there is little consequential change; the late marks do not unify shadows or restore the landform. This is not primarily a blur failure. Much of the paint is sharp enough, but the edges describe too many equal islands.

My finished-painting score: **4.5 / 10**. My process-plausibility score: **3.5 / 5**. The light-to-dark sequence is credible, but the accumulation of equal foliage patches and near-static final half looks mechanically segmented. I would preserve the connected first wash, improve green pigment and middle shadow coverage, reduce the darkest jump, and concentrate drawing around the road-side trees.

## External critique comparison

I agree with the weak composition/value score and the very low visual mark/texture scores: the woodland really does resemble segmented camouflage, and the meadow lacks its green slope. I agree with the warnings about evenly scattered dark foliage, dotted detail, and insufficiently regional development. The early grid is visible and is a real failure, even though it is mostly covered later.

I disagree with treating the sequence as merely a static image uncovered by a filter. The road is held out, separate warm and cool masses develop, and dark glazes arrive later. Those are actual light-to-dark painting decisions. Nonetheless, the globally similar treatment and small final-half changes explain why it gives that impression. My 3.5/5 process score was too generous to the grid and global accumulation; after considering those specific tells I would use **3.0/5**, rather than accept their 2.0 mean uncritically. A thin first wash alone is not evidence of a dissolve.

I reject the classic edge and mark scores of 8.52 and 8.64 as artistic evidence: sharp boundaries around repeated dark islands do not explain the trees, and correct material texture does not make their distribution purposeful. I also reject adding a late texture dump or heavy contour everywhere as a remedy. The change must begin with the woodland value ladder and connected middle planes, before accents.
