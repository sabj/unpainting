## Reading the reference

The focal point is the spreading tree beside the central path bend, around source (134,146). Three warm, winding path fragments thread through bright grassy hills and interlocking cool woodland. There is no sky. Five values: warm light path (roughly 90% lightness); luminous yellow-green sunlit grass (70–82%); olive ground turns and lit grey-green crowns (50–65%); connected cool tree shadows (30–45%); small deep blue-sepia hollows and stems (15–25%). The grass must remain lighter and more yellow than the trees. The path is a pale painted plane, with only tiny rim flecks left as paper.

1. Sparse light sepia opening drawing on dry paper. The central fork and broken path shoulders anchor the sheet; do not draw a net around the crowns.
2. Pale yellow/green/earth and then cool foundations, grouped into connected reference-derived masses. Their purpose is to establish the light family, not complete every meadow at its final value.
3. Full measured dry. Local-colour middle planes distinguish grass from crowns. Let neighbouring strokes of the same passage join while wet; each new passage uses the fixed paper-state gate.
4. Full measured dry. Small second middle-value share restores tone without four nested contour layers.
5. Full measured dry. Two complementary late glazes share a connected shadow support through the central forest and left foreground trees, across family boundaries and through the cast shade that falls on grass. Lights stay out because the support is fitted to the target's broad dark shapes, not to the family boxes.
6. Review before normal sparse touch-ups and closing drawing; medium 0.45-water shadows carry calibrated pigment, dark gain 1.1. Finish only a few branch/path junctions in sepia on dry paint.

The fixed executor must inspect measured moisture across each whole stroke footprint and its neighbours, wait/dry or rewet if required, and permit only its planned same-passage overlap.
