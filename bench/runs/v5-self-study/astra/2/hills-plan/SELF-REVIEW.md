# Hills V5, try 2: independent review

Written after viewing `feedback/compare.png` and `feedback/stages.jpg`, before reading the judges' feedback.

The winding path and the placement of the main wooded ridges are recognizable. The cool blue-green tree shadows contrast with warmer open ground, and a few branching gestures give the trees structure. The stages visibly move from light masses to darker vegetation.

The principal failure is the light/value and colour design. The target has luminous yellow-green grass, warm cream paths and layered grey-green crowns with quite deep shadows. The painting starts with a nearly continuous dull khaki field and never recovers its luminosity. The path is harsh white rather than warm pale earth. Large accidental white holes in the upper-right hillside and along the right and bottom margins break the connected landscape. The grass receives ochre islands rather than a fresh, flowing green wash. Tree masses turn into flat interlocking patches, with dark navy-black lumps and insufficient intermediate crown modelling. Ink is sparse but some isolated branches sit over indistinct washes instead of explaining convincing crowns.

The first visible 10% stage already contains the decisive errors: flat grey-khaki grass, an over-white path, and missing coverage toward the upper-right and lower edge. At 25%, small contours accumulate without restoring the big field planes. The 50% stage adds stronger ochre patches and dark clumps, increasing contrast within an already wrong base. The last half mostly increases density. These are planning and first-wash problems, not missing finishing detail.

For this small landscape I would preserve the target-derived geometry, use a grass palette that can make the target's clear yellow-green, tone the paths, and make the broad coverage continuous. I would keep dry, connected foliage shadows and only a small amount of structural ink. I would reduce repeated mid-value patching and avoid solving pale regions by adding more black.

My own finished-painting score: **4.5/10**. My own painting-process plausibility score: **3/5**. The light-to-dark order is credible, but the uniformly mapped first layer, expanding patch mosaic, and abrupt late dark islands look more like a segmented colouring process than a person choosing linked washes and selected accents.

These are subjective visual judgments, not calibrated external judge scores.

## Response to the external critique

I agree with the judges' flat olive base, evenly faded first-stage values and late texture/dark-patch accumulation. These were independently visible in the 10%, 25% and 50% frames. I also agree that mark character is the weakest finished-image criterion. The early stage scores of 1.5–2 are harsher than my aggregate 3/5, but describe a real problem.

I do not accept the claim that the process is literally diffusion or image uncovering: the sequence also contains legitimate separate wash and shadow operations. The useful diagnosis is the nearly uniform strength of the first layer and the colour domains it averages, not the asserted mechanism. Likewise the classic edge score of 8.72 and marks score of 7.01 do not match the visible hard rectangles, white holes and clumps; numerical edge agreement is not good watercolor. The finished score near 4.55 is close to my own, while the vision-only 2/10 gives too little credit to the retained path composition. I will change the early value schedule, colour grouping and physical brush choice rather than chase either sub-score.
