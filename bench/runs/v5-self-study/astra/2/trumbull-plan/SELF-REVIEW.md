# Trumbull V5 try 1: independent review before external feedback

Reviewed the target, previous comparison and five progress frames before opening
FEEDBACK.md. Scores are my visual judgments, not calibrated external scores.

Finished painting: **6.0 / 10**. Plausibility of painting process: **3.0 / 5**.

The principal silhouette, two chimneys, focal gable, paired arches, dark banners
and foreground tree are recognizable and correctly placed. A warm light facade
contrasts with cooler roofs and dark openings. The light-to-dark progression is
clear and the restrained opening drawing is a useful scaffold.

The reference has coherent golden wall planes with subordinate stone variation.
The painting has too many mottled brown, grey and white patches, especially on
the left wall/chimney, and the central facade is pale and somewhat peach colored.
The masonry is too coarse and competes with the architecture. The tree is too
brown/orange and loses its yellow-green character. The sky is too saturated in
one large patch and much too white around the roofs; cloud breaks are jagged and
map-like. Roofs and windows also contain excess white chips. Fine mullions,
window reveals, chimney ribs and the gable's circular ornament are weak. The
paint has firmer silhouettes than its internal architectural divisions.

The earliest foundation is already faceted: diagonal swaths and large unpainted
areas at the gable tip and chimney tops. At 25 percent these have become separate
patches instead of a completed broad pale wall. By 50 percent, the windows are
very dark and much of the surface is granular and fragmented; later work fills
some holes but mostly adds texture. The early coverage defect is the first thing
to repair. Later stone variation and corrections amplify the busy surface.

The process earns credit for gradual value development, reserved openings and
selective drawing. It loses credit for the piecemeal foundation and the large
amount of patch filling that supplies coherence late. A person would establish
the broad wall and sky washes earlier, dry them, and add fewer deliberate shadow
planes and structural accents. Heavier final outlines would not fix this.

Prior plan notes corroborate the early coverage defect and record W15 changing
to W13 after the foundation. The lane coverage proposal was never executed, so
it remains an experiment rather than evidence. This try should begin with a
coverage strategy, reduce subordinate masonry contrast, and fit a small amount
of drawing to the missing structural landmarks.

## Comparison with external feedback (read after the review above)

The external composite is 5.65, reasonably close to my 6.0. I agree with the
low detail and texture assessment: clear outer edges do not constitute precise
architecture when mullions, reveals and ornament disappear and masonry mottling
dominates. I reject the classic edge score of 9.45 as an artistic assessment;
the supplied picture plainly has inaccurate internal edge hierarchy.

I partly agree with the process panel's 2.5/5. The earliest pale layer contains
too much tiny fitted shape information, and much texture arrives together late.
I disagree that nothing develops except uniform saturation: the window darks
arrive later and the actual early gable coverage gap changes. Pale connected
foundations are also normal watercolor practice. I will retain light-to-dark
work, make the first planes more complete, reduce the opening to major structure,
use two middle builds, and reduce stone-block coverage and strength. I will not
add early dark fills simply to make intermediate frames look more finished.

The closing ornament and selected divisions address my own landmark diagnosis;
the judges supplied no reliable landmark score. Fewer automatic near-paper
reserves address visible white chips, not a request from their critique.
