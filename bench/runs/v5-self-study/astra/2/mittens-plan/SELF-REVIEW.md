# Mittens V5, try 1: independent review

Written after looking at `feedback/compare.png` and `feedback/stages.jpg`, before reading `feedback/FEEDBACK.md`.

The composition, ear set, cat's upward gaze, man's expression, and the broad separation between orange cat and dark hair survive. The white chest and bright forehead retain useful paper. The large cat eye is the strongest landmark.

The painting is substantially heavier and more diagrammatic than the reference's fine-line, light-wash sketch. Brown-grey fills join the cat's stripes and cheeks into jagged, posterized planes. The man's sockets are too dark and broad, the hair is brown with disconnected blue-black patches, and the cheek and mouth planes have conspicuous hard boundaries. Paint edges repeat nested shapes instead of turning softly into paper. The warm mixture dominates areas that should be nearly white. The target's slender whiskers, light muzzle hairs, and lively hair contours are largely absent; some surviving facial lines appear wiry against the bulky paint. The cat's eyes need their pale green-gold character and finer lids. The man retains likeness but looks older and more heavily modelled.

The earliest visible stage already contains an extensive opaque-looking beige map across the subject, with most later boundaries fixed. By 25% the hair is nearly a solid brown cap and the man's eye sockets are large blocks. Thus this is chiefly an early wash/value-and-edge allocation failure, amplified by successive mid-value layers. The late dark fragments increase patchiness; closing drawing cannot repair those masses. The progression is physically plausible in its light-to-dark order, but the repeated near-identical filled shapes and lack of purposeful drawing make it look more like progressive segmentation than a person choosing washes.

My independent scores: **4.5/10 finished painting**, **2.5/5 painting plausibility**. These are visual judgments, not calibrated judge outputs.

Next attempt should preserve fitted landmark geometry and the paper reserves, reduce repeated mid-layer buildup, restrain skin and fur saturation, retain a coherent cooler hair mass, and supply truly selective thin drawing at eyes, nose, mouth, whiskers and a few hair turns. Detail must remain on dry paper. I would not add uniform fur texture or compensate with heavy outlines.

## After reading the external feedback

I agree with the low vision scores for edges/detail (2.67) and marks/texture (2.33): hard nested patches and missing fine lines are plainly visible. Composition/value at 5 is reasonable because layout and broad lights remain recognizable. I disagree with treating the classic edge score of 8.84 or classic marks score of 6.95 as artistic success: accurate locations of boundaries do not make their weight, softness or brush character right. The composite 5.42 is a little more generous than my 4.5; I retain my own assessment. There are no written per-region judge notes and no returned progression scores, so no process consensus can be inferred.

The previous plan reveals a late increase in dark gain from 1.1 to 3.0. Its intended repair of pale hair also intensified skin and fur fragmentation. I reject repeating that global late correction. I also reject any inferred demand for more dark contouring based on the numerical edge score. This attempt will change the value allocation earlier, simplify the layer schedule, refine the drawing selection, and choose a more neutral pigment mixture. Preserve successful fitted geometry and small glint reserves.
