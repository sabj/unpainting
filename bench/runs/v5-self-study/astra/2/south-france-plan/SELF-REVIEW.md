# South France V5 try 2: independent review

Written after viewing `feedback/compare.png` and `feedback/stages.jpg`, before opening the external feedback.

The previous painting retains the descending cluster of houses at right, the vertical cypresses, the warm roofs, and the foreground tree's branching skeleton. The pale first stage followed by darker foliage is a credible overall order. Some roof and wall junctions are economical and readable.

The dominant failure is the foliage. A hillside of connected, rounded crowns has become a pale, fragmented map of many little color islands, contour rims, horizontal strips and floating dark patches. The target's green and yellow-green masses are too grey, and the cool turquoise intervals make the fragmentation louder. Cypresses appear as detached near-black accents because the surrounding middle values never cohere. The foreground pine has branches but too little crown to attach them to. The upper terrace, lit walls and winding path contain abrupt white holes instead of luminous pale ochre planes. The houses are recognizable, but some walls lose their planes to white gaps and the line hierarchy is too uniform. This looks more like a segmented image than a pen sketch with a few connected light washes.

The earliest visible failure is already in the 10% frame: a horizontally banded first wash, fragmented paper holes, and diagrammatic masonry. By 25%, the first foliage development establishes the small islands. Later passes mostly fill and darken those same fragments. Extra dark accents cannot repair that early organization; the final stages make the value discontinuity stronger.

My finished-painting score is **4.5/10**. My process plausibility score is **2.5/5**: broadly light to dark, but simultaneous image-wide emergence of fine fragments, repeated stripes, and nearly fixed geometry in later stages do not suggest a painter developing a few deliberate masses.

For this try I want broader, greener foliage mid-values; restrained, connected shadows; fewer layers and fewer sharp fragment boundaries; a sparse opening drawing; and clear architectural junctions concentrated at the right-hand houses. Preserve the light feeling without using large accidental paper-white holes as a substitute for light paint. Specific settings will follow inspection of the available controls and previous plan.

## Response after reading the external feedback

I agree with the judges' weak marks/texture assessment and the early-stage concerns. The stage strip does show early fine fragments followed largely by global darkening. The previous plan's own checkpoint notes confirm two avoidable physical causes: the initial single fitted sweep missed part of a large support, and the subsequent dry lane revision left rectangular overlap bands. Its two automatic correction rounds then supplied most of the brush calls, making the later sharpening excessive.

I disagree with treating every stage as literally a finished image fading in. Roof planes, cypresses and branch structure do develop, and a sparse opening drawing is appropriate for this requested style. I also reject the classic edge score of 9.52 as evidence of good painting edges: the visible rings, stripes and disconnected foliage plainly fail the reference. The final-stage process score of 4.5 seems too generous relative to those marks. Neither a 2/10 vision score nor the 5.21 composite should be optimized in isolation; my priorities remain connected value shapes, light walls, clear focal architecture and purposeful marks.

The new plan changes the value scheduler to pale foundation / middle planes / late shadows, keeps pen plus light wash as the intended result, replaces the huge fitted brush by passage-sized W15 brushes, disables broad automatic near-paper reserves for this full-bleed painted reference, and removes automatic corrective rounds initially. It simplifies the palette and drawing. I reject both more late foliage detail and another dry horizontal-lane repair as answers to this failure.
