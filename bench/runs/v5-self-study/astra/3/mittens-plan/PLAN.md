## Reference and changes

The upward-looking ginger cat is the principal subject; the smiling man's face and dark curls frame it. The big cat eye, ear set, white muzzle and bib, contact at the neck, and the man's eyes and smile carry likeness. Five values: untouched paper; pale warm tints; ginger markings and skin turns; cool charcoal curls/contact; tiny darkest lids, pupils, nostrils and mouth corners. The largest eye is the focal point, followed by the human eyes. The bib and forehead must remain broad lights.

Try 2 preserved composition but averaged fur, skin and hair into a buff underpainting and finished with heavy dark slots and jagged value patches. This try switches from one broad hue-group middle layer to the painter's two complementary region-based middle portions (`lay0`, normal middle domains). This keeps the source's six fitted local color/value families distinct in the middle stage. These are complementary shares of the same middle demand, not nested concentric value islands. The pale first wash remains connected. Two shape-level pigment refit rounds correct the planned mixture against broad characteristic colors before painting; no cell-by-cell render correction is requested. Retain ochre, raw sienna, burnt sienna and Payne's grey: warm ginger and skin, premixed cool charcoal for hair.

Use W15's actual brush choice per passage: pointed rigger/round for small fitted shapes, larger round, flat or mop for broad planes. Reduce shadow gain to 1.6, with threshold 0.42; the previous final 4.0 gain made lids and sockets too heavy. Keep a single weak correction round at 0.20. Preserve the four glint reserves. Smooth reviewed source line chains, remove redundant jagged cheek contours and eye loops, and give both human upper lids continuous fitted paths around their glints. Closing remains fine and selective at pressure 0.34. More numerous but smaller clean-water softening arcs (18 maximum, 64 cells, 0.45 mm radius) address low-contrast turns without crossing the hard focal boundaries.

Accepted critique: flat slabs, uniform early value, jagged cutout edges, poor fine mark character. Rejected: the literal fade-in explanation, treating the high numerical edge score as artistic success, and the perfect final process rating. No fur stipple or global texture rescue. No added heavy outline or late global dark jump.

## Technique, resolution and order

One native cell is 0.19509 mm; one source pixel spans four native cells. A wash body needs about four cells (0.78 mm); narrower lines belong to ink. Family boxes only name fitted color regions, and all use precise-watercolor, so a box never imposes a style seam. Actual brush sizes are selected from fitted support, not asserted as measured telemetry. The W15 brush kit has diameters 1.2, 3, 5, 7, 25.4 and 38 mm: radii about 3.1, 7.7, 12.8, 17.9, 65.1 and 97.4 native cells. The compile records support/radius ratios and the executor checks measured paper state before every physical stroke.

## Paper state, reserves and glazes

All pigment passages in this reference are wet-on-dry or dry-base glazes. Large light and middle passages use the executor's calibrated water 0.62, watery pigment for light washes and medium flowing pigment for local planes. Own-passage overlap is allowed while working the connected mass; unrelated neighboring passages require dry admission. Shadow water is 0.45 over a measured dry base. No wet-in-wet field is planned beside the four eyes. The clean-water edge pass is a deliberate local softening move at water 0.48 and checks its footprint against hard boundaries; it is not export blur. The unchanged executor measures the footprint and neighbors and must dry/wait when state is unsuitable. Never infer dryness from elapsed time.

Explicit lights: four tiny bare-paper glint cores, both cat eyes and both human eyes, 1.35 source pixels square (about 1.05 mm). Target-connected paper lights reserve background, muzzle, bib, forehead and nasal ridge. Ear interiors, sclerae, chin and skin turns are pale painted planes, not enlarged rectangular reserves. Whisker lights are negative-painted gaps; dark whisker strands are pen. Glint cores stay bare; surrounding iris tints tone their apparent contrast. No opaque light repaint.

Cross-boundary glazes: cat cheek into right neck/contact; human temple through cheek into jaw; shared valleys crossing hair locks. Two complementary shadow portions use the same coherent support and fully dried prior color. The middle local-color divisions do not define these shadows. No departure from light-to-dark is needed. Palette mixing within transparent dry layers is intentional; each region receives only the portions appropriate to its value.

Shadow stages should deepen contact/curl valleys and small features without filling sockets. At the pre-closing stop, assess paint independently of ink.

Accept only against the technique skill's final list: (1) value shapes and focal order at thumbnail size; (2) all glints, sheens and broad lights present and appropriately surrounded by pale paint; (3) hard/soft/lost edge hierarchy; (4) fitted eye, ear, muzzle and smile landmarks; (5) purposeful connected marks, no stipple, repeated bands or scratchy contours; (6) credible light-to-dark development; (7) paper-rich vignette with detail focused on the faces.
