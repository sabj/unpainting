# Mittens V5, try 3: independent review of try 2

Written after looking at `feedback/compare.png` and `feedback/stages.jpg`, before opening the external feedback.

The composition and major likeness survive: the cat's ear set, tilted gaze, white muzzle and bib, and the man's hair silhouette and smile are recognizable. Large portions of paper remain light. The restrained drawing avoids a dense contour-tracing effect. Dark hair and facial accents arrive after the first light washes, so the broad light-to-dark order is credible.

The dominant fault is the paint language. Broad beige regions are bounded by many small jagged steps and holes, like a value poster with ragged cutouts. The man's cheek and sockets look filled rather than lightly modeled; both eyes become dark slots and lose the target's iris/sclera distinction. The cat's ochre-orange markings are much too pale and grey, while its eye outlines are too broad and dark. The hair is too brown, flat and angular; it needs a cooler connected dark with lighter internal turns. Fine whiskers and loose hair gestures are weak relative to the paint masses. The white muzzle and bib are good reserves, but some hard-edged white islands within the face and fur look accidental rather than luminous.

The first failure is already visible at 10%: a nearly uniform beige map covers cat, face and hair with abrupt serrated boundaries. At 25% the same map is reinforced. By 50% the darkest cat lid and hair masses arrive before the medium values have gained enough hue or convincing form. Later stages mostly darken the existing stencil; they do not repair its flatness. This is a planning/load/value-boundary problem, not something more final ink would solve.

My finished-painting score: **5.5/10**. My plausible-painting-process score: **3/5**. The order is plausible, but mechanically complete value masks and repeated reinforcement are less plausible than connected selective washes and locally developed features. These are my visual assessments, not calibrated judge scores.

## External feedback comparison

The external composite is 5.4, close to my 5.5. I agree with the low visual assessments of edges and marks, and with the progression tells of a uniform faint value, flat slabs, and scalloped wash boundaries. The stage strip supports these observations. I disagree with treating the numerical edge score of 8.72 as evidence of good artistic edges: a sharp silhouette can be accurately placed and still look cut out. I also reject the stronger claim that this is literally a linear opacity fade; distinct dark hair and feature passages visibly appear around the middle. The early-stage criticism is fair, but the underlying process is an accumulation of paint with insufficiently differentiated passage intentions. The final process score of 5 is too generous in my judgment: the flat slabs remain visible at the end. The judges' mean 3.2 broadly agrees with my 3/5, within the stated noise.

Changes therefore target local color separation, restrained darks, actual brush selection by passage, and cleaner selective drawing. I reject heavier global ink, added fur stippling, a late global dark escalation, and accepting a high numerical edge score as a substitute for visual inspection. I also retain the broad white reserves and target-fitted landmarks, which already work.
