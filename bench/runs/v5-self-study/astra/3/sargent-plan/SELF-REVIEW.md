# Independent review before reading FEEDBACK.md

Viewed the supplied target/previous-painting comparison and the full progression contact sheet first.

What works: the composition and the bowed profile of the central blue hull are recognizable. The pale upper right, dark upper left diagonal, small brown fittings and low dark keel give the scene its main anchors. There is transparent pigment variation and a credible overall movement from pale washes to dark accents. No ink was needed to make the subject recognizable.

What is wrong: the target's hull is one luminous pale blue plane with a darker curved side and a few sweeping highlights. The previous painting breaks it into a grey-green left mass, a heavy blue vertical right mass and jagged bare gaps between them. Pale internal highlights have become large white holes. The hull is too dark and too segmented, with too many contour rims. Warm fittings at left and the lower-right rope/pole have become broad ochre-brown patches. Water has lost its fresh turquoise and yellow-green light. Thin ropes, rigging and pale strakes are incomplete, but this is less harmful than the fragmented principal value shape. The white ship at right needs gentle blue modelling without shredded patch edges.

When it went wrong: the 10% frame already resolves many small separate regions rather than establishing a coherent cool hull wash. By 25% the principal hull is a pale island with isolated patches. The 50% frame strengthens a grey, irregular perimeter; the 75% blue passages turn that into a large dark strip with white cuts. The final quarter adds further darkness and rims rather than unifying the plane. The earliest useful intervention is therefore the broad light/mid wash, not extra finishing accents.

Independent finished-painting score: 5.0/10. Independent process plausibility: 3.0/5. The light-to-dark sequence is sensible, but the globally appearing fragmented regions and repeated patch contours resemble a segmented fill more than connected human brush passages.

Desired direction before seeing external notes: reduce repeated layering and late over-darkening; reconnect the pale and middle blue planes; keep whites selectively inside the true bright shapes rather than widening all light marks; preserve cool colour in the water; and retain pure watercolor with empty drawing files. Inspect the early pale and first blue passes before allowing the darks to accumulate.

## After reading the external feedback

The composite 5.02 agrees closely with my 5.0, but it averages contradictory measures. I agree with the low mark/texture and edge/detail assessments: contour rims and broken broad masses matter more than the presence of many detectable edges. I disagree with treating the automatic edge 8.76 and marks 7.1 as evidence of a successful watercolor. The visible edges are often in the wrong places or have the wrong character.

The progression mean 2.8 is close to my 3.0. I agree that the earliest stages spread faint shapes too evenly across the sheet and that middle forms read as slabs. I also agree that the anchor is too assertive while its surrounding hull is unresolved. I reject the literal opacity-reveal claim: the strip visibly changes hue and adds distinct shadows. The other judge recognizes this layer sequence, but its description of the faults as minor is too generous.

Code inspection separates two issues that I initially grouped together: automatic paper reserves may expand the true pale planes, but the long pale slit through the mid-blue bow cannot be explained by the target's near-paper light values. Brush coverage is therefore the first concern. Use spatially distributed physical sweeps from the beginning, normalize overlapping pigment load, reserve fewer near-paper cells, restore middle colors before darks, and reduce dark/correction accumulation. Do not add ink, stronger darks or artificial texture to conceal the fault.
