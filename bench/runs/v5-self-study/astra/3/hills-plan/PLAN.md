## Reference and value intentions

No sky: three pale warm path fragments thread through yellow-green hills and cool blue-grey oak woodland. The focal point is the spreading oak at the central path bend. Five value families: warm cream/peach path, luminous grass, grey-green lit crowns and olive hill turns, blue-green woodland shade, and a few deep blue-sepia trunk/canopy hollows. Preserve the broad triangular foreground meadow, the smaller left light wedge, the upper-right rising hillside and the road's changing width. Distant woodland is grouped; close branches carry selective fine detail.

## What changed and why

The independent judgement and response to the external judges are in SELF-REVIEW.md. Try 2 established the composition but dulled the hills and paths, reiterated scalloped canopy islands and introduced physical reload bands.

1. Keep W15 brush choice and the precise-watercolor value scheduler, with sparse sepia drawing for the requested ink-and-wash appearance. Keep lay0: broad pale wash, fitted local-colour middle shapes, then two shared shadow glazes. This avoids reintroducing four nested value contours and avoids the ink-and-wash profile depositing all illuminated-plane demand at once.
2. Choose wet on dry for each family's first pale mass, connecting its flowing strokes through feathered reloads. The reference calls for connected grass and canopy undercolour, with no sharp leaf detail attempted yet. The initial wet-on-wet version was refused by the native whole-footprint moisture gate (40.4% moist versus the required state); the clean-sheet version therefore uses the supported dry-base route. Water load .62 with watery calibrated pigment for the foundation; .62 and medium pigment for local planes; .45 and medium/strong pigment for final shade. These are the executor's technique loads, not invented moisture readings.
3. Apply finite-load feathering at 1.5 brush radii from the first stroke. Adjacent reloads taper through their overlap, so a connected slope does not acquire full-pressure bands. This was requested too late in try 2 and its application was unconfirmed.
4. Keep yellow, green and cool pigments; add burnt sienna to give the pale warm road a direct peach/earth component. Palette order: hansa, sap green, ochre, burnt sienna, ultramarine, cerulean, sepia. Do not artificially warm the green hills with a global earth conversion.
5. Reduce middle demand modestly to .95. Protect broad bright warm planes using the existing planning light mechanism: 22% less middle paint where the 1 mm squinted target L* is at least 78, with no shadow glaze in those lit cells; only L*96 glints qualify as paper. The mechanism's historical log calls this 'lit stone', but it tests warm colour and brightness rather than subject, so it also describes these sunlit slopes and paths. No rectangular reserves; path bodies remain pale painted planes. This is reduced planned deposition, not lifting or recolouring a rendered image.
6. Increase minimum connected shape area from 120 to 240 native cells (about 4 mm²), simplifying the smallest canopy fragments and repeated islands. Keep fitted major boundaries and the road, rather than invent larger geometric shapes.
7. Remove both automatic touch-up rounds (`--touchpoints 0`). Try 2's late extra deposits reinforced contour edges and could undo a deliberate luminous light. The planned two dark glazes remain, with dark gain 1.1 and cool gain 1.0. Use the explicit option rather than tp0: the token also requests a touchgain which would never be consumed when zero rounds run.
8. Remove most jagged traced canopy lines; retain a small set of target-supported branch/path gestures with smoothly changing direction and taper. Sepia ink absorption multiplier 3, drawing width gain 1, opening pressure .25, closing .48. Ink explains stems, never fills canopy tone.
9. A trial revision at boundary 16 used overlapping 3 mm strokes and .6 mm planned edge overrun for the larger remaining shapes, on dry paper with feathered reloads. It was tested through the first middle-value layer and **rejected at boundary 59**: repeated horizontal field marks appeared, and the rimmed contours remained. The final configuration returns to W15's fitted broad/round paths for the small second middle layer and shared shadows. Already-painted marks remain. No heavier ink or automatic touch-up layer is added to conceal them.

Accepted critique: flat early placements, cut/scalloped edges, premature small detail, late overwork and weak mark character. Rejected: literal reveal as a proven mechanism; high classic edge/mark scores as artistic approval; arbitrary added texture or heavier blanket ink as a remedy. No physical effects, recording or paper-state gates are disabled.

## Physical sequence and checks

Opening: a few light connected sepia gestures on dry paper anchor trunk forks and interrupted path shoulders. The source paths are audited separately from execution; shorter gestures may be filtered by the fixed drawing selector.

Foundation: each target-derived warm/cool connected mass starts on fully dry paper, with flowing pale pigment and no intermediate drying between its own feathered reloads. Every family explicitly requests wet on dry. There is no detailed leaf passage at this stage. The painter must measure the whole footprint and neighbours before each contact, dry or wait if the base is wrong, and retain its own-passage continuation checks. The pale light road is painted, not held with a rectangular mask. No wet-in-wet exception remains in the executable restart.

Middle: fully dry base; local-colour planes carry the grass/crown/path distinction. The second middle band carries only 12% of remaining middle demand. No density texture and no extra general detail pass.

Glazes: fully dry again. Two complementary shares (65/35) of a shared connected shadow support cross the central crowns and their internal colour boundaries, continue through the dark left foreground, and include fitted cast shade on the grass. They do not follow family box edges. Lit warm shapes stay out. Selected crisp dark edges distinguish the oak at the bend; internal crown edges may disappear into adjacent shade.

Finish: no automatic repainting to close every gap. Inspect before closing ink; the landscape must read before its sparse structural lines. All pen contacts are on dry paper with explicit sepia pigment. No added marks to disguise blurred paint.

Before GPU: require ADMITTED; examine phase allocation, fitted brush paths, wet versus dry moves, lights and unsupported seams. The separate crop-render proof in the skill is replaced by the first actual passage stop because this task only permits bench/paint.sh to perform physics.

Checkpoints: opening drawing; first warm/green foundation; complete foundation; complete local middle values; before closing. Early paint should look incomplete and pale, with coherent broad strokes, not finished tiny crowns faded to white. At middle values the road must retain pale warmth and the grass must remain lighter than the canopy. Final shadows must connect tree depth without filling the field. Failure signs: reload bands, boxed boundaries, nested scalloped rims, thick ochre ground, uniform crown blobs, white path cutouts, scratchy or excessive ink. Revise only unpainted work when a controllable defect appears.

Artistic acceptance, in order: thumbnail value shapes; correctly placed toned lights; purposeful hard/soft/lost edges; path and trunk landmarks; connected marks without stripes/stipple/stamps; believable light-to-dark development; selective focal detail and remaining breathing room. Admission is a material/integration check, not artistic success.
