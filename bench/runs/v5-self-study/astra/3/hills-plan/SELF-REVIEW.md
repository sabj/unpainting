# Hills V5 try 3: independent review of try 2

Written after viewing the target, previous painting and progress sheet, before opening FEEDBACK.md.

The winding road, diagonal arrangement of wooded valleys, central oak and open foreground slope remain readable. The blue-green tree shadows contrast with the yellow ground, and the painting develops from light washes to dark masses. It is recognisably the same landscape.

The light ground is too ochre/olive, too evenly covered and too dark; the reference has fresh luminous yellow-green grass and many small airy light interruptions. The road should be a distinct pale warm peach ribbon, but has become a weak ivory slit inside a brownish surround. Foliage is organised into nested, rimmed islands with roughly equal emphasis across the sheet. Those contour steps look like a relief map rather than loose canopy brushwork. The biggest foreground oak has a useful branching gesture but the thin dark lines feel angular and detached from the weight of the trunk. The target's blue-grey foliage is cooler and more varied; the painting is broadly muddier. Large shapes are reasonably faithful, but the edge language and light hierarchy are not.

At 10% the pale connected wash is promising, although its whole-sheet coverage already threatens the road lights. By 25% the little outlined colour islands are apparent. Between 50% and 75% repeated mid-value applications solidify those islands and dull the illuminated ground. The late darks improve the value skeleton but cannot restore the earlier lights or remove the contour rims. Closing pen is not the main cause; adding more general ink would not fix it.

My finished-painting score: **5.5/10**. My process-plausibility score: **3.3/5**. The order is believable, but too many small separately reiterated patches and too little differentiation between meadow wash and canopy marks make the execution feel mechanical. These are visual judgements, not calibrated judge scores.

## Response to external critique

The composite 5.58 is close to my own 5.5. I agree with the low visual marks/texture judgement: the nested rimmed canopy shapes are the conspicuous defect, and the flat early placements are visible. I also agree that the light-to-dark order becomes more convincing later. I disagree with treating the classic edge score of 8.93 or marks score of 7.04 as an artistic success; the painting's contours are registered but have the wrong character.

The progression judges disagree with each other. The first sees credible wash/mid/dark development, the second a digital reveal. The stage strip supports the former order of work but also the latter's concern about complete faint shapes appearing early. I accept the appearance problem, not the unsupported claim that this was literally a reveal. Their 4.5 late-stage ratings seem generous given the contour-map finish; the early 2.5–3.0 ratings seem reasonable. I will not optimise for the roughly half-point noise in these scores.

Changes chosen from that evidence: a genuinely moist broad first wash, feathered physical reloads from the outset, restrained illuminated-plane demand, fewer tiny repeated shapes, no automatic finishing corrections, and simpler flowing focal ink. Adding random dry texture, tracing every crown, or returning to four nested middle layers would not address the defect and is rejected. I retain the pale foundation/local-colour/shared-shadow scheduler because the previous first-wash value was an improvement; its execution and later overworking need change.
