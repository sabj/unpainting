# Painting Plan: Trumbull, V5 Try 2

## Diagnosis & Changes from Try 1

**Try 1 Problems:**
- Early stages too faint (10%, 25% nearly invisible) → Fix: richer, more saturated initial washes
- Stone texture random and scattered → Fix: organized dry-brush following architectural coursing
- Edges not crisp (2.67/5) → Fix: sharper window treatment, closing ink on dry paper
- Wall tone greenish → Fix: warmer pigment mix, emphasize golden yellows
- Value separation weak → Fix: darker shadow side, more visible progression
- Progression judge saw "texture dump" and filter-like appearance → Fix: gradual layering with visible structure

**Key Changes:**
1. Explicit architectural regions in config (sky, lit wall, shadow wall, windows, foliage) so painter understands form
2. Visible reserves for cloud lights, lit wall plane, window openings
3. Richer pigment concentration in initial washes (more visible at 10%, 25%)
4. Stone coursing as organized element, not scattered texture
5. Sharper window detail on completely dry paper
6. Warmer overall color temperature

## Value Thumbnail

- **Brightest lights:** Sky at top (pale blue ~30%), cloud edges (pale ~25%), lit wall plane (~45%), window arches (reserved ~10%)
- **Mid-values:** Wall base color (~50-55%), roof/tower mid-tone (~60%), foliage dark green (~65%)
- **Shadows:** Shadow side of wall (~70%), lower street (~75%)
- **Darkest:** Window recesses and interior (~90%), cast shadow under arches (~85%)
- **Focal point:** Central tower and main window cluster (~0.4-0.6 horizontally, 0.3-0.6 vertically)

Light progression: pale sky → warm lit wall → darker foliage → dark window recesses

## Protected Lights (Reserves)

1. **Sky reserved zone** (top 0.08 height): pale blue sky to be painted wet in wet, with white cloud lights left unpainted or lifted
2. **Cloud edge, upper center** (~0.35-0.65 × 0.03-0.12): brightest cloud edge, reserved as white paper
3. **Lit wall catch light** (~0.18-0.28 × 0.35-0.48): brightest plane on central facade between windows, reserved pale
4. **Window arch lights** (~0.28-0.38 × 0.42-0.52): upper arches catch some light, reserved light
5. **Tower top** (~0.05-0.15 × 0.08-0.2): lit side of tower, reserved pale

## Per-Region Technique and Order

### Opening Drawing (Before any wash)
- **Sparse structure lines on dry paper:**
  - Horizontal stone coursing lines (5-6 key layers marking stone boundaries)
  - Window mullions and arch shapes (only main grid, not every pane)
  - Tower outline and roof peaks
  - Main door frame
- **Material:** Fine sepia ink or graphite, brush-pen or nib
- **Style:** Sparse, confident flowing lines from one dip, no ruled hatch
- **Purpose:** Carry structure and define focal points before any pigment

### Stage 1: Sky Wash (Wet in Wet)
- **Move:** Wet on wet, one continuous passage
- **Paper state:** Evenly dampened with glossy sheen (not flooded)
- **Load:** Medium to fairly strong (not pale/weak like Try 1) - pale blue mixed from ultramarine and cerulean
- **Execution:**
  1. Wet entire sky area with clean water
  2. Apply pale blue from top downward, stronger near top (more pigment)
  3. Lighten toward horizon (more water, less pigment)
  4. Charge cooler shadow on upper left (blue-grey) while still wet
  5. Reserve white cloud edge (or lift with thirsty brush) while wet
  6. Dry fully before any other pass touches sky
- **Intent:** Sky reads clearly at 25% strokes - pale but with visible cool blues
- **Avoid:** Too watery (Try 1 issue) - use medium pigment concentration so it shows

### Stage 2: Wall Base Wash (Wet on Dry)
- **Paper state:** Completely dry
- **Two separate regions, painted in sequence (allow drying between if needed):**

  **2a. Lit Wall (left/center front):**
  - **Load:** Medium watery, warm golden tone (yellow ochre + touch of light red/burnt sienna, fairly saturated)
  - **Move:** One broad connected passage, following wall plane
  - **Edge:** Hard edge at top (under sky), soft edge at bottom (toward darker street level)
  - **Execution:** Paint left side and center facade in one warm wash, leaving window openings unpainted
  - **Intent:** At 25% strokes, building form is clearly visible in warm local color (not faint)

  **2b. Shadow/Right Wall:**
  - **Load:** Similar warm tone but slightly darker/more sienna-leaning (maintaining warmth, not cool)
  - **Paper state:** Dry
  - **Execution:** Right tower side and right wall face, darker than lit side but still warm
  - **Edge:** One hard edge (tower outline), soft where it transitions to foliage

### Stage 3: Foliage (Wet on Dry)
- **Paper state:** Completely dry
- **Load:** Pale yellow-green, fairly saturated (cadmium yellow + cerulean, not pale/watery)
- **Move:** Irregular canopy shape, one connected passage
- **Execution:**
  - Reserve sky holes in canopy (don't paint over them)
  - Soft upper edge where it recedes into sky
  - Darker bottom edge where foliage meets wall
- **Intent:** Foliage reads as distinct and vibrant at 25%-50%, not subdued

### Stage 4: Mid-Value Detail (Dry on Dry)
- **Paper state:** Completely dry
- **Move:** Dry on dry, controlled load, following architectural structure

  **4a. Stone Coursing (Horizontal Lines):**
  - **Load:** Medium, grey made from ultramarine + burnt sienna (not green-tinted)
  - **Execution:** Ordered horizontal strokes marking 4-5 key stone layers on the facade
  - **Edge:** One hard edge on shadow side (where stone catches shadow), soft on light side
  - **Pattern:** Follow the actual coursing visible in the target - not random scattered marks
  - **Intent:** Building's form defined through architectural ordering

  **4b. Tower Mid-Tone:**
  - **Load:** Medium grey (same mix), slightly warmer on shadow side
  - **Execution:** Define left edge (lit) and right edge (shadow) with one hard and one soft edge

### Stage 5: Shadow Glaze (Cross-Boundary, Dry Base)
- **Paper state:** Completely dry before applying
- **One unified glaze** across shadow side of building:
  - From right tower, across center windows (leaving window shapes dark but not yet filled), down right wall
  - Runs into foliage transition at bottom-right

- **Load:** Medium semi-transparent, cool shadow (ultramarine + alizarin, muted, not opaque)
- **Execution:** One connected passage with clear hard edge on lit side, soft edge where turning
- **Intent:** Unify shadow side in value and mood, establish depth

### Stage 6: Window Recesses and Details (Dry on Dry)
- **Paper state:** Completely dry
- **Load:** Thick, darkest value (ultramarine + alizarin + drop of black, or prussian blue)
- **Move:** Small, precise marks on dry paper with controlled contact
- **Execution:**
  - Window interiors (darkest, leaving arch highlights reserved/barely tinted)
  - Dark shadow under main arches
  - Cast shadow on lower wall where door/archway recesses
  - Depth marks in doorway
- **NOT a fill:** Individual shapes defining depth and form, with purpose

### Stage 7: Foliage Detail (Darker Greens)
- **Paper state:** Completely dry
- **Load:** Dark green (prussian blue + cadmium yellow or viridian shadow)
- **Execution:** Suggest foliage texture with dry-brush or light directional marks, heavier on shadow side
- **Intent:** Foliage reads darker and more distinct than base wash

### Stage 8: Closing Ink (Last, Selective)
- **Paper state:** Completely dry
- **Ink:** Sepia or black (medium weight, not heavy)
- **Move:** Brush-pen or fine nib, selective lines only

  **Ink Application (Focal Point Only):**
  - **Mullion definition:** Reinforce main window grid where it clarifies form (central windows, tower windows)
  - **Arch lines:** Enhance gothic arch definition with light pressure, tapered lines
  - **Stone coursing refinement:** A few key horizontal lines at the darkest coursing to add crispness
  - **Door frame:** Define entry door frame clearly
  - **NO full outlines** - structure already exists from paint

  **Technique:**
  - Light, tapered lines with pressure variation
  - Selective gestural marks only (not fills)
  - Concentrate on central tower and main window cluster
  - Avoid heavy ink over blurry paint (from Try 1 lesson)

## Wet Passages and Dry Barriers

- **Sky (Stage 1):** Isolated wet passage, dry barrier of ~2mm below sky edge before wall wash touches
- **Wall base (Stage 2):** Dry on dry, separate regions allowed to dry
- **Foliage (Stage 3):** Separate region, edges soften where they touch wall but no bleed-through
- **All detail:** Eyes, mullions, stone lines → on dry paper only, no spill-over from wet regions

## Layer Dependencies

1. Opening drawing dries on dry paper
2. Sky washes fully before wall touches sky edge
3. Wall and foliage dry before any mid-value layer
4. Mid-values dry before shadow glaze
5. Shadow glaze dries before detail work
6. All paint dries before closing ink

## Resolution Checks

- **Sheet:** 280 mm = 1200 px output
- **Cell size:** 0.233 mm per cell at 1200 px
- **Window detail:** Mullions span 10-15 cells at smallest; handled by opening lines + closing ink
- **Stone coursing:** Major horizontal layers are ~40-80 cells apart; dry-brush can follow these
- **Feature sizing:** Arch shapes (focal point) are ~100+ cells wide; can paint as clear shapes on dry paper

## Progression Roadmap (Judged Against Intent)

| Stage | Content | Value Target | Visual Intent | At Progress % |
|-------|---------|--------------|---------------|---------------|
| Opening | Structure lines | — | Architecture grid visible | ~5% |
| 1 | Sky wash | Pale ~30% | Cool blue sky, basis for light | ~15% |
| 2 | Wall + foliage base | Warm ~50% | Building form emerges in local color | ~35% |
| 3 | Foliage detail | Green ~65% | Foliage now distinct and vibrant | ~45% |
| 4 | Stone coursing + tower mid | Mid ~60% | Architectural form clarified through layers | ~60% |

**Key difference from Try 1:** At 25% strokes, building and sky should both be clearly visible in warm, readable values — not faint. At 50%, clear progression with distinct regions. Texture and detail build gradually, not suddenly.

## Breaks from Default Light-to-Dark

- **Stage 3 (foliage) comes before Stage 4 (mid-values):** Foliage base is simpler and dries quickly; starting it early allows mid-value work to follow without waiting
- **Stone coursing as ordered mid-values, not random texture:** Justified by target's clear horizontal coursing pattern

## Failure Prevention

1. **Blur in detail:** All windows, mullions, stone coursing on completely dry paper with moderate load
2. **Faint early stages:** Use richer pigment concentration in Sky and Wall washes; aim for visible value at 25% strokes
3. **Artificial texture:** Stone coursing follows horizontal architecture, not scattered marks
4. **Muddy wall:** One light wash for lit plane, shadow side warmer not cooler (no grey-green muddiness)
5. **Lost edges:** Hard edges on window outlines and tower edges; soft edges on foliage and sky transitions
6. **Heavy ink with blur:** Ink over sharp paint only; selective lines at focal point
