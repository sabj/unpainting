# Painting plan: South France v2, urban sketch, V5 round 2

## Critical changes from Try 1

**Problem fixed:** Removed two large rectangular reserves that created obvious artificial mask holes.
**Approach:** Urban sketch means pen structure first (opening), light wash second (mid-layers), accents last (closing ink). Try 1 had wash + scattered dabs; Try 2 uses pen + wash progression.

## Reference cues

Tuscan hilltop village under warm afternoon light. Pale blue sky above dark cypress sentinels. Buildings with terracotta roofs on the right, strong cast shadows on the sunlit field. Rolling grass landscape with cypress trees creating vertical rhythm. Brightest area: sunlit side of buildings. Darkest areas: tree shadows and building recesses. No pure blacks—warm shadows with violet undertones.

## Value structure

- Lights: pale sky, bright grass field, lit building façades
- Half-tones: mid grass with turning planes, tree canopy shapes, building shadow sides
- Darks: cypress tree trunks and shadows, deep building recesses, cast shadows across field
- Focal point: building cluster on the right with roofline and façade planes

## Regions and techniques

**sf.grass (bottom half, 0.5–1.0):** Light wash base, pale yellowish-green, wet on dry. Damp with shine gone, few strokes, paper showing. Foreground (bottom 15%) gets dry-brush flicks for grass texture at focal zone. Shadow glaze across where terrain turns and cast shadows fall.

**sf.trees (left/center, tall cypress shapes):** Canopy mass per tree, wet on dry, with sky holes reserved. Dark mixed greens (ultramarine, sap green, burnt sienna). Charge shadow shapes into still-wet canopy for soft transitions. Trunks and branches last, tapering from belly to point. Foreground cypress lines at focal point: dry paper, shaped marks with one crisp edge.

**sf.buildings (right side, 0.6–1.0):** Architecture approach. Opening pen traces structure, rooflines, building junctions. Broad pale wall wash on dry paper. Mid values: roofs darker than walls, shadow sides darker where they turn. One connected shadow glaze per shadow side, crossing boundaries. Windows and doorway recesses as last accents. Closing ink restores roofline definition and building corners. No dark fills—ink is gestural line only.

**sf.sky (top, 0.0–0.4):** One continuous pale wash on dry or evenly damp paper. Graded pale cream-white at horizon to pale blue-grey at top. Reserve or lift cloud lights early. No over-merging—keep subtle blue-to-white transition visible.

## Drawing plan

**Opening:** Comprehensive pen lines traced from target. Rooflines, building edges, cypress tree profiles, main junctions. Light to medium pressure, defining silhouettes and structure. No fills or ruled hatches. Should read like a painter's first gestural marks, not linework.

**Closing:** Selective ink on dry paint at focal points—roofline definition, building corners where they carry structure, accent valleys between trees. Light and selective, never a fill.

## Load and moves

- Grass wash: watery, big brush, few strokes, paper showing. Damp with shine gone to avoid blooms.
- Trees: medium load, charged into wet or layered on dry per reference.
- Buildings: medium load for walls, controlled edges. Fold shadows as glazes with one hard and one soft edge.
- Pen: light to medium pressure. No tremor, spurs, uniform flicks. Width follows pressure; runs go dry naturally.

## Edge roles

- Sky–tree boundary: soft where trees blur into sky, hard where rooflines silhouette.
- Grass–tree shadow: soft where shadow turns field, hard at building edge.
- Building sunlit side: hard edge separating from grass, soft edge where it turns into shadow.
- Grass texture: dry-brush or pen foreground only; middle distance stays a wash.

## Protected lights

None reserved. Let the painter protect its own paper whites from the value analysis. No rectangular mask holes this time.

## Failure signs to watch

- Large white holes persisting through stages (fixed: no reserves).
- Scattered dabs instead of structure-first progression (fixed: full pen opening, wash follows).
- Sky banding or tiles (fixed: one continuous graded passage).
- Buildings as patchwork rectangles (fixed: connected wall plane, shadow glaze across).
- No pen linework visible early (fixed: comprehensive traced lines as opening).

## Tokens and changes

- `_lay2`: Two mid layers instead of four. Reduces opacity build-up, keeps work light and sketch-like.
- `_ink2`: Reduced ink from ink4. Selective closing, not heavy strokes.
- Base recipe kept: rec2x, rcl, sky3, leaf, cg13, vio, rg10, skyb, cpp, tv8, uncap, pgreen, dwg200, tp2, shs.

## Stage intents

1. **Opening drawing:** Pen structure fully visible. Rooflines and building edges read. Cypress silhouettes clear. No fills, minimal lines, natural gesture.
2. **First light wash:** Entire field is pale yellowish-green, nearly monochrome. Sky is pale cream-blue. Paper showing, fresh and loose.
3. **Mid layer 1 (trees and field shading):** Tree canopy shapes appear from pen structure. Shadows across grass begin to read. Building walls take warm undertone.
4. **Mid layer 2 (values):** Building shadow sides and roof planes defined. Tree shadows deepen. Grass field has turning planes visible.
5. **Shadow glaze:** Connected dark glaze across building shadow side and cast shadow on field, unifying composition.
6. **Closing ink:** Rooflines crisp at focal points. Building corners restored. Select accent lines in tree valleys. Light, selective, never a fill.

## Fast proof

- Test one cypress tree in 30 seconds: wet canopy mass, charge shadow. Does it flow naturally or blob?
- Test grass-to-shadow transition in 60 seconds: pale wash, then glaze. Is edge soft or hard as planned?
- Test building edge in 90 seconds: open pen line, pale wash, shadow glaze. Does form read or blur?
