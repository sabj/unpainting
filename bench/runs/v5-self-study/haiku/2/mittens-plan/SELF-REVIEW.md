# Self-Review: Try 1 of 3

## Examining my previous painting

**Composite score: 4.57/10** — This is poor. The vision judges all gave 2.0/10 overall, the worst rating. The progression judge gave 1.5-2.0, describing it as "a fade-in" rather than human painting progression.

### What works
- The painting captures the basic composition: cat on left, human on right
- Eye placement and shape are approximately correct
- Some value modeling in the face and hair
- The classic value rubric score (6.08) is reasonable, showing the target values were generally hit

### What is wrong

**Most critical failures:**

1. **Marks texture: 1.0/10** — This is catastrophic. The style demands "fine lines and light washes," but the painting shows undifferentiated washes without crisp, sketch-like character. No clear strokes or directed marks visible.

2. **Edges detail: 2.0/10** — The edges are blurry and undefined. The target shows clear, crisp pen lines defining whiskers, eye shapes, face contours. The painting shows soft blur everywhere.

3. **Large white voids remaining too long** — A hard rectangular void sits in the cat's chest and hair through stages 1-4, only filled at stage 5. Same issue in a smaller void in the hair. These feel like placeholder gaps, not intentional paper reserves.

4. **Progression looks like a fade-in, not human painting** — The judges noted: "The sequence shows a single image fading in from low opacity to high opacity, rather than strokes being added progressively." This is a fundamental failure. The opening drawing should be visible and strong; each pass should add new information, not just fade everything in uniformly.

5. **Missing sketch quality** — The target is a watercolor portrait *sketch* with fine lines and light washes. The painting became a soft, blended wash without the sketch character. The opening drawing either wasn't rendered or wasn't strong enough.

### Where it went wrong

The core problem is **opening drawing invisibility or weakness**. Looking at the target, every key feature is defined by clear pen lines: cat's whiskers, eye shapes, face contours, mouth. The previous plan called for "sparse structural lines" from a trace, but the execution didn't render them visibly or strongly enough for the painting to read as a sketch.

The wash layers then filled in without clear directional marks or stopping points, creating the fade-in effect rather than layered painting progression.

**Stages of failure:**
- Stage 1: Opening drawing should have been visibly rendered (sparse but confident pen lines). It wasn't, or was too faint.
- Stage 2-4: Washes filled large regions without clear subdivision, creating voids that looked awkward until the final stage filled them.
- Stage 5+: Instead of adding crisp final details and directional marks, the painting just continued softening and blending.

### My self-score

- **Finished painting: 2/10** — Poor execution of the requested style. The painting is representational but not sketch-like, not detailed, not well-progressed.
- **Progression plausibility: 1.5/5** — The judges were right: this reads like a filter fade-in, not human painting. The opening lines were not visible early, the voids were awkward, and everything softened together.

## Agreement/disagreement with judges

**Where I agree:**
- The vision judges' 2.0 rating is fair. The painting does not read as a watercolor portrait sketch with fine lines and light washes.
- The progression judge's 1.5-2.0 is fair. The fade-in effect is real and breaks the sense of human painting.
- The marks texture score of 1.0 is deserved. There are no characteristic watercolor marks or directed strokes visible.

**Where I disagree slightly:**
- The composition values score of 3.0 is reasonable but slightly harsh — the composition is correct, though the rendering is poor.

## Changes for try 2

1. **Much stronger opening drawing** — Trace the target more thoroughly, especially whiskers and face contours. Ensure the opening stage is visibly rendered and confident.

2. **Smaller, more numerous regions** — Break up large regions to avoid those awkward white voids. Map regions that subdivide naturally.

3. **Explicit subdivision of washes** — Rather than one large cat fur wash, break it into lit side and shadow side. Rather than a monolithic hair mass, break it into sections. This will make the progression clearer and avoid large unfinished gaps.

4. **Drier contact and lighter loads** — Follow the watercolor technique guidance: dry paper, controlled loads, crisp edges. Avoid over-blending. Aim for the sketch effect.

5. **Simpler recipe** — Reduce the number of passes if it's causing confusion in the render progression. Let the opening drawing carry more of the form.

6. **Better monitoring during painting** — Use the look checks to catch fade-in behavior early and revise if needed.
