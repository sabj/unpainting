# Self-Review: Hills V5, Try 1

## What works
- The overall composition reads as rolling hills with paths
- The dark forest areas establish depth and contrast
- The color palette (greens, yellows, teals) matches the target's general hue

## What is wrong
1. **Stark white reserved areas** - The two reserved path regions stayed completely white/untouched, reading as machine-like "crisp white slabs." Real paths should be toned with pale yellow-green, not reserved as pure white.

2. **Hard, scalloped wash boundaries** - The transitions between regions look mechanical rather than natural watercolor. Edges should be soft and flowing, especially where hills meet forest or background.

3. **Regional patchwork effect** - The landscape appears to fill region-by-region rather than as unified washes flowing across the whole sheet. The judge noted it looks like a digital color morph revealing regions simultaneously.

4. **Over-precise application** - Individual marks appear speckled/dotted rather than smooth washes. The painting lacks the fluidity and loose character of real ink-and-wash.

5. **No atmospheric progression** - The painting doesn't demonstrate believable human-like painting order. Entire regions appear finished while neighbors are untouched.

## When it went wrong
- **Stage 1 (opening lines)**: Lines were traced correctly, but the strategy of reserving large path areas was flawed.
- **Stage 2-3 (first wash)**: The single wash should have covered more of the landscape as one connected passage. Instead, it appears to respect region boundaries too rigidly.
- **Stages 4-5 (mid values)**: Each region painted independently, creating visible boundaries. Should blend and overlap.
- **Overall**: The plan prioritized reserving lights over creating natural atmospheric layering.

## Scores
- **Finished painting**: 4/10 (composition works, but execution is too mechanical)
- **Painting plausibility**: 2/5 (judges correctly identified it as machine-like, not human painting process)
- **Judge feedback agrees**: The tells about scalloped edges, regions finishing separately, and simultaneous filling are accurate observations.

## Changes for try 2
1. **Eliminate or minimize reserves** - Allow washes to cover the landscape; tone the paths with pale layers instead of leaving white paper
2. **Unify the composition** - Use fewer regions or one large region to emphasize flowing washes rather than boundaries
3. **Softer transitions** - Plan for soft edges where hills turn, soft blends between light and shadow
4. **One large base wash** - Instead of patchwork, lay a single connected pale yellow-green across the entire landscape
5. **Reduce closing ink** - Use sparse, gestural marks only at focal points
6. **More atmospheric depth** - Plan cooler, darker values for distant areas; warmer, lighter for foreground paths
