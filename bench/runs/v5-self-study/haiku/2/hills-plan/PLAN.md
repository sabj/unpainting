# Painting plan: Hills, V5, try 2

## Changes from try 1 and rationale

**Core issue from try 1**: The painting scored 4.21/10 and judges noted "scalloped wash edges, regions arriving finished while neighbors untouched, two crisp white slabs that stay untouched." The reserved path areas read as machine-like masks rather than natural watercolor. The progression looked like a digital color morph, not human painting.

**Strategy change**:
1. **Eliminate large reserves**: One small reserve only (brightest path accent). Allow washes to tone the entire landscape; paths emerge through value layering, not white paper.
2. **Single unified region**: One large family covering the whole landscape. Removes regional patchwork and hard boundaries.
3. **Softer transitions**: Token choices (_softs, _lf, _lit20) for feathered edges and smooth blending.
4. **Flowing washes, fewer layers**: _lay2 (2 mid layers instead of 4) for looser, more gestural feel.
5. **Atmospheric depth**: Cool, dark forest in shadow; warm, pale paths in light. One continuous wash rhythm across the whole sheet.

## Reference analysis

**Target**: Real landscape photo of rolling hills with pale tan/cream paths winding through green hillsides. Dark forest areas provide depth and value structure. Sky visible at top. Atmospheric: distant areas softer and cooler; foreground warmer and lighter.

**Value structure**:
- **Lights**: Pale yellow-cream paths (0.75–0.85 value), brightest at left and top paths. NOT pure white; they read as lit planes, toned with surrounding colors.
- **Midtones**: Yellow-green and medium green hills (0.45–0.65 value). Warmest on the lit left side of paths.
- **Shadows**: Dark forest areas and shade sides of hills (0.15–0.35 value). Cool greens and teals where shadows turn. Darkest in forest bottom.
- **Focal point**: The paths themselves, where they cross from light into shadow. Secondary: the crown of the hill at left where it catches light.

## Technique and painting sequence

**Region: hills.landscape** — ink-and-wash over dry paper. One passage covering the entire sheet, with soft layering to achieve atmospheric depth.

### Stage 1: Opening drawing
**Intent**: Sparse structural lines. Paths visible as positive shapes; main hill edges and forest mass indicated. No hatching or detail.

- **Lines**: Traced from target with low contrast. Minimal line set to guide the wash; paths as confident, flowing curves. No outline fill.
- **Paper state**: Dry throughout.
- **Move**: Pen on dry paper.
- **Load**: Sepia ink, light pressure, gestural line weight.

### Stage 2: First wash (pale base)
**Intent**: One pale, transparent yellow-green wash across the entire landscape, connecting from top to bottom. Establishes the light of the whole sheet. Paper shows through; brush marks visible but connected.

- **Region**: Full landscape (0,0 to 1,1). No boundary edges within the sheet.
- **Paper state**: Dry paper, then lightly dampen with clean water to prepare for a flowing wash, or wet-on-dry direct application.
- **Color**: Pale yellow-green mixed from yellow and cool green. Very watery, low pigment concentration.
- **Brush**: Large round or mop, few strokes. Broad horizontal or following terrain, connecting left to right and top to bottom.
- **Load**: Watery, light consistency. Water in brush; pigment carried, not deposited densely.
- **Edges**: Soft where the wash meets sky at top; internal edges soft throughout (no hard boundaries between hill and forest yet).
- **Move**: Wet-on-dry (very light damping) or direct wet load into dry paper. Allow pigment to flow slightly.
- **Duration**: One continuous passage, no lifting yet.
- **Result check**: Whole sheet tinted pale yellow-green. Paper texture visible. Paths visible as slightly lighter areas within the wash. No pure white isolated regions.

### Stage 3: Dry fully
- **Action**: Wait for paper to dry completely before next layer.
- **Check**: Press finger to back; no shine or damp.

### Stage 4: Mid-value layer 1 (forest and shadow structure)
**Intent**: Establish the dark forest mass and the shadow turn of hills. One connected glaze across the lower half and shadow sides. Defines value structure.

- **Color**: Dark cool green, teal-blue mix. Mixed from ultramarine, sepia, cool green. Medium-to-concentrated load.
- **Region**: Lower third and left side where forest mass and shadows occur. One passage, soft internal edges.
- **Paper state**: Dry paper.
- **Brush**: Medium round, deliberate placement. Follow terrain contours loosely.
- **Load**: Medium pigment, less watery than base wash. Fluid but visible brushwork.
- **Edges**: Hard edge along top of forest against lit hills (where the tree line reads crisply). Soft edges where shadow bleeds into lit areas (hills turning away). Bottom edge soft into the foreground.
- **Move**: Wet-on-dry, overlapping slightly into the base wash where dark glazes over light. No blotting; confident single load per pass.
- **Result check**: Forest areas read as dark, unified mass. Hill shadows read as a connected plane. Paths start to read as lighter shapes within the darker landscape.

### Stage 5: Dry
- **Action**: Full dry before next layer.

### Stage 6: Mid-value layer 2 (warming the lit paths)
**Intent**: Add warmth and value to the lit path areas, bringing them forward. One glaze over the right and center paths, keeping edges soft and irregular.

- **Color**: Warm pale ochre-yellow mix, semi-transparent. Less saturation than the base wash, warmer hue.
- **Region**: Right and center paths, the lit foreground. Loose bounds following where paths catch light.
- **Paper state**: Dry paper.
- **Brush**: Medium round, feathering edges with clean water to soften transitions.
- **Load**: Light-medium pigment. Flows smoothly but holds a shape.
- **Edges**: Soft diffusion into surrounding greens. No hard outlines; blend one side where the path turns into shadow.
- **Move**: Wet-on-dry, or dry brush with light water-loaded pass immediately after to soften. Feathered application using load feather token behavior.
- **Result check**: Paths read as warm, lit planes. Transition from path to surrounding hills is soft, not jagged. Forest remains dark; no muddying.

### Stage 7: Dry

### Stage 8: Shadow glaze (final depth)
**Intent**: One final, subtle glaze across the entire forest and shade areas to deepen and cool them. Unifies the shadow side.

- **Color**: Very dilute cool glaze (ultramarine + sepia), barely tinted. Semi-transparent.
- **Region**: Forest and all shade sides. One continuous pass.
- **Paper state**: Dry paper.
- **Brush**: Soft mop or large round, broad strokes.
- **Load**: Very watery; almost a tint rather than a solid glaze. Minimal pigment.
- **Move**: Wet-on-dry, floating across the paper. Light touch.
- **Result check**: Values deepen slightly. Forest reads darker. No harsh edges; the glaze is soft throughout.

### Stage 9: Closing ink (selective, very sparse)
**Intent**: Reinforce path edges and add gestural texture only at focal points. Minimal marks; the painting should stand on value and edges, not ink.

- **Lines**: Same as opening (traced paths and main edges), applied selectively. Ink only where paths meet forest or where hill edges read as too soft.
- **Paper state**: Dry throughout.
- **Color**: Sepia ink, light application.
- **Marks**: Gestural flicks following path curves and tree growth direction. No fills, no scribble; confident single strokes.
- **Load**: Light, low pressure. Ink should enhance form, not overpower the watercolor.
- **Result check**: Paths have crisp defining edges only where needed. Gestural marks suggest texture without noise. The painting reads as watercolor with pen accents, not pen with watercolor fills.

## Paper state and moves checklist

- **Opening**: dry paper, pen lines
- **First wash**: mostly dry → light damping → wet brush, connected passage
- **Mid layers 1–2**: dry paper throughout, deliberate placement, soft edge blending with clean water
- **Glaze**: dry paper, very light, floating touch
- **Closing**: dry paper, pen, selective marks

## Protected lights

- **Reserve**: Small brightest path accent [0.30–0.40, 0.35–0.42], kept bare. ~8–10 mm wide on sheet.
- **Pale wash**: The entire first wash establishes pale light. Paths emerge through layering, not reserved white.
- **Lifted accents**: Lit edges of hills and bright top of path curve (right side) can be toned but kept lighter than surroundings through controlled glaze application.

## Edges in the finished painting

- **Hard**: Top of forest line against lit hills; path edges where they cut clearly against dark trees.
- **Soft**: Everywhere else. Hills turning away → soft. Shadows into lit areas → soft fade. Path into surrounding green → soft blend.
- **Lost**: Where very dark forest merges into shadow glaze; the two read as one shape (no boundary line).

## Recipe tokens and reasoning

**Variant**: `region_w13_lay2_softs_lf_lit20`

- `_lay2`: 2 mid layers instead of 4 → looser progression, fewer hard layer boundaries, faster painting feel
- `_softs`: soft arcs with split at 12 arcs, 40 mm arc length, 0.7 mm soft mm → feathered edges throughout, soft arc style for rounded transitions instead of crisp angles
- `_lf`: load feather 1.5 → pigment load tapers toward edges, creating natural feathering, especially on washes
- `_lit20`: lit lift 0.2 → early soft lifting of the brightest areas, helps preserve path highlights as paper shows through
- Base tokens retained: `rec2x, rcl, sky3, leaf, sep8, cg13, vio, rg10, blk60, bmm40, bka20, birr, skyb, cpp, dk13, tv8, uncap, pgreen, dwg200, tp2, shs`

## Failure modes and fixes (from skill reference)

- **Scalloped edges or hard wash boundaries**: Fix with _softs token for arc-based soft transitions.
- **Regions appearing finished while neighbors untouched**: Fix by removing large regional reserves and using one landscape family. Overlapping glazes unify.
- **Stark white isolated areas**: Fix by removing large reserves. One small reserve + pale toning washes cover the entire sheet.
- **Speckled, dotted appearance**: Fix by choosing large brush and few strokes in the base wash; load feather for smooth deposition.
- **Patchwork regions**: Single family, soft edges, overlapping glazes across boundaries.

## Stage checkpoint intents

1. **Opening lines**: Paths visible as flowing curves. Hill edges and forest mass silhouette suggested. Sparse, confident.
2. **After first wash**: Entire landscape is tinted pale yellow-green. Paper texture shows. Paths read as slightly lighter shapes. No pure white slabs.
3. **After mid layer 1 (dark forest)**: Forest is now dark, unified mass. Hill shadows read clearly. Paths begin to read as lit shapes against dark.
4. **After mid layer 2 (warm paths)**: Paths are now warm and lighter, reading as foreground. Good separation between path and forest. Hills have mid-tone green.
5. **After shadow glaze**: Values deepen; forest reads very dark. The landscape has atmospheric depth: pale paths, mid green hills, dark forest.
6. **After closing ink**: Path edges are reinforced only where necessary. Gestural marks follow ground plane. The painting reads as watercolor primarily, with selective ink accents.

## Why this addresses the feedback

- **"Crisp white slabs"**: Removed large reserves. Paths now toned by layering, reading naturally.
- **"Scalloped wash boundaries"**: _softs token creates arc-based soft edges throughout.
- **"Regions finishing while neighbors untouched"**: Single region + overlapping glazes = simultaneous whole-sheet progression visible.
- **"Digital color morph"**: Fewer, larger layers with soft blending (lay2, softs) mimics watercolor diffusion instead of crisp region fills.
- **"No change between snapshots"**: _lay2 creates visible stage progression; each layer adds visible value/color shift.
