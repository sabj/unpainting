# Self-Review: Try 2 Painting

## What works

- **Edge definition:** Score 9.1 shows the finished painting has strong edge work, particularly around building silhouettes and tree boundaries.
- **Compositional structure:** The layout and placement of major elements (buildings, cypress trees, grass field, sky) is recognizable and compositionally sound.
- **Color families:** The overall palette (greens, ochres, terracottas) captures the Tuscan landscape character reasonably well.
- **Value separation:** Despite the progression issues, the finished painting shows adequate separation between light (sky, grass), mid (tree canopy, building planes), and dark (cypress shadows, recesses) values.

## What's wrong and when it went wrong

The critical failure is in **human progression perception**, not the finished image quality:

1. **The 10% stage shows a faint ghost of the entire scene** - This is the kiss of death for human realism. Instead of seeing the painter start with structure or a key light area, the judges see the whole composition at once but extremely faint—exactly like a digital fade-in or dissolve effect.

2. **Opening drawing disappears or comes as speckle trails** - The judges noted "speckle or dotted trails instead of drawn lines" (3 mentions). The comprehensive pen structure that was planned doesn't read as gestural linework; it either doesn't appear clearly or reads as noise.

3. **Flat, blocky green masses instead of flowing washes** - The judges saw "flat evenly-toned slabs standing in for a form." This suggests the grass and tree regions are being laid down as many small separate patches rather than as large, connected wet passages.

4. **Looks like automated stroke rendering** - The progression judge explicitly said: "The sequence depicts a digital dissolve or fade-in effect revealing the target image, not a human painting process."

The root cause is likely in how the painter orders and executes strokes. Even though the plan conceptually called for "pen first, wash follows," the execution appears to be laying down low-opacity strokes everywhere simultaneously, creating a global fade-in rather than building region by region from light to dark.

## Self scores

- **Finished painting:** 6.5/10 - Compositionally correct, good edges, but the mosaic-like appearance and lack of flow in washes reduce its quality. Not ugly, but not compelling.
- **Painting progression/human realism:** 1.5/5 - This is the failure. It looks like a digital effect, not a painter at work. The judges were right: 0 of 2 judges found the order of work human-like.

## Feedback agreement and disagreement

**I agree with the judges on:**
- The progression is genuinely wrong and reads as automated
- The 10% stage is a red flag (should show structure, lights, or a key area—not a ghost of everything)
- The green areas lack the flowing, connected quality of real watercolor
- Opening lines don't read as confident gestural pen marks

**I disagree with or question:**
- The finished value score (6.14) might be slightly harsh—the composition holds up reasonably well when viewed as a completed painting
- The marks score (6.55) is reasonable, though some edges show decent control

## Strategy for Try 3

The problem is not the plan concept (pen + wash is correct for urban sketch) but its execution. I need to:

1. **Ensure the opening drawing is bold and visible** - Thicker pen lines that read clearly even as the first 10% of strokes are laid
2. **Force region-by-region progression, not global fade-in** - Paint sky fully, let it dry, then grass, then trees, then shadows—not all regions at once
3. **Reduce fragmentation in washes** - Fewer, larger strokes per wash; bigger brush; more connected water
4. **Increase ink or line weight in opening** - Make sure the pen layer reads as confident gestures, not speckle
5. **Consider stricter passage ordering** - The painter should complete light washes before moving to mid-values, and complete mid-values before shadows

The edge score of 9.1 tells me the watercolor fundamentals are being executed reasonably—I don't need to rebuild the whole approach, just fix how the progression appears to a human observer.
