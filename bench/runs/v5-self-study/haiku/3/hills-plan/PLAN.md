# Painting plan: Hills, V5, try 3

## Strategy change from try 2

Try 2 scored 2.0/10 because the single unified region + experimental soft-edge tokens created a chaotic speckle dump with no coherent form. The core error: **experimental tokens (`_softs`, `_lf`, `_lit20`) made scalloped edges worse, not better**.

**Try 3 strategy**: Return to simpler, proven token choices and **clear regional structure** so the painter works systematically through zones:

1. **Four distinct regions** (not one unified): paths, lit hills, forest, sky
2. **Proper light-to-dark layering** without experimental tokens  
3. **Fixed opening drawing** with actual pen lines describing subject form, not speckle
4. **Smaller reserves**: one tiny highlight reserve on the brightest path area
5. **Closing ink sparse and selective**: reinforces path/forest edges only where they define form

## Target analysis

Real landscape: pale tan winding paths through green rolling hills, dark forest mass at bottom and in shadows. Not abstract color; organic integrated landforms.

**Value structure**:
- **Lights** (0.80–0.90): Pale tan paths, warm and lit, NOT pure white. Show as paper showing through thin warm wash. Brightest at top-left path intersection and top-right path curve.
- **Midtones** (0.50–0.70): Yellow-green and medium green hillsides, lit from above. Clear shape and form.
- **Shadows** (0.15–0.35): Very dark forest (navy, blue-green, cool), clearly painted not reserved. Hard edge where forest meets lit hillside. Softer edge where shadow side of hill turns away.
- **Sky** (0.85–0.95): Pale blue at top edge, tiny visible area.

**Focal points**: The two main paths (pale winding curves) and where forest edge creates structure.

**Key observation**: Paths are lit planes, toned with warm yellow-ochre wash, integrated into the landscape through layering. Forest is painted dark, not reserved white.

## Regions and their techniques

### Region 1: Paths (hills.paths)
- **Bounds**: [0.25, 0.30, 0.65, 0.60] (tightly around the two visible pale paths)
- **Reference cue**: "Pale warm tan paths lit from above"
- **Technique**: Wash on dry paper, then glazed warmth (ink-and-wash style with sparse pen reinforcement)
- **Style**: precise-watercolor (use painted planes with hard/soft edges, not gestural)

### Region 2: Lit hillsides (hills.green-hills)
- **Bounds**: [0.0, 0.25, 1.0, 0.65]
- **Reference cue**: "Rolling green hills, grassy slopes in daylight"
- **Technique**: Value wash on dry paper with soft edges where hills turn away
- **Style**: precise-watercolor

### Region 3: Forest/shadows (hills.forest)
- **Bounds**: [0.0, 0.55, 1.0, 1.0] (dark mass at bottom and shadow sides of distant hills)
- **Reference cue**: "Dark forest in shadow, navy blue-green, provides depth and contrast"
- **Technique**: Dark glaze on dry paper with hard edge against lit hills, soft edge where it recedes
- **Style**: precise-watercolor

### Region 4: Sky (hills.sky)
- **Bounds**: [0.0, 0.0, 1.0, 0.25] (small sliver at top)
- **Reference cue**: "Pale blue sky, subtle atmospheric top"
- **Technique**: Light wash, minimal pigment
- **Style**: atmospheric-wet (soft blended, minimal definition)

## Painting sequence

### Stage 1: Opening drawing (dry pen)
**Intent**: Sparse structural lines showing the two main paths as flowing curves and the forest/hill edges as clear forms. No outline fill; lines describe what matters.

- **Lines**: Traced from target using proper pen technique. Paths visible as curved lines. Forest edge silhouette. Hill contours only where they read as clear creases (top of forest line, main hill crests). Minimal marks—maybe 5–8 confident gestural strokes.
- **Paper state**: Completely dry
- **Move**: Pen on dry paper
- **Medium**: Sepia ink, 0.5–1mm width, light pressure
- **Result**: Lines read like a painter's opening sketch. Paths and forest silhouette clear.

### Stage 2: Sky wash (pale blue base)
**Intent**: Pale blue tint over the sky region. Establishes overall light at the top.

- **Region**: hills.sky only [0.0, 0.0, 1.0, 0.25]
- **Color**: Pale blue (ultramarine + cerulean + water, very dilute). Barely tinted.
- **Paper state**: Lightly dampened but not glossy (sheen gone)
- **Brush**: Large soft round, big strokes
- **Load**: Very watery; mostly water carrying a whisper of pigment
- **Move**: Wet-on-damp, floating touch
- **Edges**: Soft fade at bottom where sky meets hills
- **Result**: Pale blue tint visible at top; paper shows through

### Stage 3: Dry fully

### Stage 4: Base wash (pale yellow-green over hills and paths)
**Intent**: Establish the light of the entire middle ground. One connected wash across lit hills and paths. Pale, transparent, paper showing.

- **Regions**: hills.green-hills + hills.paths (the upper 70% of the sheet)
- **Color**: Pale yellow-green (yellow ochre + cool green, very dilute). Warm pale tone.
- **Paper state**: Dry paper, OR lightly dampened to help flow
- **Brush**: Large mop/round, big horizontal strokes connecting left to right
- **Load**: Watery, light consistency. Pigment carried, not deposited thickly.
- **Edges**: Soft where this wash meets the forest below (no hard line); soft sky transition at top
- **Move**: Wet-on-dry or wet-on-barely-damp. Allow slight flow.
- **One passage**: Entire middle ground in one continuous session, not piecemeal
- **Result**: All lit areas (hills + paths) tinted pale yellow-green. Paper visible. No pure white slabs.

### Stage 5: Dry fully

### Stage 6: Forest shadow glaze (dark cool green)
**Intent**: Establish the dark forest mass and shadow sides of distant hills. One connected glaze across the lower region.

- **Region**: hills.forest [0.0, 0.55, 1.0, 1.0]
- **Color**: Dark cool green (ultramarine + sepia + cool green, medium concentration). Navy-green, not pure black.
- **Paper state**: Dry paper
- **Brush**: Medium round, deliberate placement
- **Load**: Medium pigment, less watery than base wash. Visible brushwork but flowing.
- **Edges**: Hard edge at top where forest meets lit hillside (the tree line reads as sharp). Soft edges at sides and bottom (receding into distance).
- **Move**: Wet-on-dry, confident strokes. Single pass to preserve form.
- **Result**: Forest reads as dark unified mass. Tree line edge is crisp where it abuts the green hill above.

### Stage 7: Dry fully

### Stage 8: Warm glaze on paths (optional, depending on first assessment)
**Intent**: Add warmth and lift to the paths, making them read as lit foreground planes.

- **Region**: hills.paths only
- **Color**: Warm pale ochre (yellow ochre + touch of burnt sienna, dilute). Warmer than the base wash.
- **Paper state**: Dry paper
- **Brush**: Medium round, following path curves
- **Load**: Light pigment, watery but visible
- **Edges**: Soft diffusion into surrounding greens; feather one side where path turns into shadow
- **Move**: Wet-on-dry, careful placement. One stroke per path segment.
- **Result**: Paths warm and brighter. Read as lit planes. Transition to forest shadow is soft on one side.

### Stage 9: Dry fully

### Stage 10: Final shadow glaze (subtle cool tone)
**Intent**: Deepen shadows and add atmospheric cool tone to distant areas.

- **Region**: hills.forest + far portions of hills.green-hills (very soft, subtle)
- **Color**: Very dilute ultramarine + sepia. Barely tinted.
- **Paper state**: Dry paper
- **Brush**: Soft mop, light touch
- **Load**: Minimal pigment; almost a whisper
- **Move**: Wet-on-dry, floating touch
- **Result**: Values deepen subtly. No harsh edges.

### Stage 11: Dry fully

### Stage 12: Closing ink (sparse, selective)
**Intent**: Reinforce path curves and forest/hill edges ONLY where they define form. No fills; sparse gestural marks following landscape contours.

- **Lines**: Same pen paths as opening, applied very selectively:
  - Path curves: one thin line on the inside edge of path curves (not both sides)
  - Forest edge: short accent marks where the tree line meets the hill, following growth direction
  - Hill crease: one line on a major hill crest if it reads too soft
- **Paper state**: Dry throughout
- **Medium**: Sepia ink, very light pressure, 0.5mm width max
- **Load**: Light, controlled
- **Move**: Pen on dry paper, confident single strokes
- **Result**: Paths have crisp defining edge only where needed. Forest edge reinforced. No ink fills or dense scribble. The painting reads as watercolor primarily; closing ink is an accent, not a layer.

## Protected lights and reserves

**Reserve**: One small brightest-path highlight [0.45, 0.35, 0.55, 0.42] (~10mm on sheet). Keep bare white paper, toned very lightly if needed with final pale wash only.

**Pale tones** establish other lights: the entire base wash (stage 4) creates pale tones across paths and lit hills. They are NOT reserved white; they are toned with warm yellow-green pigment, reading as lit paper planes, not bare white.

**Lift early if needed**: While base wash is still wet, use a thirsty brush to lift a path edge if it needs extra brightness.

## Paper state and moves checklist

- **Opening**: Dry, pen lines
- **Sky wash**: Lightly damp → soft edges
- **Base wash**: Dry or barely damp → flowing connected passage
- **Forest glaze**: Dry paper, deliberate strokes
- **Path warm glaze**: Dry paper, careful placement
- **Shadow glaze**: Dry paper, light floating touch
- **Closing ink**: Dry, pen, sparse

## Recipe tokens

**Variant**: `region_w13_lay2_ink2`

Rationale:
- `region_w13`: Standard per-region painting (proven to work; `region_w15` per-passage is more complex)
- `_lay2`: Only 2 mid layers instead of 4 → looser progression, fewer hard boundaries, visible stage changes
- `_ink2`: 2 passes of ink instead of 4 → sparse, controlled closing ink that won't overwhelm the watercolor

**Why NOT the try 2 tokens**:
- NO `_softs`: Created scalloped edges and speckle, made things worse
- NO `_lf`: Load feather created scattered deposition, not clean edges
- NO `_lit20`: Premature lifting confused the layering

**Base tokens applied automatically**: The V096 base recipe provides the rest. No custom painter settings in `extra-args.json`.

## Why this fixes the try 2 failures

1. **Speckle dumps at 10-25%**: Fixed by ensuring opening lines are proper pen lines (not speckle) and keeping them sparse
2. **Scalloped edges**: Fixed by NOT using `_softs` token; use base recipe's edge treatment (proven to work on similar subjects)
3. **Flat slab washes**: Fixed by planning multiple layers with clear intent (sky, base, forest, path warmth, final shadow) so visible progression appears
4. **Persistent white rectangles**: Fixed by:
   - Making reserves smaller (one tiny spot, not rectangles)
   - Ensuring base wash covers all lit areas, toning the paths with pigment not white
   - Forest glaze is painted dark, not a reserve
5. **Chaotic regions**: Fixed by:
   - Four clear zones (sky, hills, paths, forest) instead of one unified mass
   - Each region has a clear value intent and stage
   - Forest edge creates a hard visual boundary (focal point) instead of soft chaos
6. **Progression not human-like**: Fixed by:
   - Planned stages with visible intent (light to dark, sky then landscape, then accents)
   - Path warmth glaze makes them "pop" mid-sequence, showing human aesthetic choices
   - Sparse closing ink shows selective refinement, not random detail

## Failure modes from skill reference and prevention

| Failure | Try 2 cause | Try 3 prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Scalloped edges | `_softs` token | Remove `_softs`, use base recipe |
| Speckle and noise | Unified region + experimental tokens | Clear regions + proven tokens |
| Flat slabs | No mid-layer variety | Multiple layers with distinct intents (sky, base, forest, warm path, shadow) |
| White rectangles persist | Large reserves + confused layering | Small reserve + covering base wash |
| Non-human progression | Everything arriving together | Staged approach: sky → base → forest → warm → shadow → ink |
| Lines that don't describe form | Opening drawing problem | Proper sparse pen lines tracing path curves and forest silhouette |

## Stage checkpoint intents

1. **Opening lines**: Two paths visible as confident curves. Forest/hill edges clear. Sparse, sketchy, natural.
2. **After sky wash**: Top has pale blue tint. Rest still white.
3. **After base wash**: Entire middle ground (hills + paths) is pale yellow-green. No pure white slabs. Paper shows through. Paths slightly lighter within the green.
4. **After forest glaze**: Forest is now dark, unified mass with crisp top edge. Paths read clearly as lighter shapes against dark. Good value separation.
5. **After path warmth glaze** (if used): Paths are now warm golden-pale, read as lit foreground planes.
6. **After final shadow**: Values deepen, forest reads very dark, atmospheric depth visible.
7. **After closing ink**: Path curves have crisp defining edge (one side only). Forest edge reinforced sparingly. The painting reads as watercolor primarily with selective ink accents, not pen with watercolor fills.
