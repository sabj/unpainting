# Painting plan: Hills, V5, try 1

## Reference
Rolling hills with winding paths: pale tan/yellow paths wind through green hillsides; dark forest areas create shadow shapes; the composition reads as layered landscape with atmospheric depth. Loose ink and wash style—gestural edges, flowing strokes, watercolor transparency showing the form through value and edges rather than detail.

## Value shape
- **Lights**: pale paths (reserved paper, ~0.85 value)
- **Main mass**: yellow-green and medium green hills (~0.45–0.60 value)
- **Shadow**: dark forest areas and path shadows (~0.15–0.30 value)
- **Focal point**: the paths themselves and where they cross into shadow

## Technique and sequence
**Region: hills.main** — ink-and-wash on wet-on-dry, one connected passage

1. **Opening drawing** (pencil/pen traced from target): sparse path outlines and main hill edges, structural gestures only. Paths define the composition.

2. **First wash**: pale yellow-green base over the entire landscape (except reserved paths). Big brush, few strokes, wet on dry paper. One coherent passage—no patchwork. Paper shows through. Load: light-medium, watery consistency.

3. **Dry**: check paper fully dry before mid values.

4. **Mid values** (two passes on dry paper):
   - First mid pass: medium green on the lit hillsides, following terrain contours. Load: medium. Edges: hard where paths border hills, soft where hills turn away.
   - Second mid pass: darker greens and muted greys in the forest areas and hill slopes in shade. Edges as above.

5. **Dry**: fully dry before shadows.

6. **Shadow glaze**: one dark wash across the forest areas and path shadows, crossing hill contours. Load: concentrated but transparent. Edges: hard on path boundaries, soft where forest bleeds into light hills.

7. **Closing ink**: selective marks reinforcing path edges and forest texture. Gestural flicks following ground plane and tree lines. Sepia ink, light and confident, no fills.

## Paper state and moves
- **Opening wash**: wet-on-dry paper, big brush, connected merging within one shape
- **Mid values**: dry paper, round brush, deliberate placement with one hard edge per shape
- **Shadow glaze**: dry paper, loaded brush, soft blending where forest meets hills
- **Closing ink**: dry paper, pen/brush-pen, gestural strokes

## Protected lights
- **Path, left-center** [0.25–0.45, 0.35–0.50]: brightest lights, reserve fully
- **Path, top-right** [0.60–0.80, 0.15–0.30]: reserve to keep paths reading as positive shapes

## Edges
- **Hard**: path boundaries where they cut cleanly against hills
- **Soft**: forest into sky, hills into background, where terrain rolls away
- **Lost**: where dark forest merges with shadow glaze (no line, value only)

## Changes from base (V096)
- **Recipe**: _lay3_ink3_sep4 — three mid layers (not four) for looser feel; ink3 for gestural marks; sep4 for softer separation between value passes
- **Reserves**: two tight bounds on known brightest paths, sized from the target reference
- **Drawing**: one simple traced file for both opening and closing, sepia ink

## Failure signs
- Sky banding or hard horizontal strokes across the landscape (fix: one connected wash)
- Paths disappearing into merged values (fix: ensure reserves are maintained)
- Dark areas reading as patchwork rather than flowing shadow (fix: one continuous glaze across boundaries)
- Blurry edges or loss of path clarity (fix: ink marks should be confident and sparse)
- Muddied greens (fix: transparent layering, allow paper and yellows to show through)

## Stage checkpoints
1. After opening lines: paths visible and structural
2. After first wash: pale, loose, paper showing, paths protected
3. After mid layers: hills have form and contour, paths still read
4. After shadow glaze: depth reads, forest integrated, no patchwork
5. After closing ink: paths crisp, gestural marks enhance (not obscure) the landscape
