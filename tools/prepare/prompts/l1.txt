Make the light pencil lay-in a watercolourist draws before painting this exact picture: thin, light grey graphite lines on plain white paper. Keep exactly the same composition, proportions, placement and scale: every contour exactly where the picture has it, and any face or animal with its features exactly as in the picture. Do not add, remove, move or reshape anything. Draw only the outlines of the big forms and the main edges between them, and the few small features that place a face (eyes, nose, mouth) with a single light line each. One thin line per edge, a little broken, sometimes running past where the paint stops. No shading, no hatching, no tone, no texture, no fur or hair strands, no grass blades, no dark lines. Draw only what is in the picture: where the picture is plain wash or empty paper, leave the paper blank; do not invent any background, scenery or objects. No border, frame or ruled box round the drawing, no signature, no lettering or numbers. Most of the paper stays white.
